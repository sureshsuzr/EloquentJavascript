# Founder-fit, satisfaction and income-speed: stage-2 evaluation

Evaluator 04, 3 Nov 2026. Lens: (a) fit with this founder's specific inputs, (b) what his weeks look like in years 1, 3 and 7, (c) speed to replace A$500k and whether he can start while still a SaaS CFO, (d) ambition ceiling. I ran 10 web searches. Figures are labelled [verified: source], [estimate] or [unverified]. Most "verified" items rest on search summaries rather than primary documents.

## Assumptions

One set of assumptions applies to every candidate.

- **Salary tax.** A$500k gross is about A$300k after tax at 2026–27 rates, including Medicare [estimate]. After living costs he saves about **A$150k a year** while employed [estimate].
- **Capital gains tax.** The 12 May 2026 Budget proposes replacing the 50% CGT discount with indexation plus a 30% minimum tax from 1 Jul 2027. Startup relief is under consultation [verified: Bloomberg Tax, EY budget alert, SmartCompany]. Founder equity has a near-zero cost base, so indexation barely helps. I therefore use a **30% effective rate on exit gains**, against the ~23.5% stage-1 agents used. That cuts every equity-heavy outcome by roughly 8%. Founder shares and holdco units should be structured before 30 Jun 2027.
- **Founder salary.** A$300–350k from any holdco, opco or fund that can pay it. A$250k on VC-backed paths until a Series B.
- **Dilution.** As in each stage-1 structure, plus one extra round (−20%) in the good and great cases for venture-funded paths (C7, C8). Stage-1 agents assumed clean cap tables.
- **Time discount.** 7% a year. Each extra year before A$500k is replaced costs about **A$120–150k** of after-tax savings, plus about A$100k of unvested employer equity forgone [estimate].
- **Stay-employed baseline.** **A$3.2M** of 10-year after-tax wealth, including about A$1M of expected employer equity. Every candidate is measured against this bar.
- **Search time.** The US/Canadian median search to first acquisition is about **20 months**, and only about 58% of searches ever close a deal [verified: Stanford GSB 2026 study via Papermark]. Australia has no comparable dataset [verified: absence; first AU traditional search-fund deal was Inerva, Nov 2021]. Every acquisition path below carries this delay.

"Adj. EV" in the table is my re-estimate on these assumptions: the stage-1 EV, haircut for CGT, dilution and a realistic start date. Use the ranking, not the decimals.

## Candidate notes

**C0. Rent-roll hybrid (benchmark).**
- His most "proven" fit: real-estate networks from LAF, statistics, a CFO's underwriting.
- My lens sees a structural problem the earlier rounds underweighted. In the hybrid he is a **minority capital and data partner in someone else's roll-up**. Year 1 is fun because he builds the engine. By year 3 he is a passenger watching a licensed operator integrate rolls. That is the opposite of the Fulfilio energy the brief says he wants.
- It never replaces A$500k by itself. It is a while-employed side bet.
- LAF confidentiality never expires and overlaps the data he would want.
- **Verdict:** keep the engine, drop the hybrid as the destination. It is a good calibration exercise for C5's scoring engine, not a career.

**C1. ANZ vertical-software holdco.**
- The highest skills fit of any candidate. A sitting SaaS CFO who builds with agents can tell sticky systems of record from seat-based tools AI will hollow out. That judgement is the scarce input.
- His weeks in year 3 are close to ideal: pricing, capital allocation, an AI operating core he designs himself, and several CEOs to coach.
- **What stage 1 missed is the employment conflict.** As an officer of a SaaS company he owes duties under ss 181–183 of the Corporations Act (misuse of position or information). His knowledge of benchmarks, pricing, churn and his employer's M&A interests is exactly what he would be using.
  - ANZ vertical-software targets may be his employer's customers, partners, competitors or acquisition targets.
  - Realistically he can build a thesis and a long list, but **cannot run a search or talk to sellers** without an explicit written carve-out. The 20-month search clock starts on resignation.
- **His CFO edge is perishable.** It decays 2–3 years after he leaves the seat.
- **The entry window is narrowing.** IGV rose ~41% from 13 Apr to 12 Oct 2026, and one 2Q26 broker note is titled "End of the SaaSpocalypse" [verified: 247wallst, POEMS]. Public multiples are not private small-deal multiples, but sellers anchor on headlines.
- **Competition is active.** Valsoft bought Eziway (Apr 2026) after Buildsoft and Easy Employer, and Volaris has bought at least 4 ANZ companies [verified: Valsoft, Volaris].
- **Year 1 will frustrate him:** first-time fundraising from family offices, plus seller courtship with a ~50% chance of no deal in 18 months.
- **Ceiling:** the highest of the holdcos.

**C2. AI-native accounting / CFO-services roll-up.**
- The field is crowded in ANZ now, not just in principle.
  - Multiplier raised US$35M in Aug 2026 and has made 10 acquisitions globally, including Oxygen Advisors and Greenhawk in NZ [verified: CFOtech, ChannelLife].
  - Kelly Partners, Count and GT/New Mountain are active, and DMY reports ~47 buyers per regional listing.
- His weeks would be **managing accountants and partners**: client retention after each purchase, staffing, review quality. He is not a CA or CPA, so he cannot be the signing professional. By year 3 this is people management of professionals, which is the part of a CFO job he is presumably trying to escape.
- **Hidden upside on speed:** with a PE sponsor rather than his own holdco, he could be hired as platform CEO at A$400–550k from first close. That is the fastest income of any candidate, but with ~5–10% ownership.
- **Conflict:** real if his employer's product sells to accounting practices.

**C3. SMSF compliance factory.**
- Year 1 is the most "Fulfilio-like" build of the services ideas: aggregating scarce signing capacity onto one AI production line.
- By year 7 it is a compliance mill. He would be bored, and he would carry regulatory key-person risk on someone else's audit registration. ASIC took 64 administrative actions against SMSF auditors in FY26, 36 of them in Jan–Jun 2026 [verified: ASIC 26-170MR]. That is the regulator leaning in just as he would lean on AI-led evidence.
- The auditor-count figures in stage 1 conflict (3,382 vs ~3,762–4,100). The decline is real; the level is unverified [verified: SMSF Adviser, SMS Magazine].
- Best treated as **a module or bolt-on inside C2**, or as a cash-flow sleeve, not as a 10-year identity.

**C4. Pricing-led B2B distribution holdco.**
- The only candidate where **all of his recurring edges act daily inside one P&L**: statistical pricing, warehousing and network design (Fulfilio), working-capital discipline, AI quoting and order entry, and capital raising.
- **Fastest credible income:** CEO salary at close, and A$500k total cash by ~18–30 months once pricing gains land.
- **Zero overlap with a SaaS employer**, so the pricing-leakage test on 3 distributors' invoice lines can run on weekends with consent.
- Debt can be secured on stock and debtors, which suits his preference against personal guarantees.
- **Where he would be out of depth:** sales-rep culture (cutting discretionary discounts will cost a rep or two and a key account), supplier politics, and change-of-control clauses on exclusive agencies. These are relationship and street-sales problems, not analytical ones.
- **Where he would be bored:** after year 4–5, unless bolt-ons keep coming. This is why it should be a holdco, not a single company.
- I found no disclosed 2026 multiple for an AU distributor deal; most terms are undisclosed (Fortitude/Richard Jay, Manuli/BL Shipway) [verified: InterFinancial dashboards]. The stage-1 entry multiple of 4–5x is [unverified]. The 90-day asks must confirm it.
- **Ceiling:** lower than C1's, and cyclical, but the median is the best of the set.

**C5. Succession-capital platform (equity).**
- The best "learning while employed" option: an AI engine that scores sale candidates, plus deal-by-deal co-investment. He uses the LAF marketplace pattern, statistics and investor credibility at once.
- Stage 1 underweighted that **by year 3 the job is mostly fundraising and backing other people's operating CEOs** (searchers). He would be an allocator rather than a builder. Some founders love that; the brief's evidence (energised by building Fulfilio) suggests he would want his hands on one asset.
- Income is lumpy: closing fees and promote, and A$500k a year only once a fund closes.
- **Its real value is as the origination engine for C4 and C1, not as a standalone GP.** I would merge it into them (see below).

**C6. Succession Credit fund (debt).**
- Intellectually a good fit (loss models, investment committee work), but it is the wrong first job for his profile.
- He has no credit track record, and first-time managers raise slowly.
- ASIC's 2026 private-credit enforcement push (surveillance of 52 funds, DDO stop orders, a stricter conflicts guide RG 181, FSC Standard 30) adds compliance load to a new manager [verified: Clayton Utz, Allens, Hall & Wilcox].
- Year 1 is mostly LP meetings and AFSL/authorised-representative paperwork. Year 4+ includes workouts against failing family businesses.
- **Best as a later capital layer** once C4/C5 have a deal record. Lenders want to see his outcomes data, and that only exists after he has bought businesses.

**C7. Independent valuation and risk layer.**
- The purest intellectual fit (Bayesian nowcasting of unobserved prices; Melbourne is where the funds are) and the most enjoyable year 1.
- On my lens it is the **slowest to replace income**, at 5–6 years, because of CPS 230 vendor onboarding (6–18 months) and the "they buy a signature" problem.
- Year 3 is enterprise sales to risk committees, which he has seen from the other side as a CFO. He has done the data-company CEO job before at LAF.
- After VC dilution the founder ceiling falls (C7 sits around A$4.3M adj. EV), and the stay-employed bar is hard to beat.
- **Better as a feature of C6**, or as a later venture once he has capital and time.

**C8. AI-priced underwriting agency (MGA).**
- The lens is blunt. Recent Australian MGA launches are led by career underwriters with carrier relationships: an ex-Chubb executive's MGA, Artisan with Liberty capacity, Planned Cover with Swiss Re CorSo [verified: Intelligent Insurer, The Insurer].
- **He would be the junior partner** in the room where capacity is decided. Year 1 is spent courting carriers with no track record. Income is 4–6 years away. It cannot start while employed.
- Excellent on intellect (pricing new risk pools), but the slowest and most binary path to replacing income. **Lowest score.**

**C9. Grid-capacity arbitrage.**
- The most genuinely "Fulfilio" idea: pricing which sites will get power, and when, then consolidating under-used physical capacity.
- Zero conflict with a SaaS employer. The site-scoring model can be built at night from public DNSP and AEMO data.
- My lens sees two problems stage 1 understated:
  - **He is out of depth in power engineering and planning**, and needs an ex-DNSP connections co-founder.
  - **Years 2–4 are waiting on DNSPs, councils and connection queues**, which earlier rounds showed tests his patience. Income is lumpy and starts around year 3.
- Big balance sheets (Keppel, Stockland, PGIM) compete directly.
- **A good option to hold cheaply, not a first jump.**

## Promotions and demotions

- **Merge C5 into C4 and C1.** The scoring engine becomes the holdco's proprietary origination, and the co-invest network becomes its LP base. C5 alone is an allocator job; inside a holdco it is an unfair advantage.
- **Merge C3 into C2 as a module.** Neither is a strong standalone identity for him, and both have the same management-of-professionals problem.
- **Demote C0 from benchmark to test bed.** Use rent rolls only to calibrate the C5 engine, since earlier rounds already built the logic. Don't co-invest A$300–500k as a minority passenger.
- **Promote one variant, not a new idea: "PE-backed operator-CEO with a promote".** A sponsor hires him as CEO of a distribution (C4) or services (C2) roll-up. This gives A$450–550k from day 1 [estimate] and 3–8% sweat equity, with no search period and no first-time fundraising. Ownership is lower, but it is the **fastest, lowest-risk way to replace income** and it builds the track record that makes his own holdco fundable at deal 2. Keep it as the fallback if the 90-day tests show he can't raise A$3–5M of first-deal equity.
- **Do not promote the home-care consolidation exec role from 09.** It is fast income, but ops-heavy, carries reputational risk and fits his satisfaction drivers poorly.
- **Do not promote aged-care underwrite-and-own.** It has the same allocator-not-builder problem as C5, with regulatory and reputational tails.

## Ranking and recommendation

1. **C4, pricing-led distribution holdco (8).** It has the best combination of fit, speed, starting while employed and median outcome.
2. **C1, ANZ vertical-software holdco (7.5).** It has the best fit and ceiling, but the employer conflict delays the search clock to his resignation and the window is narrowing.
3. **C5, succession-capital engine (7).** It is the best thing to build while employed, as the origination engine for 1 and 2.

Then: C9 (6), C3 (5.5), C6 (5.5), C2 (5), C0 (5), C7 (5), C8 (4).

**My choice: C4, structured from day one as a multi-sector "pricing-led acquirer" holdco.** Distribution is platform 1 because he can test it while employed, it pays fastest, and the debt doesn't need a personal guarantee. Vertical software (C1) becomes platform 2 in years 3–4, once his employment ties are gone and the holdco has a track record and cash flow.

What he'd experience:
- **Year 1:** invoice-level pricing science on a real P&L, with results visible within a quarter.
- **Year 3:** a warehouse-network redesign and 2–3 bolt-ons, plus the first software acquisition.
- **Year 7:** a capital allocator running a A$10–15M EBITDA group with an AI operating core he designed.

The honest caveats:
- Year-2 grind with sales reps and suppliers is the part most likely to make him question the move.
- C4 beats staying employed mainly through the good and great cases. My adj. median is ~A$3.8M against the A$3.2M bar.

## Sequencing

**Now to 31 Jan 2027 (employed, around A$30–40k):**
1. **Employment and tax first.** Get written outside-interest consent that names "acquisitions of non-software businesses" explicitly. Do not ask for software carve-outs yet; that conversation flags C1 and invites refusal. Engage a tax adviser before any entity or units are issued (CGT change from 1 Jul 2027).
2. **C4 pricing-leakage test.** Run it on 3 distributors' 24-month invoice lines under NDA, presented as a free "succession readiness" report. Continue if ≥150 bps of leakage shows up in 2 of 3.
3. **Build the C5 scoring engine** as the origination tool. Calibrate it cheaply on existing rent-roll logic and on broker-listed distributors.

**Feb to 30 Jun 2027 (still employed):**
- Get ≥15 distributor targets with A$1.5–4M EBITDA at asks of ≤5x.
- Soft-circle ≥A$3–5M from family offices and BCG/exit-network investors for a sector-flexible vehicle.
- Get one asset-based-lending (ABL) term sheet without a personal guarantee.
- Run the PE operator-CEO fallback in parallel: talk to 3–4 mid-market sponsors (it costs nothing and sets a salary floor).

**Resign when:**
- an LOI is signed on a platform at ≤5x;
- ABL debt is indicated;
- ≥A$3M of equity is soft-circled.

If nothing lines up by Dec 2027, take the sponsor-backed CEO route or stay employed. Both beat a forced first deal.

**Years 2–4:**
- Bolt-ons onto the distribution platform.
- Platform 2 in ANZ vertical software (C1), using his then-ex-CFO judgement before it goes stale. Sign a non-solicit understanding with his former employer if needed.
- From year 4, consider a credit sleeve (C6) or a co-invest fund (C5) only if outside LPs ask for it.

**Hold as cheap options, not jobs:** a C9 site-scoring model, and C7 as a data product spun out of the holdco's pricing core. Drop C8, and drop C0 as a destination.
