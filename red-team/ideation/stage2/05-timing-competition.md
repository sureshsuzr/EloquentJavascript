# Timing and competitive window: stage-2 evaluation

Agent: ideation2-timing-competition, 3 Nov 2026. Lens: for each candidate, is the "why now" real and dated? Who is already in the space in AU/NZ and globally? Are entry prices really depressed? Which regulatory deadlines apply, and how long does the window stay open? This report used 20 web searches. Most sources are search summaries of trade press or law-firm notes. Labels: **[verified: source]**, **[estimate]**, **[unverified]**.

## Assumptions

All ten candidates use the same assumptions.

- **Bar to beat.** Staying employed is worth about **A$3.2M** of after-tax wealth over 10 years, including about A$1M of expected employer equity. That is extrapolated from the 7-year figure of A$2.0–2.4M [estimate].
- **Tax on salary.** Salary is taxed at a 47% marginal rate.
- **Tax on equity.** The May 2026 Budget proposes replacing the 50% CGT discount with indexation plus a 30% minimum tax from 1 Jul 2027. Disposals made before then keep the discount. The change was not yet law as of mid-2026 [verified: Pitcher Partners, EY, Ainslie summaries; legislative status unverified]. None of the candidates will exit before mid-2027, so I tax every equity outcome at about **30% effective** [estimate]. This lowers all candidates' EVs by roughly the same amount, so it does not change the ranking. Small-business CGT concessions reportedly continue, but they are unlikely to apply at platform scale.
- **Dilution.** I apply 20–30% per external equity round. Holdco promotes follow each stage-1 structure.
- **Founder salary.** About A$300k from the first close or seed. Nothing extra while he is still employed.
- **Time discount.** 8% a year. This lens adds a **window-decay cost**: the EV lost for each 12 months of delay, from competitors arriving, catalysts passing or entry prices rising. I report it as **delay cost** in the table [estimate].
- **Regulation common to the roll-ups (C1–C5).** Australia's mandatory, suspensory merger-control regime started on 1 Jan 2026. Penalties go up to A$50M [verified: Hopgood Ganim, Carter Newell]. The draft thresholds centred on A$200M combined turnover [verified: ACC, a draft figure]. Commentary says related acquisitions over a rolling three-year period are aggregated, with roll-ups explicitly targeted [verified: Mondaq "Serial killers" headline; detail unverified]. Small first deals fall below the thresholds. By years 4–6, though, a successful roll-up should expect ACCC notification, a few months' delay per bolt-on, and legal cost. This is a new 2026 friction that the stage-1 agents did not price.

## Candidate notes

**C0: Rent-roll pricing-engine hybrid (timing score 5).**
- **No fresh AU catalyst.** It rests on slow agency succession. Rolls still trade at about 3.2–3.3x fees, and up to about 4x for quality books [unverified: Pendium sold listings, IFA broker note]. That is normal pricing, not depressed.
- **No obvious institutional roll-up competitor found in 2026.** A search for a PE-backed AU rent-roll roll-up turned up none [search absence]. Buyers are private (e.g. Managex buying The Agency's WA roll for A$3.6M, date unverified).
- **The clock that matters is data.** PropertyMe's AiMe Connect went live in Oct 2026 (round 3). From then on, about 60–70% of a generic DD report is reproducible by anyone. The engine's edge is the **outcome dataset**, and that accrues to whoever starts licensing post-sale exports first.
- **Verdict.** The window is long but slowly eroding. Start collecting data now; there is no rush to deploy capital.

**C1: ANZ vertical-software holdco (timing score 7).**
- **The stage-1 premise is only half true.** The SaaSpocalypse hit *public* multiples: SEG's public SaaS index fell from 5.7x to 3.2x EV/revenue between 2Q25 and 2Q26. *Private* SaaS M&A medians barely moved, from 4.1x to 4.0x [verified: secondary summaries of SEG]. Private deal volume rose: 2,784 TTM transactions, +16% [same].
- **So the panic discount has not yet reached private sellers.** The arbitrage is the gap between what public comparables imply and what founders still expect. That gap closes in one of two ways:
  - sellers capitulate in 2027 as their exit options dry up, which is the good case; or
  - public markets recover first and the gap vanishes.
- **Competition is active and named.**
  - Valsoft/Fluent bought Eziway (Apr 2026).
  - Constellation's Vela/Libra bought Expedient Software in Queensland (Jun 2026).
  - Arcadea bought JAIX (2026) and Agdata (2025).
  - Invincible bought ProSpend.
  - Bain is buying MYOB at the top end [verified: press releases via search].
- **Where the gap is.** Most of these buyers are global permanent-capital players with lower hurdles. The white space is the sub-A$1M EBITDA tail they ignore, which also pays least.
- **Why there is urgency.** The 2026–27 vintage is when AI fear is highest and buyers are fewest. A 2029 entry is likely at higher prices or with AI churn already visible.
- **Two blockers.**
  - He is a sitting SaaS CFO, so the outside-interest conflict is sharpest here.
  - Low founder ownership (15–25%) means he needs AI *cost-out* to be real within 18 months of the first deal.

**C2: AI accounting / CFO-services roll-up (timing score 3).**
- **The window has largely closed for a new, sub-scale entrant.**
  - Multiplier raised US$35M in Aug 2026 and has done 10 acquisitions, including NZ firms serving AU clients [verified: Dealroom, ChannelLife, IAB].
  - Grant Thornton US (New Mountain-backed) is acquiring GT Australia [verified: CityAM, Pulse].
  - Kelly Partners and Count are buying continuously.
  - Pemba-backed SuperConcepts operates in the adjacent space.
  - Regional listings already draw about 47 buyers each, and multiples are rising from 1.13x to 1.26x fees (stage 1, DMY).
- **New 2026 frictions.**
  - IESBA has a workstream on independence and ethics in PE-owned accounting firms [verified: secondary].
  - ACCC aggregation applies to serial acquisitions.
- **Why waiting is no remedy.** He would be the 5th–10th entrant into a consensus trade. Entry prices only rise, and vendor AI (Xero, Basis-type tools) erodes the margin thesis.
- **Exception.** A narrow regulated niche is still open, and that niche is C3.

**C3: SMSF compliance factory (timing score 8).**
- **The best-dated catalyst on the list.**
  - Division 296 passed on 10 Mar 2026 and started on 1 Jul 2026.
  - The first measurement is at **30 Jun 2027**, and the first assessments follow lodgement of 2026–27 returns [verified: Clearlaw, ATO newsroom, BDO, RSM].
  - So the first full compliance workload arrives in **FY28 (Jul 2027 onward)**. A platform has to own capacity and have its Div 296 workflow running before then. That is about 8 months from today.
- **Supply is shrinking and enforcement is rising.**
  - Auditors fell from 4,162 to 3,882 (Jun 2024 to Jun 2025), with only 37 new registrations [verified: ASIC REP 825 via SMSF Adviser].
  - ASIC took **64 actions against SMSF auditors in FY26**, 36 of them in Jan–Jun 2026 [verified: ASIC 26-170MR].
  - Enforcement pushes small and older auditors to sell or exit now, which is a supply flush in 2026–28.
- **Who else is buying.**
  - Perpetual bought smartsuper (reported Nov 2025).
  - Pemba owns SuperConcepts (2023).
  - ASF Audits uses an AI assistant [verified/promotional].
  - No US-style AI roll-up in SMSF was found [search absence].
- **Corrections to stage 1.**
  - **Div 296 hits a minority of funds** (balances above A$3M), so it adds per-fund fees at the top end; it does not raise volume.
  - **ASIC's enforcement cuts both ways.** It makes AI-drafted workpapers a regulatory risk unless approved auditors visibly own sign-off.
- **The window.** It is wide open for about 18–24 months: retiring auditors' books become available before Perpetual, SuperConcepts or Class/HUB24 consolidate them. After that it narrows.
- **Employer conflict:** none, since this is not in SaaS.

**C4: Pricing-led distribution holdco (timing score 6).**
- **No dated catalyst.** Boomer succession runs for 10 years. AI quoting and pricing tools are newly cheap, but that is a gradual effect.
- **Competition in the A$1.5–4M EBITDA band is thin.**
  - Mid-market PE (Anacacia: Procal 2025, Florabelle 2024) buys larger or food assets.
  - "Buyer-ready" individuals post on BusinessForSale [verified: listings].
  - No AI-pricing-led distribution roll-up was found in AU [search absence].
- **Entry pricing** of about 4–5x [unverified] is structurally low, but not newly depressed.
- **Headwinds.** Higher 2026 rates [unverified: four hikes] squeeze ABL leverage. ACCC aggregation matters later for bolt-ons in the same product market.
- **Timing verdict.** The best *durability* on the list. He can run the 90-day leakage test while employed and lose almost nothing by deciding in 2027–28. That is a strength for de-risking, but it means no urgency premium.

**C5: Succession-capital platform (equity) (timing score 6).**
- **A slow-growing, still nascent space.** SmartCompany reports search funds are "increasing in popularity" for deals under A$50M. No AI-origination sponsor platform was found in AU [search absence].
- **Global analogues are ready to enter, but slowly.** Teamshares, Relay and the US search accelerators have not entered AU.
- **The catalyst is demographic and durable.** There is no deadline, so the window is long.
- **Main timing risk.** The 2026 private-credit stress (C6) tightens family-office appetite for illiquid, first-time-sponsor deals through 2027.
- **Verdict.** Build the scoring engine now, because data compounds. Raise capital later.

**C6: Succession Credit fund (debt) (timing score 5).**
- **The stage-1 agent's "allocators want non-property credit" is right in principle but mistimed.**
  - Bathla's ~A$3.4B collapse (Aug 2026) and Metrics freezing two funds of more than A$9B amid an auditor valuation dispute [verified: MPA, Clayton Utz].
  - ASIC interim stop orders (8 Oct 2026) and a 2026 enforcement priority on private credit [verified: Clayton Utz, ASIC, HSF Kramer].
- **Effect on a first-time raise.** Raising a new private-credit fund in 2027 is hard. LPs are redeeming, and ASIC compliance costs are rising.
- **But the gap is real and confirmed.**
  - Banks still shun SME acquisition debt, and non-bank SME finance demand is at a record 55–57% [verified: ScotPac via MPA].
  - No dedicated SME acquisition lender has launched [search absence].
  - Post-stress vintages historically perform best.
- **Timing call.** Build the underwriting dataset and a track record via BookCo-scale lending, or via the C5 equity side, in 2027. Raise the fund in **2028**, when "clean, non-property cash-flow credit with independent valuation" is the post-crisis product. This is the one candidate where moving *now* is worse than waiting.

**C7: Independent valuation and risk layer (timing score 7).**
- **The regulatory moment is now and unusually loud.**
  - ASIC REP 841 (14 Oct 2026) found persistent weaknesses in how super trustees **and auditors** value unlisted assets, including private credit, and threatened regulatory action [verified: ASIC 26-243MR, Regulation Tomorrow].
  - ASIC's June 2026 notice told private-credit funds to refresh 30 June valuations [verified].
  - Metrics' auditor dispute and fund freezes show exactly the gap.
- **But the white space is smaller than stage 1 said.**
  - **Kroll Australia already sells private-credit valuation services** with a 15-year AU track record [verified: Kroll site].
  - MSCI is pushing private-credit transparency [verified: InvestorDaily].
  - Big 4 and mid-tier valuation teams are the default.
  - I could find **no 2026 APRA amendment to SPS 530** mandating independence [search absence]. The "mandate" is ASIC pressure and expectation, not a rule.
- **Where the gap really is.**
  - the cross-fund exposure network view;
  - daily nowcast marks for switching members;
  - tooling sold *to auditors*, who REP 841 now puts on notice.
- **How long the window lasts.** It closes within roughly **12–24 months**, as incumbents add AI and the crisis fades. Hence the high urgency.
- **The catch.** He cannot build while employed, and APRA-regulated procurement takes 6–18 months. The cheap action now is 15 interviews and a data-sharing pilot, while buyers are scared.

**C8: AI-priced niche MGA (timing score 5).**
- **AI-agent liability is moving fast globally.**
  - Armilla (Lloyd's coverholder) offers US$25M limits.
  - Testudo began writing GenAI liability on Lloyd's capacity in Jan 2026.
  - Incumbents are adding AI exclusions (the Chubb commentary).
  - Tego reportedly plans "Australia's first standalone AI insurance policy" [unverified, undated].
- **The local first-mover slot may already be taken.** A new entrant with no underwriter co-founder realistically binds its first risk in late 2027 at the earliest.
- **The battery and installer warranty niche is better timed.**
  - The rebate went tiered on 1 May 2026.
  - Installer and seller liquidations are under way (e.g. Ampetus Energy) [verified: RenewEconomy].
  - Workmanship warranties are void once an installer fails [verified: secondary].
- **But there is still no mandate.** A voluntary market is slow, and lobbying runs on a 2–3 year clock.
- **Verdict.** Moderate urgency, gated by a binary (capacity), and the founder lacks the key input. Keep it as a cheap option.

**C9: Grid-capacity arbitrage (timing score 4).**
- **The catalysts are real but are shifting value away from a small originator.**
  - Victoria's Sustainable Data Centre Action Plan (22 Sep 2026) requires new data centres to match load with new renewables and storage, and to pay connection and augmentation costs. It is **not retrospective** for applications already under assessment [verified: Energy-Storage.news, ESS News, Urban Developer].
  - The federal energy minister has sought an AEMC rule forcing data centres to contract new firmed energy.
  - The AEMC's cost-recovery consultation closes on 5 Nov 2026 [verified: PV Tech, W.Media, Mallesons "three major reforms", 5 Aug 2026].
  - AEMO reports **5.4 GW** in the transmission connection process (about 40% in Victoria) against more than 18 GW of enquiries [verified: AEMO, AEMC request].
- **Three effects follow.**
  - Grandfathering rewards whoever is *already* in the queue (Keppel, HyperDC, Stockland, PGIM).
  - The new costs cut the land premium a new data-centre buyer can pay.
  - Connection-process reform is likely to clear speculative positions. VicGrid now plans Victorian transmission.
- **The window for a capital-light optionholder is closing, not opening.** The behind-the-meter battery leg faces CleanPeak/KKR and Enervest, and merchant revenues fell to about A$44–85k/MW/yr (stage 1).
- **Verdict.** High urgency, but in a race against balance-sheet players. Weakest timing for this founder after C2.

## Promotions and demotions

1. **Promote "stalled-housing completion / special situations" (agent 02 Idea 2), but only as an extension of C7.**
   - The private-credit stress of Aug–Oct 2026 (Bathla, Metrics, stop orders, A$9B frozen) creates a classic 2027–29 distressed window. Frozen funds will need to sell loans and complete projects.
   - The valuation layer (C7) is the information edge that tells you which loan books to buy. That is round 3's "capture the edge as an investor" logic.
   - **Merge:** C7 becomes "Mark + special situations". Sell independent marks to auditors and trustees in 2027, then co-invest with a distressed-property partner in 2028.
   - The founder lacks workout credentials, so this needs a partner. It is not a standalone candidate.
2. **Merge C5 and C6 into one "succession capital" platform with staged timing:** the equity and origination engine in 2027, then the credit fund in 2028. They share data, deal flow and LPs. Raising credit in 2027 is the wrong vintage.
3. **Fold C0 into C3 and C5 as an engine, not a business.** A fee book (SMSF administration, audit, accounting) prices like a rent roll: retention under ownership change. The round-3 engine becomes C3's acquisition-pricing tool immediately.
4. **Demote C2.** The window has closed for a new entrant. Keep only its SMSF/regulated slice (C3).
5. **Demote C9 to a watching brief.** The rules of 2026 favour incumbents in the queue.
6. **Not promoted:** aged care and home care (09). The Support at Home transition (from Nov 2025) is a dated catalyst, but PE and large providers are active and the founder has no edge. The compliance-services roll-up (ESM) is durable, with no urgency and the same profile as C4.

## Ranking and recommendation

Ranked by timing score, with tie-breaks on how much a move in the next 6–12 months matters:

1. **C3 SMSF compliance factory: 8.** A dated catalyst (Div 296's first measurement on 30 Jun 2027, assessed work landing in FY28), an enforcement-driven supply flush of auditors, consolidation by strategics but no AI roll-up yet, and no employer conflict.
2. **C7 Independent valuation layer: 7.** The loudest regulatory moment (REP 841, private-credit freezes), but incumbents (Kroll, MSCI, the Big 4) exist, and the window is 12–24 months.
3. **C1 ANZ vertical-software holdco: 7.** The 2026–27 vintage matters, but private multiples haven't yet fallen and the field is contested. The SaaS-employer conflict is sharpest here.
4. **C4 Distribution holdco: 6.** The most durable window and the least competition, with no urgency.
5. **C5 Succession-capital platform: 6.** Durable, nascent, and best built as data now.
6. **C0 Rent-roll hybrid: 5.** The outcome-data clock matters; the capital doesn't.
7. **C8 MGA: 5.** Global AI specialists are ahead; the battery niche lacks a mandate.
8. **C6 Succession Credit: 5.** The right idea in the wrong vintage. Raise in 2028.
9. **C9 Grid-capacity arbitrage: 4.** The window is closing in favour of balance-sheet incumbents.
10. **C2 AI accounting roll-up: 3.** A consensus trade with funded entrants already in AU/NZ.

**My one choice from this lens: C3, the SMSF compliance factory.**
- It is the only candidate where a calendar deadline (FY27 returns, then FY28 assessments) and a supply flush (FY26 enforcement, auditor attrition) coincide in the next 6–18 months.
- Its competitors are consolidators without an AI-native model, not AI-native funds.
- If he waits until 2028, the best auditor books will have gone to Perpetual, SuperConcepts or Class/HUB24 partners, and the Div 296 workflow will be a BGL/Class feature. I estimate EV erodes about 15–20% a year from delay.
- **Caveat from this lens.** C3's ceiling is lower than C1's. If the founder's priority is the right tail rather than timing, C1 is the alternative, and its window is still open through 2027.

## Sequencing

The aim is to use the employed months to buy options whose windows close soonest, and to defer the durable ones.

**Nov–Dec 2026 (employed)**
- Get written employer consent for outside activity. This is the gating item for everything below.
- Get tax advice before incorporating. The structure has to be decided before the 1 Jul 2027 CGT change, even though no exit happens before then.
- **C7 cheap option:** while REP 841 is fresh, run 15 interviews with CIOs, CROs, super audit partners and private-credit trustees. The only aim is to find out whether anyone will share data and pay for a pilot. Cost: under A$15k.

**Jan–Apr 2027 (employed): C3 90-day test**
- Talk to 25 audit and administration owners through DMY and NTAA. Prioritise auditors facing ASIC action or aged 60+.
- Build the agent pipeline on 300 de-identified files with one friendly approved auditor.
- Line up referral LOIs from accountants.
- Price the books with the rent-roll retention engine (C0 reused).
- **Pass:** at least 2 books at ≤1.1x fees, and at least 3,000 funds' worth of referral LOIs.
- In parallel, run a low-effort C1/C4 broker pipeline. Those windows are long, so just keep a list.

**May–Jul 2027: decision**
- If C3 passes, jump. Close the first audit book and the first administration book before 30 Jun 2027, so the platform processes the first Div 296 return season (FY27 returns, Div 296 assessments in FY28).
- If C3 fails, C1 is the next jump, provided employer consent allows it and quality targets trade at ≤6x EBITDA. Otherwise use C4.

**2028 onward: combine**
- The C3 platform becomes deal #1 of a C5-style independent-sponsor holdco. Its fee-book pricing engine generalises to other books.
- Raise the C6 credit fund in 2028, in the post-stress vintage, using C5 deal flow and outcome data.
- If C7 pilots converted in 2027, run C7 as a separate, venture-funded entity with a special-situations co-invest attached, never alongside C3 full-time.

**Never in parallel full-time:** C8 and C9. Each needs a dedicated domain co-founder and is racing better-capitalised players.
