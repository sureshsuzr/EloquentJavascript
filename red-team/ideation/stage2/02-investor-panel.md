# Investor Panel (VC, PE, family office, private credit): stage-2 evaluation

3 Nov 2026. Lens: **fundability for this founder**. For each candidate the panel asks who would write the cheque, on what terms, how fast, what proof they want first, and what the founder ends up owning. The panel is a composite of a lower mid-market PE partner, a family-office CIO, an Australian seed/Series A VC, a private-credit allocator and an insurance capacity provider. 15 web searches were run. Labels: **[verified: source]** means a named source via search summary (primary pages were not opened); **[estimate]** is the panel's own figure; **[unverified]** means one weak source.

## Assumptions

These apply to every candidate.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Horizon and metric | 10 years from Jan 2027. **Founder after-tax wealth at year 10**: cumulative after-tax pay and fees, plus after-tax equity, carry or promote, minus personal capital put in. Nominal AUD. |
| Discount for time | Value realised in years 8–10 (exits, carry) is haircut **25%** against cash in years 1–5. That reflects 7% a year over ~4 extra years plus the extra risk that exits slip. Carry from a fund is haircut **35%**, because it is the most back-ended and the most often delayed. |
| Tax | Pay and fees at a ~47% marginal rate. Exit gains at a **~25% effective rate**, a blend of today's discounted CGT (~23.5%) and the unverified May 2026 Budget proposal (indexation plus a 30% minimum tax from 1 Jul 2027). Carry is assumed to be structured as a capital gain, which needs advice. |
| Founder pay | Set by what the backer will tolerate. Sponsor-backed operating CEO: A$300–350k. VC-backed CEO: A$200–250k until Series A. Fund GP pre-scale: A$0–250k. |
| Dilution and terms (2026 AU market norms) [estimate unless labelled] | **Search fund:** searcher 20–25% common vesting in thirds; search capital steps up 1.5x. **Independent sponsor:** 1–3% closing fee, monitoring fee, 15–25% promote over an 8% pref (tiered by MOIC), plus pro-rata on co-invest. **Holdco/GP:** 15–20% carry plus co-invest, with the seeder taking 15–25% of GP economics. **VC:** seed 20–25%, Series A 20%, ESOP 10–15%, so the founder holds ~25–35% at exit. **MGA:** an underwriter co-founder takes 20–30%; a capacity provider may take a 10–20% strategic stake. |
| Personal capital | ≤A$1.0M at risk; **no personal guarantees** (a hard filter: anything that needs one is penalised). |
| Baseline | Staying employed ≈ **A$3.2M** after tax over 10 years, including ~A$1M of expected employer equity. This is the midpoint of the stage-1 range of A$3.0–3.5M. |
| Market backdrop (late 2026) | AU startup funding A$5.1B in 2025 (+24%), but deal count fell 17% to 390, and 61% of capital went to AI companies [verified: Cut Through Venture/Folklore via Forbes AU, SmartCompany]. Family offices made up 40% of active AU private-capital investors in 2024, up from 10% in 2020 [verified: AIC/Preqin 2025 Yearbook via Firstlinks]. AU mid-market PE is subdued, and LP money favours large pan-Asia funds [verified: EY AU PE update Q2 2026]. In 2025, first-time private-credit managers raised about 79 of 301 funds, and managers founded in the last five years took <1% of ~US$240B raised [verified: WithIntelligence]. |

**What the panel thinks of the founder.** His profile is **easy to back as a buyer of cash flow and hard to back as a manager of other people's money**.
- **Easy.** BCG plus two exits (one to Australia Post) plus a sitting SaaS CFO is exactly the "operator-allocator" profile that AU family offices and search-fund investors back deal by deal. He can do his own quality-of-earnings review and model, which lowers investors' diligence cost.
- **Hard.**
  - No fund, credit or underwriting track record, which matters for a fund, a credit vehicle or an MGA.
  - No sector operating track record in distribution, accounting, insurance or energy.
  - He won't give personal guarantees, which AU banks expect on goodwill-heavy deals.
  - He can't run a full-time search or raise while employed without his employer's consent.

## Candidate notes

**C0. Rent-roll pricing-engine hybrid (6.0).**
- **Easiest to start.** It needs ~A$40k plus a A$0.3–0.5M co-invest, and no outside raise.
- **What stage 1 missed:** as an *investment product* it is too small. A roll-up of 250–900 deals a year nationally won't absorb more than ~A$20–50M of equity. Institutional PE won't treat it as a platform, so the founder's outcome is capped at a minority stake in someone else's company.
- **Who backs it:** family offices that know property services, alongside bank senior from NAB or Macquarie. Banks will want **PGs from the operator**, not from him, which is acceptable.
- **Proof required:** a backtest on ~30 rolls with outcomes.
- **Panel verdict:** a good *option* and a credibility builder (he gains a deal record in his own name). It is not the main event.

**C1. ANZ vertical-software holdco (7.5).**
- **Best fit of any candidate.** A sitting SaaS CFO and computer scientist who can judge AI-rewrite risk is precisely who a family office wants doing software diligence.
- **What stage 1 under-weighted: the deal-flow contest.** In 2025–26, Valsoft (Eziway, Apr 2026), Arcadea (JAIX, Apr 2026; Agdata 2025), Banyan, Invincible Software (Strada-backed; ProSpend, Nov 2025) and Access all bought small AU vertical software [verified: Business Wire, PrivSource, ACCESSWIRE]. LPs will ask, "Why do you win against Valsoft at 5x when they pay 6–7x and close in 6 weeks?" His answer has to be **sub-scale targets (A$0.5–2M EBITDA) that the global buyers' hurdles skip**, sourced proprietarily.
- **LP nervousness:** the 2026 AI repricing of software makes family offices nervous as well as bargain-hunting. Expect them to require **≥90% GRR evidence and a 15–25% vendor earn-out or rollover**.
- **Terms:** deal by deal at first (founder ~20–30% through co-invest plus promote), then an A$20–40M holdco round around year 3, diluting him to ~15–20%. Software private credit at 2–3x ARR or EBITDA is available without a PG.
- **Income:** slower; A$500k cash takes 3+ years.

**C2. AI accounting/CFO-services roll-up (5.0).**
- **Capital is the most abundant of any candidate**, but it is going to others. Multiplier raised a US$35M Series B (Aug 2026) after US$27.5M at launch and has done ~6–8 acquisitions including NZ's Oxygen Advisors. New Mountain-backed Grant Thornton Advisors bought GT Australia (~A$1B, May 2026). KPG does ~1 deal a month [verified: DealStreetAsia, IT Brief, pulse.bot; KPG ASX].
- **Market sentiment:** trade press now runs "roll-ups hitting reality check" stories about normalising multiples [verified: The Finance Story, LearnFormula].
- **How a PE investor sees him:** without a CA partner pedigree, he is a CFO/COO hire, not the founder. Founder economics fall to 5–12% sweat.
- **Panel verdict:** fundable, but not on terms that beat staying employed. Demote unless it merges into C3.

**C3. SMSF compliance factory (6.5).**
- **Strong fundability that stage 1 didn't stress: small cheques.** Practices trade at ~1–1.3x fees, so the first two deals (A$2–4M) can be funded with **his own A$0.5–0.8M, seller earn-outs and 2–4 family offices**. There is no institutional raise until the platform works, so the founder keeps 30–50%.
- **Exit route is credible:** strategics already buy in this space (Pemba–SuperConcepts 2023; Perpetual, Netwealth, AMP SMSF admin deals) [verified: MergerLinks, Money Management].
- **Investors' worries:**
  - fee deflation from Class and BGL AI;
  - auditor-independence structuring;
  - the need for a **registered auditor and tax agent as equity partners**, a hire investors will want named before funding.
- **Panel verdict:** a high floor, a moderate ceiling and a clean raise.

**C4. Pricing-led B2B distribution holdco (8.0).**
- **Most bankable structure of the ten.**
  - **Debt:** receivables and inventory support asset-based lending, which is the most realistic route to **no PG**.
  - **Equity:** AU search-fund and independent-sponsor investors explicitly target private businesses with **≥A$2M EBITDA and EV up to ~A$50M** [verified: searchfunder/AU ETA commentary]. That band is exactly this target.
- **Who backs it, in practice:**
  - an independent-sponsor raise of A$3.5–4.5M per platform from 6–12 family offices and ex-founders, closed **3–6 months from a signed LOI** [estimate];
  - or a traditional search fund (~A$0.5–0.8M of search capital from AU/APAC search investors; Pete Seligman's network is the hub [verified: searchfunder/AFR-style summaries]). This gives 25% vesting common with no capital at risk, but requires full-time search, i.e. quitting first.
- **Exit route:** lower mid-market PE is under-served and actively buying recurring buy-and-build platforms [verified: Fortitude, StepStone 2025], so there is a buyer at 7–9x.
- **What investors will require:**
  - a named operating chair or COO with distribution experience;
  - pricing-leakage evidence on the target's own invoice data;
  - change-of-control clearance on the top-3 supplier agencies.
- **Founder economics:** ~25–30% (pro-rata on A$0.9M plus a 15–20% promote), CEO salary A$300–350k from close.

**C5. Succession-capital platform (5.5 standalone, 7.5 merged).**
- **Standalone:** an AI scoring engine plus "Verified Exit" packs is not venture-fundable (thin revenue, broker-dependent), and the sponsor/search-accelerator fund is a **first-time-manager raise with no record**.
- **Where an LP would back it:** only after **3–5 sponsored deals**. Gen II-type data puts emerging-manager closes at ~16 months from first to final close [unverified: secondary], which pushes Fund I to year 4–5.
- **What stage 1 missed:** the engine's real value to investors is as **the proprietary origination behind C4 or C1**. It answers the "why do you win deals?" question, which is the first question every sponsor LP asks. Merge it in, don't raise for it.

**C6. Succession Credit fund (3.5).**
- **The hardest raise of the ten.**
  - Private credit is under ASIC scrutiny (REP 814, Sep 2025; REP 820 surveillance of 28 funds, Nov 2025), focused on valuation, conflicts and fees [verified: Clayton Utz, Investor Daily, S&P]. Compliance and trustee cost for a new manager has risen.
  - New managers raise <1% of global private-credit capital [verified: WithIntelligence].
  - A seeder will take 15–25% of the GP, and super funds want a 5-year record.
- **Pricing pressure:** banks are cutting acquisition-loan pricing to stay competitive [verified: Chambers Acquisition Finance 2026].
- **Adverse selection is the panel's main worry:** a 11–14% goodwill lender sees the deals banks declined.
- **Panel verdict:** the founder has no credit workout history, and a credit allocator would not back a first loss book on a model alone. Better to **use** private credit as a borrower in C4 and C1 than to become it.

**C7. Independent valuation and risk layer (5.5).**
- **Real tailwinds:**
  - APRA's Dec 2024 SPS 530 review found material gaps in revaluation frequency and triggers [verified: APRA via Investor Daily, Clayton Utz].
  - ASIC flagged weak valuation controls in private credit (REP 814/820).
  - AI is 61% of AU venture dollars.
- **Raise:** a seed of A$2–4M is raisable within ~6 months of a 90-day proof, and a custodian or research house is a natural strategic investor.
- **What stage 1 missed:** the **sales cycle into APRA-regulated funds** (CPS 230 vendor onboarding, 6–18 months) means the seed must last 24+ months on 1–2 design partners. VC salary (~A$220k) doesn't replace income for 4–5 years. Founder ownership falls to ~25–35%.
- **Kill risk:** incumbents (MSCI/Burgiss, Kroll, custodians) bundle a "good enough" product.

**C8. AI-priced niche MGA (4.5).**
- **Capacity exists in AU.** 2025–26 launches include Amparo (Munich Re), Sync (Tokio Marine), Codex (MS Amlin), Kaibridge, and new Lloyd's coverholders CoLab and Pinnacle [verified: Insurance Asia News headlines]. **Every one is led by career underwriters** (e.g. ex-Chubb). Capacity providers fund an underwriter's track record, not a statistician's model.
- **The AI-agent liability niche is being taken offshore:** AIUC raised a US$15M seed plus a US$40M Series A, and Armilla offers Lloyd's-backed AI liability limits to US$25M [verified: fintech.global, Coverager, Dealroom].
- **Panel verdict:** the founder would be the third-best-funded entrant in his own niche, and would own ~20–30% after an underwriter co-founder and VC. The battery/solar warranty variant is more defensible but small (~A$25–30M premium). Fundable only with a named lead underwriter, and slow to profit commission.

**C9. Grid-capacity arbitrage (4.5).**
- **Large capital chases powered Melbourne land, but it backs owners and developers with balance sheets.** Examples: PGIM's 20.7ha site (A$1.2B campus), AirTrunk MEL2, Stockland's 350MW secured across Laverton North and Brooklyn, Keppel's 720MW "powerbank" access arrangement, and Zerra on the Ford Broadmeadows site [verified: w.media, Baxtel, IREI, pulse.bot].
- **What stage 1 missed:** the arbitrage is visible and already being harvested by REITs (Stockland, Centuria). Keppel's powerbank shows that connection owners are **selling the option themselves**.
- **Investor view:** a newcomer without an energy/planning record gets deal-by-deal option money at best, and option fees are the risk capital.
- **The behind-the-meter battery variant** is fundable as a devco (climate VC plus CEFC-style debt), but founder look-through ownership is ~10–15%.

## Promotions/demotions

1. **Merge C5 into C4 (and C1): "Pricing-led independent-sponsor holdco".** The origination engine is what makes the sponsor fundable, and the sponsor is what monetises the engine. Investors will write cheques for the combination, not for either alone.
2. **Merge C2 into C3.** If he does professional services, the SMSF niche gives him better terms than general accounting, where PE and Multiplier already set prices and he would be a hired executive.
3. **Demote C6 to "later and only as a sleeve".** If C4 or C1 works, a credit sleeve can be added in years 5–7 with a track record. Not now.
4. **Consider promoting one unshortlisted fallback: the PE-backed executive route** (09's home-care consolidation is one instance, but the sector should be his choice).
   - **Terms:** a sponsor hires him as CEO or CFO of a platform at A$450–600k with 3–8% sweat equity plus a A$0.3–0.5M co-invest.
   - **Not a "build" idea,** but it is the **most fundable outcome of all** and has the highest floor (~A$4M median per 09).
   - **Why it matters:** it is a credible fallback if C4's search fails, so he can quit with lower downside.
5. **No promotion** for aged-care underwrite-and-own or stalled-housing completion. Both are first-time-fund raises in sectors that are ASIC- or APRA-sensitive or reputationally sensitive, and family offices will want a sector track record.

3. **C3 SMSF compliance factory: 6.5** (~A$4.8M; 45%)

5. C5 Succession-capital platform: 5.5 standalone (7.5 merged into C4)
6. C7 Valuation/risk layer: 5.5
7. C2 AI accounting roll-up: 5.0
8. C8 AI-priced MGA: 4.5
9. C9 Grid-capacity arbitrage: 4.5

**The panel's choice: C4, structured as an independent-sponsor holdco, with the C5 engine as proprietary origination and C1 as the second vertical once the playbook is proven.**

**Why C4 over C1:**
- **Faster cheques, cheaper debt.** Distribution assets carry debt secured on stock and debtors, the only realistic way to honour "no PG". A family office will close A$4M behind a signed LOI in months.
- **Fewer rival bidders.** Fewer global consolidators bid for A$2–4M-EBITDA distributors than for software.
- **Pay from close.** It pays a CEO salary from day one.

C1 has the better background fit and the fatter tail, but it is contested by Valsoft, Arcadea and others, and LPs are nervous about AI substitution in 2026. If his network surfaces a proprietary software deal at ≤5x with ≥90% GRR, take it: the holdco can own both.

**Terms to aim for (deal 1)** [estimate]:
- A$10–12M EV, ~4.5x.
- ABL/senior ~2x EBITDA, no PG.
- Vendor note 15%.
- Equity A$4–4.5M: founder A$0.8–0.9M; 6–12 investors with a 1x preference plus 8% hurdle.
- Promote 15% rising to 20–25% above 2.5x MOIC.
- 1.5–2% closing fee.
- CEO salary A$300–350k.

This puts founder economics at ~25–30% of equity value.

## Sequencing

**While employed** (only after written employer consent, by ~Dec 2026):
1. **Credential the engine cheaply.** Run C0's backtest and C4's pricing-leakage test on 3 distributors' invoice data (~A$25–40k in total). These become the "proof" slides in every investor conversation.
2. **Soft-circle capital, not deals.**
   - Meet 15–20 family offices, ex-BCG and ex-founder angels, and 2–3 AU search investors.
   - **Pass:** ≥A$4M of indicated deal-by-deal interest and one ABL indication without a PG.
   - Stay quiet about specific targets until employment allows.
3. **Recruit the named partner investors will ask for:** a retired distribution MD or COO as chair or operating partner, with 2–5% sweat. Check employer conflicts for any software targets (C1).
4. **Keep C0 alive** as a small co-invest (≤A$0.3M) if a licensed operator appears. It builds a deal record cheaply.

**Jump point:**
- **When:** quit when the A$4M is soft-circled and ≥2 targets at ≤5x are in conversation.
- **Back-out:** the A$3.2M baseline favours staying until then. The PE-backed executive role is the fallback if 12 months of search produce no LOI.

**After deal 1 (years 2–4):**
- **Bolt-ons:** bolt on distributors from cash flow plus debt.
- **Second vertical:** add one vertical-software acquisition (C1) when a proprietary one appears.
- **Holdco raise:** raise a A$20–40M holdco vehicle around year 3 on the deal-1 track record.
- **Engine:** productise the origination and pricing engine for the holdco only (no outside sales that conflict with buying).

**Year 5+:**
- **Credit sleeve:** with 4–6 deals and a record, a small credit sleeve (C6) or a sponsor fund (C5) becomes raisable on real numbers.
- **Not before:** fund-manager and MGA paths (C6, C8) and the VC paths (C7) get **easier after a sponsor track record**, not before it.

**How candidates combine:**
- **Spine:** C5 (engine) feeds C4 (platform 1) and C1 (platform 2).
- **Option:** C0 is a parallel small co-invest.
- **Fallback:** C3 is the alternative spine if distribution owners won't share data or ask ≥6x.
- **Not on the critical path:** C2, C6, C7, C8 and C9.
