# 08 Acquisition entrepreneurship and holdco strategist

Lens: **buy, don't start.** Use a search fund, a self-funded search or a holdco to buy Australian SMEs from retiring owners, then lift them with AI, pricing and CFO discipline. 3 Nov 2026. Ran 16 web searches. Figures are labelled [verified: source], [estimate] or [unverified]. Most sources are search summaries, not primary documents.

## 1. Why this lens fits this founder and this moment

**The supply side is real.**
- About 48% of boomer SME owners (aged 60–78) intend to exit within 1–5 years, and 87% of those cite retirement [verified: MYOB Business Monitor, Aug 2025, via IT Brief].
- Only ~24% of SME owners have an exit plan [same source].
- More than 500k businesses (22%) are owned by people aged 60+ [verified: SmartCompany summary].
- Nearly half of SME owners expect retirement to force closure or a sale outside the family [verified: NAB, May 2025].

**Prices are low and the buyer pool is thin.**
- Australian SMEs under ~A$5M of value trade at roughly **2–5x EBITDA**. Brokers quote 3–6x for good service businesses [unverified: broker guides; no clean dataset exists].
- Australia has no SBA-style government loan scheme and only a tiny search-fund ecosystem. Australia's first traditional search-fund acquisition was Voyager Equity's purchase of Inerva in Nov 2021 [verified: searchfunder / podcast]. Searchfunder threads in 2024–26 still describe raising money with no local ecosystem.
- Fewer qualified buyers means lower entry multiples. That is the mispricing this lens exploits.

**Exit prices are much higher at scale.**
- Example: SCEE bought Force Fire from Anacacia for ~A$53.5M (Mar 2025), and BGIS bought Fire Services Queensland for ~A$28.6M (Apr 2025) [verified: MergerLinks listings; multiples not disclosed].
- Listed business services trade around 9.4x EV/EBITDA [verified: InterFinancial dashboard].
- Buying at 3.5–5x and selling a scaled platform at 7–10x is the core engine. AI and pricing are accelerants on top.

**The track record is strong but skewed.**
- Stanford 2026 study: US/Canadian search funds returned **33.9% aggregate IRR and 4.75x** [verified: Stanford GSB summaries].
- About 25% of acquisitions lost money, and only ~48–58% of searches ever acquire. The median search takes ~20 months. The median 2024–25 deal was ~US$16M of price on ~US$2.5M of EBITDA [verified: secondary summaries of the Stanford study].
- International (IESE): 30% IRR, 2.8x [verified: IESE summary].

**Why this founder has unfair inputs.**
- **Credibility to raise.** His BCG background and a prior exit to Australia Post make him the profile family offices back.
- **CFO-grade underwriting.** Most SME buyers are tradespeople or ex-bankers. He can run a quality-of-earnings review himself.
- **Statistics for pricing.** Pricing is the single biggest lever in under-managed SMEs.
- **Hands-on AI building**, for back-office cost-out.
- **Operator experience.** He has built and sold a physical-operations business.
- **Weak spots.**
  - He has never run a P&L of 100+ blue-collar staff.
  - His no-personal-guarantee preference clashes with Australian bank habits. Banks dislike lending against goodwill and typically lend ~50% of price with PGs [unverified: searchfunder forum, Jan 2025].
  - His current employer's outside-interest clause limits how far a search can go while he is still employed.

**The bar to beat.** Staying employed is worth ≈ A$2.0–2.4M of 7-year wealth (earlier rounds), or roughly **A$3.0–3.5M over 10 years** [estimate]. Outcome values below are 10-year founder wealth: after tax, equity after CGT (~23.5% effective on discounted gains) plus accumulated after-tax savings from pay.

## 2. Ideas generated (long list)

1. A Victorian compliance-services roll-up (essential-safety inspections, fire, backflow, test-and-tag, lifts).
2. An Australia/New Zealand vertical-software holdco (Constellation for the long tail).
3. A pricing-led B2B distribution holdco.
4. An accounting/bookkeeping practice consolidation with AI. Rejected: Kelly Partners already runs it at scale (~A$164M run-rate revenue, ~1 deal a month) [verified: KPG ASX announcement, Dec 2025], and other stage-1 lenses (AI services, finance function) cover it.
5. An insurance-broker book roll-up. Rejected: small broker deals price at ~7.5–12x EBITDA [unverified], and Steadfast and AUB are being bid for by Amwins/Dragoneer (A$7.7B) and KKR. Entry is too expensive and arbitrage is thin.
6. Rent-roll acquisitions. Already covered by the hybrid from earlier rounds, so excluded.
7. Electrical, solar and battery installers (energy transition). Rejected: project-based, warranty tail, no recurring revenue.
8. NDIS and aged-care providers. Rejected: regulatory and funding-cliff risk, reputational exposure.
9. Freight forwarders and 3PLs. Rejected: Fulfilio fit, but ops-heavy and density-driven, which earlier rounds penalised.
10. Specialist B2B testing labs (NATA-accredited). Kept as a sub-thesis of #1.
11. **A succession-capital platform: an AI deal-origination engine plus an independent sponsor / search accelerator** (the bold idea).
12. Buying a sub-scale listed microcap to use as a permanent-capital vehicle. Rejected for now: governance and ASX overhead. It is an exit route for #2 or #3 later.

**The best four:** #1, #2, #3 and #11.

## Idea A: Victorian compliance-services platform ("ESM-Co")

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | Buy a Melbourne essential-safety-measures (ESM) and fire-maintenance contractor with A$1.5–2.5M EBITDA. Bolt on adjacent periodic-inspection trades (backflow, emergency lighting, test-and-tag, lifts, NATA labs). Building owners, owners corporations and facility managers pay recurring annual contracts. |
| **Why now (2026)** | The boomer-owned trade-contractor tail is retiring. Demand is non-discretionary: under Building Regulations 2018 Pt 15, owners must maintain ESMs and produce an Annual ESM Report, and the duty can't be passed to tenants (Building Act s 251) [verified: VBA/council guides]. | **Market & model** | Victorian ESM/fire maintenance market ≈ A$0.8–1.5B [estimate; the national fire protection industry is commonly cited at A$3–4B+, unverified]. Fragmented: hundreds of contractors under A$5M revenue [estimate]. Model: annual inspection contracts (sticky, ~90% renewal [estimate]) plus defect-rectification work (higher margin). Buy at 4–5x, bolt-ons at 3–4x, exit at 7–9x to PE or strategics (SCEE, BGIS, Wormald-type) [estimate]. |
| **Capital & structure** | Platform EV ~A$8–11M. Funding: senior debt ~2–2.5x EBITDA (cash-flow or private credit; expect PG pressure), vendor note 15–20%, equity ~A$3.5–4.5M. Founder puts in A$0.8M; family offices and BCG-network investors put in the rest under a self-funded search structure. **Founder ownership ~30–40%** (own capital plus ~15–20% promote over a hurdle). Search-fund route instead: ~25% vesting common with no founder capital at risk. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | P(fail) 0.20 → A$1.5M (equity lost, back to a job by year 3–4); P(modest) 0.35 → A$3.5M (single business, ~A$2.5M EBITDA, sold at 5x); P(good) 0.30 → A$8M (A$5M EBITDA platform at 7x); P(great) 0.15 → A$18M (A$10M EBITDA at 8–9x). **EV ≈ A$6.6M; median ≈ A$3.5M.** |
| **Time to replace $500k** | Search 6–18 months, mostly full-time. After close, CEO salary A$300–350k from day 1; total cash A$500k+ by **~24–30 months from start**, once pricing lands. The search can begin while employed (broker screening, investor soft-circling), subject to his outside-interest clause. Operating requires quitting. |
| **Job satisfaction** | Medium. Pricing, reporting automation and M&A are energising. Technician hiring, safety incidents and scheduling are daily grind. Lowest fit with CS/stats of the four. |
| **Key risks** | (1) Technician shortage caps growth and erodes margin. (2) Liability: an inspection miss followed by a fire means exposure, so insurance cost matters. (3) Key-person and customer loss at handover (facility-manager panel contracts). **Kill:** a mispriced first platform with high debt plus technician attrition above 25%. |
| **90-day test** | While employed: get 25+ Victorian targets of A$1–3M EBITDA from brokers and direct outreach. **Pass:** ≥3 owners willing to discuss at ≤5x with ≥85% contract renewal evident; ≥A$3M of soft-circled equity; one lender term sheet at ≥2x without a PG. **Kill:** asking prices ≥6x, or no lender without a PG. |

He also has the investor credibility. **Weak:** global consolidators are active in AU. Model: buy at 4–6x EBITDA, raise prices (most are under-priced and untouched for years), AI-rebuild support and dev, hold forever, and recycle cash into new deals at a 20%+ IRR hurdle [estimate]. |

## Idea C: Pricing-led B2B distribution holdco ("the 150-bps company") **(TOP PICK)**

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | Buy boomer-owned Australian specialty B2B distributors with A$20–60M revenue and A$1.5–4M EBITDA. Segments: industrial/MRO consumables, fasteners, hydraulics, packaging, HVAC parts, lab and medical consumables. Install statistical pricing, AI quoting and inventory science; bolt on adjacent distributors. Trade customers pay; value comes from margin expansion plus multiple arbitrage. |
| **Why now (2026)** | (1) The succession wave is heaviest in wholesale trade, a family-owned, low-glamour sector with 2–5x multiples [estimate]. (2) Pricing is the biggest lever. McKinsey estimates a 1% price rise lifts distributor EBITDA by ~22% [verified: McKinsey]. Vendor case studies show 100–310 bps gross-margin uplift [verified as vendor claims: PROS, Zilliant]. Owner-run AU distributors run on cost-plus spreadsheets and rep discretion [estimate]. (3) LLMs now make quoting, catalogue clean-up, email-order entry and demand forecasting cheap for a A$30M firm. Two years ago that required a PROS-scale budget. (4) Working capital is bank-lendable (receivables and inventory), so less goodwill-only debt and less PG pressure than services. |
| **Unfair inputs** | All of his main edges in one place. **Pricing under uncertainty** (statistician; earlier rounds' strongest edge). **Physical arbitrage and warehousing** (Fulfilio: slotting, 3PL, delivery hubs, Australia Post network for outbound). **CFO discipline** (working capital, cash conversion). **AI building** (quote agents, order entry). **Capital access.** **Weak:** no distribution-industry network; supplier relationships (exclusive agencies) are owner-held and must be transferred. |
| **Market & model** | Australian wholesale trade is a ~A$500B+ revenue sector [unverified, ABS order of magnitude], with thousands of distributors in the A$10–100M band [estimate]. Illustrative platform: A$35M revenue, 28% GM, A$2.5M EBITDA, bought at 4.5x (**A$11.3M**). A 150 bps GM lift = **+A$0.5M EBITDA**, plus AI back-office cost-out of ~A$0.3M, takes EBITDA to ~A$3.3M within 24–30 months [estimate]. Then bolt-ons at 3.5–4x, sharing warehouse and pricing engine. Target A$8–12M EBITDA by year 7–8; exit to PE or strategic at 7–9x [estimate; comparables to verify: Bapcor, Reece, Blackwoods' parent Wesfarmers buy at a premium]. |
| **Capital & structure** | Platform: senior/ABL debt ~A$5M (2x EBITDA, secured on receivables and stock), vendor note ~A$1.7M (15%), equity ~A$4.5M. Founder puts in A$0.9M and 8–15 investors A$3.6M (1x pref + 8% hurdle). **Founder ~30% fully diluted** (pro-rata 20% plus promote) [estimate]. Later bolt-ons funded from cash flow plus debt; one more equity raise expected. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | P(fail) 0.20 → A$1.5M (a supplier agency is lost or working capital squeezes; equity impaired); P(modest) 0.35 → A$4M (single platform at A$3–4M EBITDA, sold at 5–6x); P(good) 0.32 → A$9M (A$6M EBITDA at 7x); P(great) 0.13 → A$20M (A$10M EBITDA at 8x: ~A$55M equity × 30% ≈ A$16.5M pre-tax, ~A$12.6M after CGT, plus savings and dividends). **EV ≈ A$7.2M; median ≈ A$4.0M.** |
| **Time to replace $500k** | Search 6–12 months; distributors are more plentiful than quality software. CEO salary A$300–350k at close. **A$500k total cash by ~18–30 months from start** (pricing gains plus dividends at ~A$3.3M EBITDA). Of the four, this has the fastest credible path combined with the highest median. The search can start while employed; operating requires quitting. |
| **Job satisfaction** | High. Data science on real transactions with immediate P&L feedback; warehouse and network design (the Fulfilio muscle); M&A and capital allocation; building an AI-native operating system to deploy across bolt-ons. Grind: sales-rep culture change and supplier politics. |
| **Key risks** | (1) **Price-increase churn:** reps or key accounts leave when discounting is cut. Mitigate with test-and-control rollout and guardrails. (2) **Supplier or agency concentration:** an exclusive brand pulls the agency on change of control. Diligence change-of-control clauses. (3) **Disintermediation** by Amazon Business or manufacturers going direct, plus cyclicality (construction and mining). **Kill:** the top-3 suppliers make up >40% of COGS without assignable agreements, or a pricing pilot loses >3% of volume for <100 bps. |
| **90-day test** | (1) Get 3 SME distributors (via brokers or his network) to share 24 months of invoice lines under NDA. Run a pricing-leakage analysis (discount dispersion and price waterfall) as a free "succession readiness" report. **Pass:** identified leakage ≥150 bps in ≥2 of 3. (2) Get ≥15 targets with A$1.5–4M EBITDA at ≤5x asks. (3) Get ≥A$3M soft-circled equity plus one ABL term sheet without a PG. **Kill:** leakage <75 bps (owners already price well), or asks ≥6x. Cost: ~A$15–25k (legal NDA templates, data tooling, broker meetings). |

## 3. Acquisition versus venture paths (the comparison the role asks for)

| | Acquisition (A/C) | Holdco (B) | Venture (e.g. a fresh AI startup) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting revenue | A$15–50M on day 1 | A$2–8M | 0 |
| Founder pay at A$500k | ~2–2.5 yrs | ~3–4 yrs | 5–8 yrs (seed and A rounds cap pay at ~A$200–300k) |
| P(lose most invested capital) | ~20–25% (Stanford: ~25% of deals lose money) | ~25% | ~70–80% |
| Founder ownership at outcome | 25–40% | 15–25% plus promote | 10–25% after 3–4 rounds |
| Right tail (10 yr) | A$15–25M | A$30M+ | A$50–200M+ (rare) |
| Fit with his edges | Pricing, CFO, capital allocation, physical ops | SaaS CFO, AI | Builder, AI; his CFO edge is under-used |

Acquisition replaces income fastest and has the highest median, while venture has the fattest tail. For a founder who must replace A$500k and has proven edges in pricing and capital allocation, buying is the better risk-adjusted start. A holdco can still incubate a venture-scale software product, such as the pricing engine, on top of real cash flows.

## 4. Top pick and ranking

**Top pick: C, the pricing-led B2B distribution holdco.** It is the only idea where all of his recurring edges compound inside one asset:
- statistical pricing (an immediate, measurable 100–300 bps lever on boomer-run cost-plus books);
- physical warehousing and network arbitrage (Fulfilio);
- CFO working-capital discipline;
- AI-built back office.

Entry multiples are low (~4–5x), and debt can be secured on stock and debtors, which eases the no-PG constraint. It has the fastest credible path to A$500k (~2 years) and the highest median (~A$4M against a ~A$3.2M stay-employed bar). Its pricing engine can later be productised across bolt-ons, or for other holdcos, giving a software option on top.

The honest caveat: it beats staying employed **mainly through the tails**. EV is ~A$7.2M, but the median is only modestly above employment. Run the 90-day pricing-leakage test before quitting.

**Ranked list:**
1. **C. Pricing-led distribution holdco.** EV ≈ A$7.2M, median ≈ A$4.0M; A$500k in ~2 yrs.
2. **B. ANZ vertical-software holdco.** EV ≈ A$8.0M (fattest tail but most uncertain), median ≈ A$3.5M; A$500k in ~3+ yrs. Best fit with his job; AI-substitution risk is the swing factor.
3. **D. Succession-capital platform (bold).** EV ≈ A$7.8M, median ≈ A$4.0M; can start while employed; slow income. It is the natural phase 2 of C or B, once one deal proves the playbook.
4. **A. Victorian compliance-services platform.** EV ≈ A$6.6M, median ≈ A$3.5M; fast income but the weakest fit and most ops-heavy.

**Suggested sequence:**
1. Run C's and B's 90-day tests in parallel while employed (~A$25k total).
2. Close one platform.
3. Use the deal-scoring engine (the D core, which shares DNA with the earlier rent-roll engine) as the holdco's proprietary origination.
4. Raise the sponsor or holdco capital only after deal #1 shows a ≥150 bps lift.
