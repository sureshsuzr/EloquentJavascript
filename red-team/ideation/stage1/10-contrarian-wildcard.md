# Stage 1 — Contrarian wildcard lens

Agent 10, 3 Nov 2026. Lens: ignore the obvious and look for high-ambition, non-consensus ideas that fit this founder's particular mix (CS + statistics + CFO + logistics founder + marketplace CEO + AI fluency + Melbourne + credibility with capital). 13 web searches. Figures are labelled **[verified: source]** (search summary of a named source; several were secondary and are flagged), **[estimate]** or **[unverified]**.

## How I read the brief

The earlier rounds give a clear signal: the founder's edge is **pricing an asset under uncertainty and then owning it**, not running a dense operating business and not selling reports to a thin market. The rent-roll engine ranked first because the edge is worth $50–100k per deal to the buyer and only $3–15k per report to a reader. The contrarian question is therefore: **where is a large, liquid asset class being mispriced in 2026 because the consensus is scared or not paying attention, and where does this founder's mix let him buy, build or underwrite it?**

Three 2026 dislocations stand out:
1. **The "SaaSpocalypse".** After Anthropic's Claude Cowork launch (12 Jan 2026) and its plugins, about US$300bn left software stocks in two days in early Feb 2026. IGV fell about 21–32% YTD by March **[verified: Motley Fool, Fintool, Bloomberg headline; figures vary by source]**. The consensus is that AI kills small seat-based SaaS. The contrarian view is that for sticky, regulated, workflow-embedded *vertical* software, AI is a cost-out and repricing tool for the owner.
2. **The agent economy has no balance sheet behind it.** Enterprises are deploying agents that can make costly mistakes. Insurers are retreating from AI risk and specialist MGAs are filling the gap.
3. **Energy-system flexibility is being mandated, not just encouraged.** On 22 Sep 2026 Victoria required new data centres to match new load with new renewables and storage **[verified: Premier of Victoria, Energy-Storage.news]**. Meanwhile C&I storage "has remained absent" from the NEM build-out **[verified: Modo Energy headline, Aug 2026]**.

## Idea 1 — "Bought in the panic": an AI-era vertical-software holdco for ANZ

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | Buy profitable, founder-owned ANZ vertical-market software (A$1–4M EBITDA; niches like trades compliance, salary packaging, freight/transport, allied health, local government, agribusiness) at panic-depressed multiples. Run them with an AI-native shared operating core (support, implementation, code maintenance, finance) and reprice from seats to outcomes or agents. Customers pay subscriptions; returns come from cash yield plus multiple arbitrage at holdco exit or IPO. |
| **Why now (2026)** | (a) Sentiment: the SaaSpocalypse discounted *all* software, including sticky vertical systems of record that agents need to read and write. One Q1 2026 commentary says sub-scale SaaS valuations are about half of 30 months earlier **[unverified: low-authority blog]**. (b) The proof the playbook works: Bending Spoons (50+ acquisitions, deep post-deal cost-out) listed on Nasdaq in July 2026 at about US$18.4bn **[verified: Axios, Forbes Italia]**. (c) Coding agents have cut the cost to maintain legacy codebases, which is the main operating cost of small VMS. (d) Founder succession: many 2000s-era ANZ vertical-software founders are 55–70. |
| **Unfair inputs** | **Strong:** CFO in a SaaS company *today*, so he reads SaaS P&Ls, cohorts and churn natively. Ex-BCG and prior exit make him credible to family offices and lenders. CS plus heavy agent use lets him personally specify the AI-native operating core, which most PE operators can't. His statistical pricing skill drives underwriting each deal (retention, price elasticity, cost-out achievability). This **generalises the leading rent-roll hybrid** (pricing edge captured as an *investor*) to a far bigger and more liquid asset class. **Weak spots:** he has never done a buy-out. Competing buyers exist: Constellation via Caudex/Jonas AU, Valsoft/Fluent (bought Eziway in 2026), Arcadea (bought JAIX) **[verified: Caudex site, Moore/PrivSource summaries]**. He may also have an employer conflict, since his employer is a SaaS company; check outside-interest clauses before sourcing. |
| **Market & model** | ANZ has hundreds of private vertical-software businesses with A$1–10M revenue **[estimate; no census]**. Moore puts mid-market IT/software at a 15.7x median EBITDA multiple in FY26 (12.7x FY24–25) **[verified: Moore Australia APAC M&A FY26]**. Small private deals (<A$3M EBITDA) typically trade at about 4–7x **[estimate]**. Model: buy at 4.5–6x, take EBITDA margin from ~30% to ~45% via AI shared services, grow price 5–8%/yr. The holdco re-rates to 10–14x at A$20M+ EBITDA. Comparables: Constellation, Bending Spoons, Valsoft, Lumine. Search-fund base rate: 33.9% aggregate IRR, 4.75x ROI, but only 58% ever acquire **[verified: Stanford GSB 2026 study via secondary summaries]**. |
| **Capital & structure** | Independent-sponsor / holdco hybrid. Founder co-invests A$0.6–0.9M (inside his range, no personal guarantee: senior debt and vendor notes sit at opco level). Deal 1 is about A$8–12M EV, funded ~45% senior debt and vendor finance and ~55% equity from 5–10 family offices/ex-exit angels, with a 5-year earn-out for the vendor. Later deals are funded by cash flow plus a A$20–40M holdco equity raise around year 3. Founder ownership: ~20–30% after promote and co-invest at deal 1, diluting to **~15–20%** at year 10. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | Founder wealth after tax, total, including salary drawn. Bar = staying employed, about A$3.2M at 10 yrs **[estimate, extrapolated from A$2.0–2.4M at 7 yrs]**. **Fail 25%** (no deal, or deal 1 impaired by AI churn): **A$1.8M**. **Modest 35%** (2–3 businesses, EBITDA ~A$6M, small exit): **A$3.5M**. **Good 30%** (6–8 businesses, EBITDA ~A$15–20M, exit ~10x): **A$10M**. **Great 10%** (A$35–40M EBITDA platform, IPO or secondary at 12x): **A$40M**. **EV ≈ A$8.5M; median ≈ A$3.5M** (modest band). |
| **Time to replace $500k** | Holdco CEO salary of A$300–400k from deal 1 close (~month 9–15); total comp of $500k at 2–3 businesses (~year 3). **Partly while employed:** thesis, deal screening, lender and investor soundings (≈months 0–6, subject to the employment contract). Signing deal 1 requires quitting. |
| **Job satisfaction** | Very high fit. It combines capital allocation, pricing/underwriting, building an AI-native operating core (a hands-on CS role), and recruiting GMs. It is a builder role with a CFO's discipline, and closer to Fulfilio's "assemble from nothing" energy than running one SaaS company. |
| **Key risks** | (1) **The contrarian bet is wrong:** agents really do commoditise small vertical SaaS, so churn and price erosion eat the cost-out. This kills it if gross revenue retention falls below ~85%. (2) **Competition for deals** from Constellation/Valsoft pushes entry multiples to 7x+, removing the arbitrage. (3) **First-time buy-out execution:** integration, key-person loss, covenants. Mitigation: underwrite to "no multiple expansion, 5% churn uplift" downside cases; buy only systems of record with regulated data or payments flows. |
| **90-day test** | (a) Build a long list of ≥150 ANZ VMS targets (A$1–10M revenue) from ABN data, ASIC filings, app marketplaces and brokers; contact 40 founders; **kill if <8 are willing to talk and <3 at ≤6x EBITDA**. (b) Indicative capital from 2 lenders (senior/unitranche) at ≥2.5x EBITDA, plus soft circles from family offices of **≥A$4M; kill if <A$2M**. (c) For one target, a 2-week AI cost-out spike (support deflection, code maintenance by agents) showing **≥25% opex reduction without touching sales**. Cash cost <A$30k. Clear employment conflicts first. |

## Idea 2 — Underwriting the agent economy: an AI-agent liability MGA for Australia and APAC

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | A managing general agent (Lloyd's coverholder or AU insurer binder) that certifies and insures businesses' AI agents against errors, hallucination losses, IP/privacy breaches and runaway actions. It uses an agent audit standard plus live telemetry for pricing. Paid by enterprises and SaaS vendors (premium, of which the MGA keeps ~25–30% commission plus audit fees), and possibly embedded into AI vendors' contracts ("insured agent" warranties). |
| **Why now (2026)** | Insurers are retreating from AI risk while specialists move in. Armilla (Lloyd's coverholder, Chaucer/Axis capacity) raised limits to US$25M **[verified: Armilla release; date inconsistent]**. AIUC raised a US$40M Series A in Sep 2026 (US$55M total) for audit plus insurance, and ElevenLabs bought the first AIUC-1-backed policy in Feb 2026 **[verified: Coverager, Fintech Global; partly secondary]**. Deloitte projects global AI-insurance premiums of ~US$4.8bn by 2032 at ~80%/yr growth, from ~US$80M in 2025 **[verified: Insurance Business citing Deloitte]**. Australia's automated-decision disclosure rules start 10 Dec 2026 (from round 2). Nobody local owns APAC. |
| **Unfair inputs** | **Strong:** a statistician who prices uncertainty with little history (exactly Armilla's claim to fame), CS/agent fluency to design telemetry-based underwriting, and CFO and capital credibility for capacity providers. **Weak spots (serious):** no insurance, actuarial-licensing or Lloyd's network. Needs an experienced underwriter co-founder and an AFSL (or authorised-representative status). Australia is a small premium pool. |
| **Market & model** | APAC ~10–15% of the 2032 pool = US$0.5–0.7bn; Australia ~US$100–200M **[estimate]**. At 15% AU share and 30% commission, AU revenue ≈ A$7–14M plus audit fees. Becoming a venture-scale outcome needs APAC-wide or white-label pricing for global carriers. Comparables: Armilla, AIUC, Coalition (cyber MGA → US$5bn) **[unverified for Coalition valuation]**. |
| **Capital & structure** | VC-backed: A$3–5M seed (incl. capacity-partner strategic), A$15–25M Series A by year 3. Founder ownership ~20–25% at exit after 3 rounds. Personal capital at risk ≤A$300k. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | **Fail 55%** (capacity withdrawn after early losses, or global players arrive): **A$1.8M**. **Modest 20%** (acqui-hire by a broker or insurer): **A$3M**. **Good 17%** (APAC leader, A$30–50M revenue, ~A$200M exit): **A$12M**. **Great 8%** (category leader, global white-label pricing engine): **A$50M**. **EV ≈ A$7.6M; median ≈ A$1.8–3M** (very right-skewed). |
| **Time to replace $500k** | VC-funded salary of A$250–300k from seed. $500k total comp only after Series B (~year 4–5). Can't really be built while employed (needs a regulated entity and a full-time underwriter), but a 90-day validation can be done. |
| **Job satisfaction** | High intellectual fit (pricing under uncertainty, adversarial testing of agents). Heavy regulatory, broker and capacity-provider work could drain energy. |
| **Key risks** | (1) **A big early loss** (correlated: one model bug, many insureds) causes capacity to pull out. This kills it. (2) **Incumbents bundle AI cover into cyber/PI** at near-zero marginal price. (3) **Global specialists** (AIUC, Armilla) expand into APAC with more capital. |
| **90-day test** | (a) 25 interviews with Australian enterprises/SaaS vendors deploying customer-facing agents: **≥8 say they'd pay ≥A$25k/yr for cover or a certification their buyers demand; kill if <4**. (b) 2 capacity conversations (Lloyd's syndicate via a London broker, plus one AU insurer): **≥1 term sheet for a binder or pilot; kill if none**. (c) Recruit an underwriter co-founder candidate. Cash cost <A$25k. |

Hosts pay through a 10-year shared-savings or capacity contract (demand-charge cuts, backup, compliance with the Vic data-centre rule). The fleet earns wholesale arbitrage and FCAS through an aggregator licence. Value comes from a growing, contracted asset base financed with infrastructure debt. |
| **Why now (2026)** | Vic data-centre rules (Sep 2026): new load must be matched by new renewables and storage, developers pay network upgrades, and they must offer demand flexibility "to the maximum extent" **[verified: Premier of Victoria, Energy-Storage.news; ESS-News differs in detail]**. C&I storage is the missing piece of the NEM build-out **[verified: Modo Energy headline]**. Residential batteries are taken (500k+ installed, Amber with >40–50% of automated home batteries and a A$78.5M Series E) **[verified: Energy-Storage.news, Forbes AU]**, so the gap is C&I, not homes. |
| **Unfair inputs** | **Strong:** Fulfilio's real edge was consolidating under-used warehouse assets, and he knows warehouse owners, 3PLs and Australia Post property. As CFO he can structure project finance (CEFC, green banks, infra debt). His statistics fit dispatch/trading optimisation and degradation pricing. **Weak spots:** no energy-market, NEM-registration or electrical-engineering background. Trading needs a specialist partner (Habitat, GridBeyond, Tesla Autobidder) at first. |
| **Market & model** | Victorian C&I sites over 160 MWh/yr number in the tens of thousands **[estimate]**. Data centres need hundreds of MW of matched storage over 10 years **[estimate]**. Unit economics: a 1 MW/2 MWh C&I battery costs ~A$0.8–1.1M installed and earns ~A$150–250k/yr from stacked revenue → 9–14% unlevered IRR **[estimate; highly sensitive to spreads]**. No personal guarantees. Founder ownership of devco ~30–40%, equal to ~10–15% look-through of the platform at year 10. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | **Fail 35%** (spreads compress, hosts won't sign 10-year deals): **A$1.9M**. **Modest 35%** (100–200 MWh fleet, sold to a retailer): **A$3.8M**. **Good 22%** (500 MWh–1 GWh, infra-fund buyout): **A$9M**. **Great 8%** (national large-load flexibility platform including data centres): **A$28M**. **EV ≈ A$6.2M; median ≈ A$3.8M.** |

This kills it if stacked revenue falls below ~A$110k/MW/yr. (2) **Host contract and tariff risk:** network tariff redesign removes demand-charge savings. (3) **Data-centre rule softened** after industry pushback (the DCCA warned projects may become unviable). |
| **90-day test** | (a) Pull 12 months of interval data from 15 warehouse/cold-store hosts (via his network). Model stacked value: **proceed if median ≥A$160k/MW/yr; kill if <A$110k**. (b) 3 data-centre developers: **≥1 LOI for storage-as-a-service** for Vic compliance. (c) A debt indication (CEFC or bank) at ≥60% gearing. Cash cost <A$40k. |

## Top pick and why

**Idea 1: the AI-era vertical-software holdco, bought in the SaaSpocalypse.**

- **It is the leading rent-roll hybrid's logic at 10–100x the market depth.** He captures a pricing and underwriting edge as an *owner*, not as a report vendor. Instead of 250–900 rent-roll deals a year in a niche, there are hundreds of ANZ vertical-software businesses, and the asset class has a proven, re-rating exit path (Constellation, Bending Spoons at ~US$18bn).
- **It is contrarian in the right way.** The consensus is that AI kills small software. The founder's edge (a SaaS CFO who builds with agents) is to tell systems of record that AI makes *cheaper to run and easier to reprice* apart from seat-based tools AI will hollow out. That judgement is exactly the scarce input.
- **It best satisfies the brief's constraints at once.** A salary from the first deal (~year 1–1.5), $500k total comp around year 3, ~70% chance of beating staying employed, the highest EV *and* a decent median, personal capital inside A$0.6–0.9M with no personal guarantee, and day-to-day work that mixes capital allocation, building and pricing.
- **Combine, don't choose.** The rent-roll engine can sit inside the holdco as a capability (both are "buy recurring books, price retention, integrate with AI"), and the PM-software and roll-up lessons from rounds 1–3 transfer directly.
- **What would change my mind:** the 90-day test shows entry multiples at ≥7x for quality targets (no arbitrage), or the cost-out spike shows <15% opex reduction. Either outcome makes the holdco an ordinary PE play without an edge, and Idea 3 (physical arbitrage, contracted assets) moves up.

**Ranked:** 1) Vertical-software holdco (EV ~A$8.5M) · 2) AI-agent liability MGA (EV ~A$7.6M, low median) · 3) Behind-the-meter storage fleet (EV ~A$6.2M) · 4) Unlisted-asset valuation engine + secondaries (EV ~A$5.6M).
