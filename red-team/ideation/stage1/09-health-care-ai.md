# 09: Health, aged care and the care economy (operator strategist lens)

Ideation agent 09, 3 Nov 2026. Lens: large, government-funded, admin-heavy sectors where AI and better operations create value. For each idea I asked whether the founder should be the operator, sell software, or run a roll-up.

**Source caveat.** I used 17 web searches. Most aged-care trade press (The Weekly Source) was blocked for direct fetch, so many figures rest on search-result summaries of those articles. They are labelled **[verified: source, via summary]** and should be checked against the primary StewartBrown, GEN and IHACPA documents before acting.

**Common yardstick.** Every outcome below is the founder's **10-year after-tax wealth on that path**. If the venture fails, this includes his return to employment afterwards. Staying employed for 10 years scales the brief's 7-year bar (A$2.0–2.4M) to roughly **A$3.2M [estimate]**. A "fail" outcome is therefore not zero. It is about A$1.8–2.6M, reflecting 2–3 years of lost income plus lost capital.

---

## 1. What the sector looks like in late 2026 (the facts that drive the ideas)

**Residential aged care: distressed operators, scarce beds, deep capital pool**
- **Losses are widespread.** 65% of residential operators in the StewartBrown survey made an operating loss in FY26, up from 62% at March 2026 and 49% a year earlier **[verified: StewartBrown FY26, via The Weekly Source / Inside Ageing summaries]**.
- **Sector size.** About 2,500 facilities and 225,000 places, with occupancy up to ~90%. Senior-living transactions run at about A$3B a year **[verified: CBRE Australian Senior Living 2026, via summary]**.
- **Supply is lagging demand.** Supply has grown ~0.7% a year since 2020 against forecast demand growth of ~4% a year. Only 802 net beds were added in FY25, against a projected need of more than 10,000 a year **[verified via summary: CBRE; Ansell Strategic Board Pack Q1 FY26]**.
- **Beds trade below what they cost to build.** 2026 deals were done at A$154k–350k per bed, net of RADs; replacement cost is about A$500k per bed **[verified via summary]**:
  - vacant Mercy Newport: ~A$154k per bed
  - Medical and Aged Care Group, two Melbourne homes: ~A$200k
  - Regis/Rockpool: A$225k
  - Trinity Burwood (vacant): A$280k
  - Regis/OC Health: ~A$350k
- **New Act cash flows (from 1 Nov 2025).**
  - **Accommodation:** providers retain 2% of the RAD each year, capped at 5 years (10%), and can charge a higher everyday living fee **[verified: Challenger; adviser guides]**.
  - **RAD pool:** about **A$30.2B in RADs** is held, against only A$5.3B of new capital invested **[verified: headline, The Weekly Source]**. The big four banks hold about A$9B of exposure **[unverified: headline only]**.
  - **Financing gap:** ACFA says many providers can't get commercial debt despite holding RADs. Specialist lenders such as La Trobe are entering **[verified via summary]**.

**Home care (Support at Home, live since 1 Nov 2025): margins have collapsed and the sector is consolidating**
- **Market size.** 950 approved Support at Home providers and 338,049 people receiving care, with 100,191 waiting, at 31 Mar 2026 **[verified: GEN Support at Home Program Data Report Q3 2025–26]**.
- **Margins.** The average operating result was **−A$0.46 per client day** in the March 2026 quarter. Year-to-date EBITDA fell to **A$425 per client per year**, from A$1,381 a year earlier **[verified: StewartBrown Support at Home survey, via summary]**. StewartBrown says a 9.5% margin is the "investible" level.
- **Regulation.** Care management is capped at 10% of each budget. Price caps were due on 1 July 2026; the government has since signalled phased or delayed implementation **[verified: health.gov.au fact sheets; Weekly Source headline Sep 2026]**.
- **Consolidation deals:**
  - myHomecare (Quadrant PE) bought Let's Get Care, creating a provider with about 18,000 clients.
  - Prestige Inhome Care made three acquisitions in eight weeks, including Omni-Care in Melbourne.
  - HammondCare is taking Anglicare Sydney's ~3,200 clients.
  - Regis bought Royal Freemasons Victoria's home care business.
  - Kirinari merged with LiveBetter.
  - **[verified via summaries]**

**Hospitals, NDIS and clinical AI**
- **Private hospitals.**
  - The sector turns over about A$22–24B a year, roughly two-thirds funded by insurers **[unverified: vendor market study]**.
  - Healthscope went into receivership in May 2025 with more than A$1.6B of debt and is being broken up **[verified: Healthscope receivers' release; press]**.
  - Clinical coding is short of staff. Northern Health has moved to AI-assisted coding. HIMAA has published an AI adoption guide but says there is no Australian evidence yet **[verified: Healthcare IT News; Medical Republic]**.
- **AI scribes are taken.** Heidi Health (Melbourne) raised at about US$0.9–1.26B in Sep 2026 on ~US$50M annualised revenue **[verified: Forbes AU / Pulse2 / Startup Daily; the two valuation figures conflict]**.
- **NDIS.** The 2026 "Securing the NDIS" amendments do three things that matter here **[verified: health.gov.au fact sheets; ndis.gov.au]**:
  - create a government **panel of plan managers**, who may provide only plan management;
  - move price-setting to the Minister;
  - leave plan-management prices unchanged for 2026–27.

**What follows.** Value is being created where **capital meets distressed, regulated physical capacity** (residential beds) and where **sub-scale operators must sell** (home care). Pure admin AI (scribes, coding) is real but crowded and needs clinical-sector distribution the founder lacks. This fits the pattern from earlier rounds: his edge is **underwriting and capital allocation, plus physical-asset arbitrage**, not running ops-heavy density businesses.

## 3. The four kept ideas

### Idea 1: Residential aged-care "underwrite-and-own" platform (bold)

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | A capital platform (fund or deal-by-deal syndicate) that buys distressed or exiting not-for-profit aged-care homes below replacement cost and turns them around with a hired experienced operator and an in-house underwriting/AI operations engine. Family offices, PE and later super funds supply equity; the founder earns fees, carry and co-invest returns. |
| **Why now (2026)** | 65% of homes lose money, so motivated sellers abound, especially single-site not-for-profits. The new Act adds long-dated cash flows: 2% a year RAD retention, the higher everyday living fee and AN-ACC indexation. Supply is frozen (802 net beds in FY25), so existing beds are scarce. Assets trade at A$150–350k per bed against ~A$500k replacement cost. A financing gap (ACFA: many providers shut out of debt) means capital is the binding constraint. Bain/Estia and Regis show the strategy can be backed. |
| **Unfair inputs** | **Strong:** pricing and underwriting under uncertainty (occupancy, AN-ACC mix, RAD flows, refurbishment capex). CFO-grade modelling. Credibility to raise from his exit investors, BCG alumni and family offices. Fulfilio-style physical arbitrage (buying idle or under-used capacity below its value). A heavy AI user who can build a rostering/AN-ACC classification/occupancy engine. **Weak spots:** no aged-care operating or clinical background; no regulator relationships; first-time fund manager. Under the new Act he would be a "responsible person" with personal duties **[unverified detail; check the Act]**. **He must partner with a proven operator CEO**, for example a former Regis, Estia or Bupa executive. |
| **Market & model** | ~2,500 homes and 225k places; senior-living deals ~A$3B a year **[verified via summary]**. Illustrative platform: 600–800 beds over 3 years at ~A$220k per bed net (~A$150M enterprise value), funded by ~A$70M equity plus debt, with the RAD float as working capital. Lift EBITDA per bed from roughly break-even to ~A$15–20k through occupancy, correct AN-ACC classification, higher everyday living fee and additional services, rostering, and procurement **[estimate]**. Exit at ~A$350–400k per bed or 10–12x EBITDA to Regis, Bupa, PE or offshore buyers (an overseas buyer set the A$280k benchmark at Burwood). Value created ~A$80–120M **[estimate]**. Comparables: Regis roll-ups; Bupa's 10 homes (1,114 beds) for ~A$225M; Bain's take-private of Estia. |
| **Capital & structure** | Phase 1: deal-by-deal syndicate (1–2 homes, ~A$15–30M equity) via a special-purpose vehicle. Phase 2: a A$100–200M fund. Founder puts in A$0.5–0.8M co-invest, with no personal guarantees (non-recourse asset-level debt). He owns ~40–60% of the GP alongside the operator partner, taking a 1.5% management fee and 20% carry over an 8% hurdle. Optional later: a credit sleeve lending to providers against RAD and asset cover. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | Fail 35% → A$1.8M (cannot raise, or first deal stalls, then returns to work). Modest 30% → A$3.5M (one syndicate, fees cover salary, small promote). Good 25% → A$8M (A$150M fund, ~2x gross; founder share of carry ~A$6–7M plus fees). Great 10% → A$22M (fund II, ~A$500M+ assets under management, platform sale). **EV ≈ A$5.9M; median ≈ A$3.5M.** |
| **Time to replace $500k** | 18–36 months. GP fees pay a A$400–500k salary once ≥A$100M is committed or two syndicates have closed. The engine, target list and capital soft-circling can be built while employed; the first acquisition needs him full time. |
| **Job satisfaction** | Deal origination, underwriting, raising capital, turnaround strategy, building an AI operations layer: very strong fit with his CS, statistics, CFO and builder skills, and visibly meaningful work. The cost: resident incidents, media scrutiny and regulator interaction are emotionally heavy, and he is accountable for care he doesn't deliver. |
| **Key risks** | **1. Reputational/care failure:** one serious neglect case destroys the brand, and possibly the fund. **2. Policy:** changes to RAD rules (the Royal Commission recommended phasing RADs out; ACFA currently recommends keeping them), accommodation price caps or care-minute mandates can move value overnight. **3. Workforce:** award rises, 24/7 RN rules and agency costs. **What kills it:** no top-tier operator partner, or bids for decent assets clearing above ~A$300k per bed net (the arbitrage closes). |
| **90-day test (~A$20–40k)** | (a) Build the underwriting engine on public data (GEN facility data, star ratings, quality indicators, occupancy, AN-ACC mix) and rank ~200 sub-scale homes. (b) Get information memoranda or bid packs on ≥5 live deals from CBRE/Colliers and model them. (c) Sign or term-sheet an operator partner. (d) Soft-circle capital. **Go:** ≥A$15M in soft commitments from ≥3 family offices or PE, a credible operator partner, and ≥2 deals pricing ≤A$250k per bed net with a believable path to ≥A$15k EBITDA per bed. **Kill:** any of these fails. |

### Idea 2: Home-care consolidation platform (lead executive + co-invest + underwriting/integration engine)

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | Lead (as CEO/CFO and co-founder), or co-invest in, a PE- or family-office-backed Support at Home roll-up. It buys sub-scale providers (<500 clients) that can't survive at −A$0.46 per client day, and moves them onto one AI-native back office: care-management triage, scheduling, claiming, contribution billing and compliance evidence. |
| **Why now (2026)** | Margins collapsed after Support at Home started (EBITDA A$425 per client per year). The 10% care-management cap and coming price caps force scale. 950 providers serving 338k clients is a long tail. Buyers are active: myHomecare/Quadrant, Prestige, HammondCare and Regis. 100k people waiting is guaranteed demand. |
| **Unfair inputs** | Strong: CFO/ops leadership, acquisition underwriting (the same skill as rent-roll pricing, applied to client books), AI back-office build, capital credibility. Weak: home care is a **density/workforce business**, the pattern that failed in earlier rounds. He has no sector network. Large PE platforms already run this playbook. |
| **Market & model** | 338k clients × average budget ~A$30k a year ≈ A$10B a year of funded services **[estimate; class budgets range from ~A$11k to ~A$78k a year, unverified]**. Platform target: 5–8k clients ≈ A$150–240M revenue. At 6–8% EBITDA that is A$9–19M, worth ~A$80–170M at 8–10x **[estimate]**. Books are bought at low multiples because sellers are loss-making. |
| **Capital & structure** | Sponsor equity A$30–60M. Founder co-invests A$0.3–0.5M plus 5–10% sweat equity on a PE vesting ladder. Salary of A$400–500k is paid from day one, so his income is replaced immediately. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | Fail 25% → A$2.6M (salary for ~3–4 years, equity worthless). Modest 35% → A$4.0M. Good 30% → A$7M. Great 10% → A$15M. **EV ≈ A$5.7M; median ≈ A$4.0M** (the highest median of the four, because of the salary floor). |
| **Time to replace $500k** | 0–6 months once hired or backed. It can't really start while employed beyond networking and modelling the books. |
| **Job satisfaction** | Medium. M&A and systems build are energising. But years 2–6 are dominated by workforce, complaints, rostering and regulator audits, and he answers to a PE board. This is less "built from nothing" than Fulfilio. |
| **Key risks** | **1.** Price caps and award increases compress margins faster than integration savings arrive. **2.** Client churn after acquisition (clients can switch provider freely) and care-worker attrition. **3.** Crowding: PE platforms bid up the good books. **What kills it:** caps set below a ~9.5% "investible" margin for providers with 5,000+ clients. |
| **90-day test (~A$10k + time)** | Meet 15 sub-scale owners and 5 sponsors. Get data on 5 books and build a client-book underwriting model (churn, class mix, unspent funds, care-management cost). **Go:** a sponsor term sheet, and ≥3 targets at ≤0.3x revenue with a modelled path to ≥7% EBITDA. **Kill:** sponsors already have CEOs, or targets price as if the caps won't come. |

### Idea 3: AI revenue integrity for private and day hospitals (software)

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | An AI layer that reads the episode record and the health-fund contract, then (a) proposes and audits AR-DRG/ICD codes, (b) checks each claim against the contracted tariff and rules, and (c) chases rejections and under-payments. Sold to private and day hospitals and their PE owners, at first on contingency (15–25% of recovered revenue), then as SaaS. |
| **Why now (2026)** | The coding workforce is short and HIMAA is formalising AI adoption. Private hospital margins are under "the most stress in a generation" (Healthscope's collapse, insurer–hospital tariff fights). LLMs can now read discharge summaries and contracts well enough to do first-pass work. Capital is hungry for Australian health AI (Heidi). |
| **Unfair inputs** | Statistician plus CFO (contract and tariff analytics, recovery maths), builder, raise credibility. **Weak spots:** no hospital network, no health-information-management (HIM) or clinical-coding expertise, and long hospital IT procurement and privacy reviews. He'd need a coding-lead co-founder. |
| **Market & model** | A$22–24B private revenue **[unverified]**. If leakage recovered is 0.3–0.5% and the take is 20%, the Australian pool is ~A$13–24M a year **[estimate]**. Realistic Australian ARR A$5–12M by year 6, plus NZ/UK. Competitors: Code Focus, Solventum (3M) coding tools, EHR vendors, and possibly Heidi moving downstream. |
| **Capital & structure** | VC: A$2–4M seed, A$10–15M Series A. Founder ownership ~55% after seed, ~35–40% after A, ~25% at exit. Personal capital ~A$0.2M. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | Fail 50% → A$1.9M. Modest 25% → A$3.5M (acqui-sale). Good 18% → A$9M. Great 7% → A$35M (AU/UK leader sold to a revenue-cycle-management major). **EV ≈ A$5.9M; median ≈ A$2.7M** (below the stay-employed bar). |
| **Time to replace $500k** | 3–5 years (a VC salary of ~A$250k before that). A concierge audit for 1–2 day hospitals can start while employed if the employer approves and privacy terms are sorted. |
| **Job satisfaction** | High building energy and a good stats fit. Slow enterprise sales and clinical-governance gatekeepers are frustrating. |
| **Key risks** | **1.** EHR and coding vendors bundle it as a feature (the scribe-style commoditisation story). **2.** Fraud/upcoding perception: insurers resist, and reputational and legal exposure follows. **3.** Distribution: hospitals buy slowly. **What kills it:** recovered value per bed-day under ~0.2% of revenue on backtests. |
| **90-day test (~A$15k)** | With 2 day hospitals (via PE owners such as Day Hospital Partners), backtest 1,000 de-identified episodes against their contracts. **Go:** ≥0.4% of revenue identified and ≥0.25% confirmed recoverable, and a paid pilot signed. **Kill:** under 0.2%, or no access to data within 60 days. |

### Idea 4: Aged-care navigation and funding marketplace ("LocalAgentFinder for aged care")

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | A comparison and concierge platform for families choosing a home-care provider or residential home: star ratings, real prices, vacancies and Support at Home budget fit. It is paid by providers per successful placement (flat and identical across providers), plus aged-care financial advice (RAD vs DAP, means test) through an authorised-representative entity, plus credit broking for bridging or reverse-mortgage funding. |
| **Why now (2026)** | The rules are new and confusing (RAD retention, the higher everyday living fee, means-tested Support at Home contributions, lifetime caps). Clients can now switch home-care providers freely, and 950 providers are fighting for them. Lottie in the UK (US$31M raised, Accel; 6,000+ providers) shows the model works **[verified: PR Newswire 2023; 2026 profile, secondary]**. |
| **Unfair inputs** | **The closest pattern match in this report:** he ran LocalAgentFinder, a high-stakes, infrequent-decision comparison marketplace where the supplier pays. He has SEO/marketplace know-how and data-product skills. Weak: no aged-care brand, and the earlier rounds' warning about conflicted rankings applies. |
| **Market & model** | ~100k people waiting for home care at any time, plus switchers, plus ~60–70k residential admissions a year **[estimate]**. At 4k placements a year × A$2.5k plus advice fees of A$3–5k × 1.5k clients, revenue is ~A$15–17M **[estimate]**. Incumbents: Aged Care Decisions (placement), Aged Care Guide (directory), and the free government Care Finder and My Aged Care. |
| **Capital & structure** | Bootstrap plus angels, A$1–3M. Founder keeps ~70–80%. Personal capital ~A$0.3–0.5M. |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | Fail 45% → A$2.0M. Modest 30% → A$3.5M. Good 20% → A$7M. Great 5% → A$20M. **EV ≈ A$4.4M; median ≈ A$3.5M.** |
| **Time to replace $500k** | 4–6 years. A content/SEO site and a concierge service can start while employed. |
| **Job satisfaction** | Good: product, growth and data, with a familiar playbook. Lower stretch ("LAF again"); fewer big capital-allocation decisions. |
| **Key risks** | **1.** At ~90% residential occupancy providers won't pay for leads, so it becomes a home-care-only business with smaller fees. **2.** Regulators or the ACCC act on referral-fee conflicts, and the Care Finder program expands. **3.** LLM answer engines take comparison traffic. **What kills it:** under A$1.5k realised per placement, or a regulator banning provider-paid fees. |
| **90-day test (~A$25k)** | Launch an SEO site for 3 Melbourne LGAs and run a concierge line. Sign 20 home-care providers to pay-per-placement. **Go:** ≥40 placements in 90 days at ≥A$1.5k each and ≥15% advice conversion. **Kill:** under 15 placements. |

## 5. Top pick and ranking

**Top pick: Idea 1, the residential aged-care underwrite-and-own platform, started as deal-by-deal syndicates with a proven operator partner.**

It is the only idea in this lens that uses all three edges the earlier rounds found:
- pricing under uncertainty (occupancy, AN-ACC mix, RAD flows);
- capital allocation and raising;
- physical-asset arbitrage: buying scarce regulated beds at ~40–60% of replacement cost from distressed sellers while supply is frozen and demand grows ~4% a year.

It is the same move as the rent-roll hybrid (own the engine, capture value as equity and carry), but in an asset class with about 10x the deal flow and a structural supply shortage. It can be de-risked while he is employed: build the engine, line up an operator, soft-circle capital.

Its main weaknesses are reputational and policy risk, and its fail case is real (35%). That is why he should never run care himself, and why the 90-day kill criteria are strict.

The same engine also underwrites home-care books, so Idea 2 is the natural fallback, or a second sleeve. If the platform can't raise, the most bankable alternative is to lead a sponsor-backed home-care roll-up (fast income replacement, highest median).

**Ranked:**
1. Aged-care underwrite-and-own platform: EV ~A$5.9M, median ~A$3.5M
2. Home-care consolidation platform (exec + co-invest + engine): EV ~A$5.7M, median ~A$4.0M
3. AI revenue integrity for private and day hospitals: EV ~A$5.9M, median ~A$2.7M (a fat tail, but weak unfair inputs)
4. Aged-care navigation and funding marketplace: EV ~A$4.4M, median ~A$3.5M
