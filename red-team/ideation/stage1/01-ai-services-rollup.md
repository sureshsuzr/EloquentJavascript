# Stage 1, agent 01: AI-native services roll-up strategist

3 Nov 2026. Lens: buy labour-heavy professional-services businesses and transform them with AI ("services as software"), funded by PE, family offices or a VC-backed holdco, including search-fund and independent-sponsor variants. Sixteen web searches; techcrunch.com was blocked from the session, so its articles rest on search summaries. Labels: **[verified: source]** = confirmed in a search result; **[estimate]** = my arithmetic or judgement; **[unverified]** = a single secondary or marketing source.

**Benchmark used for every outcome table:** staying employed is worth about A$2.0–2.4M of after-tax wealth over 7 years (earlier rounds), which I extend to **≈A$3.0–3.5M over 10 years** [estimate]. All wealth figures below are the founder's **total 10-year after-tax wealth from the path**, including salary, so they compare directly with that bar.

---

## 1. What the 2025–26 comparables actually show

**Capital is flooding in; audited proof of margin uplift is not.**
- **Crete/Current (Thrive):** US$500M to buy US accounting firms; 20+ firms, US$300M+ revenue by mid-2025; sellers keep a minority stake [verified: Reuters; BusinessWire, Jun 2026].
- **General Catalyst "Creation" (US$1.5B):** targets lifting services EBITDA from 5–10% to 30–40%; Titan (MSPs, US$74M, claims 38% of tasks automatable) [verified: Sourcery, PE Wire].
- **Beacon:** US$225M Series C (Jun 2026), 30+ acquisitions, claims EBITDA +50% [verified: TNW; performance is company-reported].
- **Multiplier (Ribbit/Lightspeed)** has bought Oxygen Advisors (NZ/AU) and Greenhawk (NZ) in 2026 [verified: IT Brief]. **An AI accounting roll-up is already in ANZ.**
- **Sceptics:** Fortune and Air Street call these "tech-enabled PE" (one AI-services firm still ~10% EBITDA); Xeinadin's exit reportedly stalled [unverified].

**The Australian benchmark is Kelly Partners Group (ASX: KPG).** In FY26 it had A$159.2M revenue (+18.2%), but only **2.9% organic growth**. Underlying EBITDA margin was 25.3%, net debt was 1.52x EBITDA, and it ran 43 businesses with 105 partners [verified: KPG FY26 results via secondary coverage]. A$159M over 711 staff is about A$224k of revenue per head [estimate].

**What the Australian practice-sale market looks like:**
- **Prices:** Practices sell at about **1.13–1.26x fees** (113c in the dollar under A$1M of fees, 126c above A$1M, May 2026). Larger practices are moving to **4–6x maintainable earnings** [verified: DMY market data].
- **Competition for listings:** Each regional listing draws about **47 buyers**, up from 41 [verified: DMY]. **That is a warning: the buy side is crowded and prices are rising.**

**Insurance broking is already being consolidated by global capital:**
- Amwins/Dragoneer (later with KKR) bid A$7.7B for Steadfast, a 51.9% premium.
- Ardonagh paid A$2.3B for PSC.
- EQT bid A$3.4B for AUB.

[verified: Insurtech Insights, Insurance Business, Insurance Age] Macquarie says listed brokers get "limited accretion" at current deal multiples. **Broking roll-up arbitrage is gone.**

**Lesson carried in:** vendors commoditise AI cost-out within 12–24 months, so durable returns must come from multiple arbitrage on sellers who won't modernise, a regulatory/complexity catalyst, a pricing/underwriting edge, or a data/distribution position. Sell to whoever owns demand; avoid density-dependent operations.

## 3. The four kept ideas

### Idea 1: the "SMSF Compliance Factory", an AI-native SMSF administration and audit platform sold wholesale

**One-liner.** Buy 4–10 SMSF administration and audit practices from retiring owners at about 1–1.3x fees. Merge them into one AI-native back office: data feeds, reconciliation, valuations evidence, Div 296 calculations, and audit workpapers drafted by agents and signed off by registered auditors. Sell it **white-label to accountants, financial advisers and platforms**, who own the client and pay per fund. Trustees pay indirectly through their accountant or adviser.

**Why now (2026).**
- **Division 296** passed on 10 Mar 2026 and started on **1 Jul 2026**. It taxes realised earnings on balances above A$3M and A$10M. Funds must calculate and report Div 296 earnings from the 2026–27 return, may need actuarial certificates, and can reset cost bases at 30 Jun 2026 [verified: ATO newsroom, Bentleys, Moore, MLC]. **The first full compliance cycle lands in FY27–28**, which adds complexity per fund at the top end.
- **Auditor supply is falling:**
  - ASIC-registered SMSF auditors fell from 4,162 to 3,882 (Jun 2024 → Jun 2025), and to about 3,762 by Feb 2026 [verified: SMSF Adviser, SMS Magazine].
  - 11.5% of auditors audit 75% of funds, so the work is already concentrating [verified: ATO via SMS Magazine].
  - About 41% of auditors were over 60 in 2022 [verified: 2022 ATO stats].
  - Only about 60–80 new entrants arrive each year [verified, industry estimate].
- **The sector keeps growing:** about **664k funds** (Dec 2025) and **A$1.06T** of assets (Mar 2026) [verified: ATO]. 12,264 new funds were set up in the June 2026 quarter [verified: SMSF Association]. Average assets per fund are about A$1.88M [verified: Class benchmark via Motley Fool].
- **Agents can now do most of the work.** The job is a closed world of documents: bank and broker feeds, contract notes, valuations, minutes and a statutory checklist. That is the task shape where 2026 agents reach high automation.

**Unfair inputs.**
- **Statistician and CFO:** audit sampling, valuation evidence, and the Div 296 earnings arithmetic are his native ground. He can build the agent pipeline himself.
- **BCG:** operating-model redesign and post-merger integration.
- **Capital raising:** credible to family offices, and many of their principals *are* SMSF trustees, so investors also understand the product.
- **Weak spots:** no SMSF industry network; he isn't a registered auditor or tax agent (he must hire both); no relationships with administration software vendors (Class/HUB24, BGL).

**Market and model.**
- **Spend pool:** about 670k funds × (median administration/accounting about A$3.2k + audit median about A$550) ≈ **A$2.3–2.5B a year** of compliance spend [estimate from verified medians: ATO-derived figures via Industry Super and SMSF Adviser; fee data is 2022–24]. Much of it sits inside general accounting practices that would rather outsource.
- **Wholesale pricing:** administration about A$900–1,800 per fund; audit about A$350–550, with low-cost audit already near A$290 [verified, provider price].
- **Economics of one acquisition:** 1,500 funds × A$2k = A$3M revenue, bought at about 1.1x = A$3.3M. At a 25% margin that is A$750k EBITDA (≈4.4x). Lifted to 38–42% after the platform, it is about A$1.2M [estimate].
- **Platform at year 6:** 10–15k funds ≈ A$20–30M revenue, A$7–11M EBITDA. Strategic or PE exit at 8–11x ≈ A$60–120M EV [estimate]. Platform buyers include HUB24/Class, Netwealth, listed wealth groups and PE.
- **Structural constraint:** auditor-independence rules (tightened from 1 Jul 2021) stop the same firm preparing the accounts *and* auditing the same fund [verified in part: Accurium via search summary]. So audit must run as a separate entity serving *other* firms' funds. That is fine for a wholesale model, but it must be designed in from day one.
- **Comparables:** Heffron, SuperGuardian, Smarter SMSF and esuperfund (incumbent administrators); KPG's partner-owner model; Crete/Current and Multiplier as the AI analogues.

**Capital and structure.**
- **Raise:** a holdco with about **A$6–10M of equity** from 3–6 family offices, plus senior debt at 1.5–2x EBITDA, to buy about A$15–25M of practices over 4 years.
- **Founder:** puts in A$0.5–0.8M and takes sweat equity plus a performance promote. That gives **25–35% ownership** at exit [estimate].
- **Sellers:** keep 10–20% to protect client retention (the Crete model).
- **No personal guarantees:** debt at holdco level, and earn-outs instead of vendor finance where possible.

**Outcome distribution (10 years, founder after-tax wealth, including salary).**

| Case | P | Wealth (A$) | Scenario |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail | 25% | 1.0M | 2–3 deals don't integrate, fee deflation; sold at cost, return to employment by year 4 |
| Modest | 35% | 3.0M | 4–5k funds, about A$3M EBITDA, CEO salary, small exit |
| Good | 28% | 7.0M | 10k funds, sold at about A$60M EV in year 7 |
| Great | 12% | 18M | 15k+ funds, strategic exit about A$120M+, or kept as a dividend-paying holdco |

- **EV ≈ A$5.4M.**
- **Median ≈ A$3.0M**, roughly level with staying employed.

**Time to replace $500k.**
- **Before the first deal:** feasible while employed (market mapping, broker conversations, building the automation stack on public data), subject to his employer's outside-interest clause. The first close needs him full-time (months 6–9).
- **Salary:** a holdco salary of about A$300–350k from the first close. A$500k cash equivalent (salary plus distributions) in **about 2.5–3.5 years**.

**Job satisfaction.**
- **Builder work:** he designs the AI factory and its controls. He also does capital allocation, choosing deals and pricing them.
- **Less energising:** seller courtship, people integration, and regulated sign-off culture.
- **Closest to Fulfilio:** the consolidation arbitrage (fragmented capacity pulled onto one platform).

**Key risks.**
1. **Fee deflation as AI spreads.** Class/BGL ship AI to every practice, so in-house accountants keep the work and wholesale price falls 30–50%.
2. **Client attrition after acquisition.** SMSF clients follow the accountant, not the administrator.
3. **A policy or market shock to SMSF formation,** or seller multiples bid up by PE or Multiplier.

**What kills it:** a wholesale price below about A$800 per fund across all bands, *and* retention under 80%.

**90-day test (about A$15–25k).**
- **Supply:** talk to 25 SMSF administration and audit owners (via DMY, the NTAA practices-for-sale list, and SMSF Association events).
  - *Pass:* ≥5 open to selling at ≤1.3x fees within 24 months, and ≥2 data-room-ready.
- **Demand:** talk to 30 accountants and advisers.
  - *Pass:* ≥10 would outsource ≥50 funds each at ≥A$1,100 per fund for administration + Div 296.
- **Automation:** build an agent pipeline on 50 anonymised fund files from one friendly practice.
  - *Pass:* ≥60% reduction in preparer hours, with error rate no worse than human.
- **Kill if:** sellers demand ≥1.5x, or wholesale demand is under A$900 per fund.

### Idea 2 (bold): Broker-to-MGA, an AI-native underwriting agency built on a small niche broker

**One-liner.** Buy a small, specialised commercial broker (about A$1–3M commission) for its niche distribution and claims history. Use it to launch an **underwriting agency (MGA)** that prices one under-served niche with statistical and AI underwriting, on capacity from an insurer or Lloyd's syndicate. The MGA earns commission plus profit commission on premium.

Candidate niches:
- **landlord/residential investment property:** links to the rent-roll data and round 2's finding that the agency must *distribute* insurers' cover rather than guarantee rent;
- **small strata;**
- **trades;**
- **cyber cover for small professional firms.**

**Why now (2026).**
- **Broking is fully priced** (Steadfast A$7.7B, PSC A$2.3B, AUB A$3.4B bids). But **underwriting agencies** are where strategics now pay up.
- About 301 Australian agencies write about **A$7.5–10.8B GWP**, roughly a fifth of general-insurance premium. Ownership is concentrating fast: AUB bought Aon's One Underwriting on 23 Oct 2026, and Steadfast's 20 agencies write A$2.5B [verified: Insurance Business, insuranceNEWS; the market-size figures use different bases].
- **LLMs now read submissions, leases, building reports and claims notes.** That lets a small team underwrite thin, document-heavy niches that big insurers price crudely.

**Unfair inputs.**
- **Strongest fit with his record:** pricing and underwriting under uncertainty was the clearest edge across all earlier rounds.
- **CFO:** capital and reinsurance literacy.
- **Data:** real-estate industry network and data (LocalAgentFinder, rent-roll work) for a landlord or strata niche.
- **Weak spots, and serious ones:**
  - no underwriting track record (capacity providers want one);
  - no insurance network;
  - AFSL or authorised-representative status is required;
  - he must hire a credentialed lead underwriter.

**Market and model.**
- **Commission:** the MGA earns about 20–30% of GWP, plus profit commission when the loss ratio beats target [estimate, industry norms].
- **Scale:** at A$60M GWP that is about A$13–16M revenue, A$5–7M EBITDA. Agencies change hands at high multiples to AUB, Steadfast-type buyers or PE (about 12–15x [estimate; the 18.4x forward P/E for the insurance sector is the only verified anchor]) ≈ A$70–100M EV.
- **The broker acquisition (A$2–5M)** supplies distribution and loss data and covers the founder's salary from day one.

**Capital and structure.**
- **Raise:** A$4–8M equity from insurtech VC, family offices, or a capacity provider taking a strategic stake.
- **Founder:** puts in A$0.5–1M and holds **30–45%** at exit.
- **Capacity:** the carrier bears the underwriting risk. The MGA risks only its reputation and its expenses (and any capped profit-share clawback).

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Case | P | Wealth (A$) | Scenario |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail | 40% | 0.9M | No capacity, or the first-cohort loss ratio blows out; broker sold back |
| Modest | 25% | 3.0M | Broker plus a small MGA at A$15–20M GWP |
| Good | 23% | 9.0M | A$50–70M GWP, sold to a consolidator |
| Great | 12% | 30M | Multi-line agency, A$150M+ GWP, carrier-backed |

- **EV ≈ A$6.8M** (highest of the four).
- **Median ≈ A$3.0M.**

**Time to replace $500k.** About 3–4 years: the broker pays about A$250–350k at first, and the MGA needs 2–3 underwriting years to earn its profit commission. Only the research and capacity conversations can happen while employed.

**Job satisfaction.**
- **Very high on the core work:** pricing, model building and capital structuring, at his statistical frontier.
- **Lower on the rest:** broker relationship-selling.

**Key risks.**
1. **Can't secure capacity** without an underwriting track record.
2. **Adverse selection or cat exposure** in the first cohort wrecks the loss ratio. The data is thin for 3–5 years.
3. **Regulation** (AFSL, Design and Distribution Obligations, claims-handling rules) and consolidators copying the niche.

**What kills it:** no carrier term sheet within 12 months.

**90-day test (about A$20k).**
- **Niche:** pick one and build a pricing prototype on public and loss data.
- **Capacity:** pitch 8 capacity providers (Lloyd's coverholders, local insurers, AUB/Steadfast agency arms).
  - *Pass:* ≥2 term-sheet-level conversations conditional on hiring a named lead underwriter.
- **Broker supply:** identify ≥3 niche brokers selling at ≤1.5x commission.
- **Kill if:** zero capacity interest.

### Idea 3: Independent-sponsor AI transformation holdco, raising deal by deal across services verticals

**One-liner.** The founder becomes an **independent sponsor**: he sources Australian services businesses with A$1–5M EBITDA where the AI cost-out is deepest, and raises the equity per deal from family offices and ex-BCG/tech angels. Target verticals include:
- bookkeeping and payroll bureaux;
- rent rolls, using his **rent-roll pricing engine**;
- SMSF administration;
- insurance claims administration.

He installs a shared AI operations team across the portfolio. His pay is closing fees (2–3%), monitoring fees and a **15–25% promote** over a hurdle. This is the "holdco variant" that generalises the leading rent-roll hybrid.

**Why now (2026).**
- The US has validated "AI + acquisition" as an asset class (General Catalyst US$1.5B, Beacon US$225M, Crete/Current).
- Australian family offices and SMSF-rich investors want direct private deals.
- The baby-boomer ownership transfer across professional services leaves sellers with few buyers outside PE.

**Unfair inputs.**
- **Credibility:** BCG plus a prior exit, which gives sponsor credibility.
- **CFO:** deal modelling.
- **AI hands-on:** he can run the portfolio's AI team himself.
- **A pricing engine** for at least one vertical.
- **Weak spots:**
  - no sponsor track record, so the first deal is the hardest;
  - deal-by-deal capital is slow, and the time between deals is unpaid;
  - the employer conflict while sourcing.

**Market and model.**
- **Deal size:** A$5–25M EV per deal at 4–6x EBITDA.
- **Pace:** 1–2 deals a year.
- **Fees:** about A$0.2–0.5M a year across the portfolio, plus about A$0.15M in closing fees per deal.
- **Wealth:** comes from the promote. On A$40M of equity over 10 years at 2.5x gross, the promote is about A$6M before co-invest returns [estimate].

**Capital and structure.**
- **Founder co-invest:** A$0.3–1M across deals.
- **No dilution in the usual sense:** he owns about 20% of the upside on each deal through carry.
- **Optional:** convert to a committed fund after 3 deals.

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Case | P | Wealth (A$) | Scenario |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail | 30% | 1.6M | One deal at best; back to an executive role within 2–3 years with little capital lost |
| Modest | 35% | 3.5M | 3–4 deals, fees plus a small promote |
| Good | 25% | 7.0M | 6–8 deals, a fund I raised |
| Great | 10% | 16M | A recognised AU "AI holdco" with a A$100M+ fund |

- **EV ≈ A$5.1M.**
- **Median ≈ A$3.5M**, the highest floor and median of the four.

**Time to replace $500k.** About 2–3 years (fees plus executive-chair pay from portfolio companies). Sourcing can begin while employed, *with employer consent*.

**Job satisfaction.**
- **Strong on:** capital allocation, pricing deals, and building the AI stack across companies.
- **Weaker on:** less "building from nothing", and more fundraising than he might like.

**Key risks.**
1. **No first deal**: family offices won't back a first-time sponsor.
2. **A spread-thin AI team:** the cost-out doesn't transfer across verticals.
3. **Bad underwriting on one deal** sinks credibility.

**What kills it:** no signed LOI with committed equity in 9–12 months.

**90-day test (about A$10k).**
- **Capital:** put a one-page thesis to 15 family offices and HNW investors.
  - *Pass:* ≥A$5M of soft-circled equity from ≥4 investors.
- **Deals:** source 30 targets and get ≥3 NDAs and ≥1 conversation about a ≤5.5x EBITDA LOI.
- **Kill if:** under A$2M is soft-circled.

### Idea 4: AI-native SME accounting and bookkeeping holdco (Melbourne-first)

**One-liner.** Buy general SME accounting and bookkeeping practices (A$0.8–3M fees each) at 1.1–1.3x fees, standardise them on Xero and an agentic bookkeeping and tax workflow, raise margins from about 25% to 35–40%, and roll up toward a platform exit. SME clients pay monthly fees.

**Why now (2026).**
- Partners are retiring and the graduate pipeline is shrinking (the Big Four now hire "AI-empowered grads" from outside accounting [verified: Bloomberg Tax]).
- Agentic bookkeeping and tax drafting is now reliable for routine work.

**Unfair inputs.**
- **CFO credibility** with accountants and SME clients.
- **AI builder.**
- **Weak spot:** the founder isn't a CA. On edge, this is the least distinctive of the four.

**Market and model.**
- **Benchmark:** KPG shows the model works at A$159M revenue and 25% margin, but organic growth is only 2.9% [verified].
- **Buying:** multiples of 1.13–1.26x fees and 4–6x EBITDA [verified: DMY].
- **Exit:** platform multiples of 8–12x if AI margins are real [estimate].
- **Competition:**
  - in Australia: KPG, Findex (Mercury Capital owns 40%), and boutique PE platforms;
  - entering ANZ: Multiplier;
  - possibly coming: Cotrugli (an AI-native bookkeeping roll-up pitch) [unverified].
- **Buyer crowding:** 47 buyers per regional listing, rising [verified: DMY].

**Capital and structure.** As in idea 1: A$6–12M of family-office equity plus debt. The founder holds 20–30%.

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Case | P | Wealth (A$) | Scenario |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail | 30% | 1.0M | Integration and attrition |
| Modest | 35% | 2.8M | — |
| Good | 25% | 6.0M | — |
| Great | 10% | 15M | — |

- **EV ≈ A$4.3M.**
- **Median ≈ A$2.8M**, below staying employed.

**Time to replace $500k.** About 3 years.

**Job satisfaction.** Moderate. This is the most people-heavy and least differentiated of the four: lots of partner management.

**Key risks.**
1. **Buy-side competition** bids prices up.
2. **Vendor AI (Xero JAX, Intuit) commoditises the cost edge.** Clients then expect lower fees, and margin gains are passed through.
3. **Partner and client attrition** after the deal.

**What kills it:** buy multiples above 1.4x fees together with no organic growth.

**90-day test.** As in idea 1, but for general practices.
- *Pass:* ≥5 sellers at ≤1.25x fees; a pilot shows ≥40% fewer hours per return.

## 5. Top pick and ranking

**Top pick: idea 1, the SMSF Compliance Factory, financed through idea 3's deal-by-deal family-office structure.**

It is the only roll-up here that has all four ingredients earlier rounds said a winner needs:
1. **A dated regulatory catalyst:** Div 296's first compliance cycle runs FY27–28, and auditor numbers are falling.
2. **Buying from sellers with no successor** at about 1.1–1.3x fees, while the platform sells at a multiple.
3. **Selling wholesale to whoever owns demand:** accountants, advisers and platforms, not trustees.
4. **Fit with his quantitative and capital-allocation strengths** (valuation evidence, Div 296 maths, audit sampling).

It also rides the A$1T SMSF pool. The US AI roll-ups haven't targeted it yet (Multiplier is in general advisory). And it is testable in 90 days for under A$25k. Its honest weakness is that vendor AI will deflate prices, so the plan must assume a 30–40% price fall and still work on volume.

**Idea 2 (MGA) has the highest EV and uses his deepest edge.** But it carries a 40% fail probability gated by one binary (capacity), so run its 90-day capacity test **in parallel** as a cheap option.

**Ranked list:**
1. **SMSF Compliance Factory**: EV ≈ A$5.4M, median A$3.0M.
2. **Broker → AI-native MGA (bold)**: EV ≈ A$6.8M, median A$3.0M. Higher variance; capacity-gated.
3. **Independent-sponsor AI holdco**: EV ≈ A$5.1M, median A$3.5M. Best floor; the natural wrapper for #1 and for the rent-roll engine.
4. **SME accounting/bookkeeping holdco**: EV ≈ A$4.3M. Crowded (KPG, Findex, Multiplier) and least distinctive.
