## Idea 1: an independent loan-level valuation and risk layer for Australian real-estate private credit

**One-liner.** A data and valuation company that revalues every real-estate private-credit loan (construction, land, residual stock, bridging) each quarter: cost to complete, presale cover, end values and developer exposure across funds. Funds, responsible entities and trustees pay for independent valuations. Super funds, platforms, research houses and auditors pay to subscribe to the exposure and risk data. Think "Cotality plus Lincoln International for Australian private credit".

**Why now (2026).**
- **The market is large.** Australian private credit is about A$213B, up from A$35B a decade ago [verified: Clayton Utz via search], or A$234.5B in 2025 [verified: EY-Parthenon summary]. ASIC estimates about half is real-estate focused [verified: ASIC via Mallesons/Hall & Wilcox summaries].
- **It is in stress.**
  - Bathla collapsed in late Aug 2026 owing about A$3.4B, with roughly 40 private-credit funds exposed [verified: Urban Developer; Alternative Credit Investor].
  - About A$9B of funds are frozen [verified: MPA headline]. MA Financial suspended redemptions on A$2.3B of property funds; Metrics gated funds during a **valuation dispute with its auditor KPMG**; Longwave and CVS Lane froze [verified: IPE, DealStreetAsia, ACI].
  - Rates rose four times in 2026 [unverified: single commentary source].
- **Regulators now expect independent valuation.**
  - ASIC's REP 820 found funds lacking valuation policies and separation from the people overseeing third-party valuation. A snap review followed (52 funds, A$76B), and ASIC now requests weekly data [verified: summaries of REP 820, Bloomberg Mar 2026].
  - Lawyers describe ASIC's expectation as **independent quarterly valuations and loan-level reporting** [verified: Clayton Utz summary]. This is an expectation, not yet a binding instrument.
  - APRA is pressing super trustees on unlisted-asset valuation under SPS 530 (about A$500B unlisted in 2024) [verified: APRA information paper].
- **Nobody local has built it.** I found no Australian loan-level private-credit data or valuation platform. Houlihan Lokey launched a Private Credit DataBank in the US [verified: ABF Journal].
- **The gap no single party sees.** No one can see one developer's exposure across ~40 funds. That network view is new information only an independent layer can build.

**Unfair inputs.**
- **Strong:**
  - Statistics and CS: this is valuation under uncertainty, cost-to-complete distributions and correlated default.
  - CFO credibility with fund CFOs, REs and auditors.
  - BCG polish for selling into super-fund investment committees.
  - He has already designed a pricing engine for an illiquid asset (the round-3 rent-roll engine), and LocalAgentFinder gives real-estate data experience.
  - Melbourne is home to the largest industry funds (AustralianSuper, ART's Melbourne presence, Aware's Victorian base, UniSuper, Cbus, HESTA).
- **Weak:** no credit-workout or property-valuation credentials. He needs a co-founder who is an ex-credit-risk or ex-receivership person, plus certified valuers on panel for site values.
- **Conflict risk:** the employment contract (outside interests) must be cleared before any customer work.

**Market and model.**
- **Pricing [estimate].**
  - Per-loan valuations at A$750–3,000 per loan per quarter. A typical real-estate credit fund holds 50–400 loans; I estimate 8,000–15,000 live real-estate private-credit loans nationally [estimate; first thing to verify].
  - Allocator subscriptions (super funds, platforms, research houses like Lonsec and Zenith, auditors, trustees such as Perpetual, Equity Trustees and IQ-EQ) at A$50–200k/yr.
- **Addressable spend:** A$25–60M/yr at full share [estimate]; a realistic share is 15–25%.
- **Path:** about A$1M ARR in year 2, A$4–6M in year 4 and A$8–12M in year 6 if it becomes the market reference.
- **Comparables:**
  - Lincoln International and Kroll value thousands of private loans each quarter in the US.
  - Houlihan Lokey DataBank.
  - 73 Strings and Chronograph (private-markets valuation and monitoring software, VC-funded) [unverified amounts].
  - MSCI's private-credit transparency push [verified: InvestorDaily headline].
- **Strategic acquirers:** Cotality, MSCI, S&P, Houlihan Lokey, Lincoln, Equifax and the Big 4.
- **Exit:** 5–8x ARR, so A$40–90M in the good case.

**Capital and structure.** Bootstrap the first 6–9 months on A$150–250k of personal money (concierge valuations of 2–3 loan books). Then a A$2–3M seed from a fintech VC plus a strategic (a trustee or research house) at A$10–14M pre-money, and a A$8–12M Series A in year 2–3. **Founder ownership at exit: ~35–45%** after a co-founder grant and ESOP.

**Outcome distribution (10 years, founder wealth after tax including salary drawn).** Bar for comparison: staying employed ≈ A$2.0–2.4M over 7 years, so about **A$3.0–3.5M over 10 years [estimate]**.

| Outcome | P | Founder wealth (A$) |
|---|---|---|
| Fail (data access refused or Big 4 capture it; back to an exec role by year 3) | 35% | 1.2M |
| Modest (A$3–5M ARR niche, acqui-sale ~A$15–25M) | 30% | 3.0M |
| Good (market reference, ~A$50M exit) | 25% | 8.0M |
| Great (expands to NZ/Asia private credit or all private-markets valuation, A$150M+) | 10% | 25M |

**EV ≈ A$5.8M; median ≈ A$3.0M.**

**Time to replace $500k.** Seed-funded salary of about A$250–300k from roughly month 9. A $500k-equivalent (salary plus vested secondary/dividend capacity) by year 4–5. **Discovery can start while employed (with consent). The build cannot.**

**Job satisfaction.** Very high fit: modelling, data engineering with heavy AI use (extracting loan tapes, QS reports and DA documents), selling to CFOs and CIOs, and building a company from nothing. Energy risk: long enterprise sales cycles and compliance paperwork.

**Key risks.**
1. **Data access.** Managers under stress may resist an independent mark ("we'd rather not know"). Mitigant: sell through REs, trustees, auditors and allocators, who *can* compel loan tapes.
2. **Incumbents.** The Big 4, Kroll or Lincoln bolt on an Australian practice. Mitigant: the cross-fund network and outcome data (loans followed to resolution) is a moat that consultants don't productise.
3. **Liability.** A wrong mark on a frozen fund invites litigation. This needs professional-indemnity cover, carefully scoped engagements and panel valuers for site values.
- **What kills it:** ASIC settles on manager-run "directors' valuations" plus audit review, and no one is compelled to buy independence.

**90-day test (~A$30k).**
1. Clear the employer conflict.
2. 25 interviews: 8 fund CFOs or credit heads, 5 REs/trustees, 5 super-fund private-debt teams, 4 research houses or platforms, 3 audit partners.
3. Obtain 2 anonymised loan tapes (≥300 loans) and produce a sample revaluation with a cross-fund exposure map.
- **Proceed if** ≥2 paid pilots at ≥A$30k each, or LOIs worth ≥A$250k ARR, *and* ≥1 RE, trustee or allocator willing to require the product of its managers.
- **Kill if** fewer than 2 parties will share loan data or nobody will pay before ASIC mandates it.

## Idea 2 (bold): "finish-the-job" capital for stalled housing, a special-situations vehicle

**One-liner.** A GP that raises A$150–300M from family offices, offshore special-situations investors and super funds' opportunistic sleeves. It buys distressed residential construction loans and half-built projects at a discount from frozen funds and receivers, completes them through fixed-price builder partners, and exits to owner-occupiers, BTR operators, community-housing providers or super funds. The fund pays management fees and carry.

**Why now (2026).**
- Bathla alone has about 219 sites and roughly 2,000 homes mid-construction; Teneo has halted every site and receivers are selling [verified: Urban Developer, Business News Australia]. Some 40 exposed funds, A$9B frozen and redemption pressure mean **forced sellers of loans and sites**.
- Meanwhile Australia needs housing (the 1.2M-home Accord target) and super funds want BTR (ART's first BTR deal in Jan 2026; a A$30B+ BTR pipeline [verified: realcommercial, BDO]).
- **The physical arbitrage:** a half-built townhouse project is worth far less to a gated fund that can't fund completion than to a completer with a builder and an exit buyer. This has the same shape as Fulfilio's spare-capacity arbitrage.
- **The window:** about 2026–2029, before the cycle turns.

**Unfair inputs.**
- **Strong:** underwriting under uncertainty; capital-raising credibility (BCG, prior exit); capital-allocator mindset; idea 1's data (if built) gives proprietary deal flow and pricing.
- **Weak (serious):**
  - No development, construction or workout track record. LPs will underwrite the team's past deals, so he needs a co-GP with 15+ years of residential delivery and workouts.
  - No AFSL; he would use a third-party RE or trustee platform.
  - The GP commitment (1–2% of the fund, A$1.5–6M) strains a A$0.75–1.2M risk budget. He would need to negotiate a seed GP partner.

**Market and model.**
- **Fees:** a A$200M fund at a 1.75% management fee ≈ A$3.5M/yr against ~A$2–2.5M of team costs.
- **Carry:** 20% over an 8% hurdle on a 1.5–1.7x gross multiple ≈ A$12–20M carry pool; founder share about 35–45% of GP economics [estimate].
- **Competition:** PAG, Leda (which seized sites directly), KKR, Bain Credit, Qualitas, Payton, Merricks and receivers' own buyers. Large players prefer cheques over A$50M. **The gap is A$5–40M townhouse and small-apartment projects**, especially in Melbourne's middle ring [estimate].

**Capital and structure.** PE-style fund. Pre-fund: A$0.5M personal plus warehouse deals with 2–3 family offices (deal-by-deal). First close of A$75–100M by month 12–18. Founder owns about 40% of the GP and 0% of the LP beyond his commitment.

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Outcome | P | Founder wealth (A$) |
|---|---|---|
| Fail (can't raise beyond deal-by-deal, or first deals lose money) | 45% | 0.9M |
| Modest (one ~A$100M fund, ~1.3x) | 20% | 3.0M |
| Good (two funds, A$200–400M) | 25% | 9.0M |
| Great (a franchise residential credit/opportunity manager, A$1B+ AUM) | 10% | 30M |

**EV ≈ A$6.3M; median ≈ A$3.0M** (fat tails both ways).

**Time to replace $500k.** About 12–18 months, from first close; deal-by-deal fees give some income earlier. **Requires quitting by about month 6–9.**

**Job satisfaction.** High intensity: deal-making, negotiating with receivers, capital raising and physical delivery. Uses capital allocation and underwriting; it is less of a builder's job and more of an investor-operator's. The variance suits his ambition.

**Key risks.**
1. **Track record.** LPs won't back a first-time GP without a credible delivery partner.
2. **Builder risk.** Builder insolvency and cost blowouts during completion (Victoria's builder collapses).
3. **Timing.** The window closes if rates fall and banks return, or the cycle deepens and end values fall a further 15%+.
- **What kills it:** failing to get a co-GP with a delivery record within 90 days.

**90-day test (~A$40k, mostly time; needs employer clearance or exit).**
1. Sign a co-GP or delivery partner.
2. Source 15 opportunities via KordaMentha, Teneo, McGrathNicol and the REs of gated funds; underwrite 3 fully.
3. Soft-circle LP capital.
- **Proceed if** ≥A$50M soft-circled and ≥1 deal under exclusivity at ≥20% projected gross IRR.
- **Kill if** <A$25M soft-circled or no partner.

## Idea 3: a data-led Support at Home consolidator (PE-backed)

**One-liner.** A PE-backed platform that buys small and mid-sized home-care providers squeezed by Support at Home. It prices each target on client-level retention and margin models, then migrates them onto one AI-enabled care-management, rostering and claiming backbone. Revenue comes from government-funded Support at Home services plus participant contributions.

**Why now (2026).**
- Support at Home replaced Home Care Packages on 1 Nov 2025. About 364,723 people had access at 31 Mar 2026 [verified: GEN aged care data Q3 2025–26].
- The removal of package management fees and incoming price caps (start date apparently deferred [verified: DoH fact sheet May 2026]) have pushed operating margins to "unsustainable" levels. Exits have started (Annecto, two Canberra providers) [verified: Weekly Source / Inside Ageing summaries].
- Consolidation is under way: Australian Unity–myHomecare (A$285M), HammondCare–Anglicare Sydney (about 3,200 clients), Regis–Royal Freemasons, and Quadrant raising more than A$1.2B with home care in view [verified: search summaries; dates inconsistent across sources].
- Demographics: the 80+ cohort roughly doubles by about 2040 [estimate].

**Unfair inputs.**
- **Strong:** acquisition pricing under uncertainty (the round-3 muscle transfers almost directly: a client book is a rent roll with care workers); CFO; PE credibility.
- **Weak:** no aged-care or clinical background; a workforce-heavy business; the new Aged Care Act's strengthened provider duties. He needs a clinical COO.
- **Pattern warning:** the earlier rounds' finding that ops-heavy density businesses did badly applies here.

**Market and model.**
- Home-care funding is about A$12–15B/yr and rising [estimate]. Mid-size providers trade at about 5–8x EBITDA, distressed ones lower [estimate; no disclosed multiples found].
- Platform thesis: buy at 3–5x, integrate and earn 8–10% EBITDA margins, then exit at 9–12x to a strategic or larger PE firm, as Quadrant is attempting with myHomecare.

**Capital and structure.** PE sponsor provides A$50–150M of equity. Founder as CEO takes 5–10% sweat plus a co-invest of A$0.5M and a market salary (A$450–600k).

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Outcome | P | Founder wealth (A$) |
|---|---|---|
| Fail (integration or regulatory problems; exits after 3–4 years on salary) | 30% | 2.5M |
| Modest | 35% | 3.5M |
| Good (A$300M+ exit) | 25% | 7.0M |
| Great | 10% | 15M |

**EV ≈ A$5.2M; median ≈ A$3.5M.**

**Time to replace $500k.** About 6–12 months, from the sponsor-backed CEO salary. **Requires quitting.**

**Job satisfaction.** Medium to low: rostering, workforce shortages, quality audits, political pricing and incident management. Deal pricing is fun; running 2,000 care workers is not his stated energy source.

**Key risks.**
1. Government price caps compress margins further.
2. Care-worker shortages and wage cases.
3. A serious-incident or reputational event under the new Act.
- **What kills it:** no PE sponsor will back a non-aged-care CEO.

**90-day test.** Pitch 4 mid-market PE firms. Build a target list of 20 providers (A$5–40M revenue) and get data rooms from 3.
- **Proceed if** a sponsor signs an exclusivity or term sheet and ≥2 targets will sell at ≤5x.
- **Kill otherwise.**

## Idea 4: rental electrification and compliance-as-a-service (Victoria 2027)

**One-liner.** A zero-upfront "compliance plan" for Victorian landlords, sold through property managers. It covers an audit, then installation of a heat-pump hot-water system, efficient split-system heating and cooling, ceiling insulation and draughtproofing. Upgrades are financed after Victorian Energy Upgrades discounts and repaid monthly from rent over 3–7 years. Landlords pay. PM agencies earn a disclosed coordination fee. A property-level compliance dataset is sold to rent-roll buyers and lenders.

**Why now (2026).**
- Victoria's minimum energy-efficiency standards for every rental phase in from **1 Mar 2027** across six areas: heating, cooling, hot water, shower heads, insulation and draughtproofing. Every upgrade is VEU-discount eligible [verified: Energy Vic, CAV].
- Compliance is weak: only 54% of 11,000+ surveyed rentals met even the 2021 standards [verified: La Trobe survey via search].
- Cheaper Home Batteries has grown to about A$7.2B, though discounts were cut from May 2026 [verified: ESD News, Canstar], so batteries are an add-on, not the core.

**Unfair inputs.**
- **Strong:** real-estate agency network (LocalAgentFinder) and the round 1–3 work on PM economics and rent-roll pricing; physical-logistics operating experience (Fulfilio: coordinating dispersed physical jobs); finance structuring.
- **Weak:** trades and installer quality control. The VEU installer market has a history of poor-quality and phoenix operators.

**Market and model.**
- About 650–700k Victorian rental homes [estimate]. If ~40% need one or more upgrades at A$3–8k net of VEU, that is about A$1–2B of one-off work over 2027–32 [estimate], with a long tail from failure-triggered replacements.
- Gross margin about A$800–1,500 per job plus a finance origination fee or spread.
- A $500k founder draw needs about 300–500 jobs a month [estimate].
- **Competitors:** installers, Brighte and Plenti green loans, and PM software adding compliance checklists.

**Capital and structure.** Bootstrap A$300–500k. The receivables are financed off balance sheet by a green-asset lender (CEFC-backed warehouse lines exist for green loans [unverified for this use]). Founder ownership 70–90%.

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Outcome | P | Founder wealth (A$) |
|---|---|---|
| Fail | 40% | 1.5M |
| Modest | 35% | 3.0M |
| Good | 20% | 6.0M |
| Great (national expansion as other states copy the standards) | 5% | 12M |

**EV ≈ A$3.5M; median ≈ A$3.0M.**

**Time to replace $500k.** About 3–4 years. A pilot can run while employed through a manager or partner.

**Job satisfaction.** Medium: an operational and channel business. Clever finance, but a lot of installer management. It is a one-off regulatory wave that decays after about 2030.

**Key risks.**
1. Landlords comply minimally or late; enforcement is complaint-driven.
2. Installer quality and liability.
3. PM agencies won't promote it (s 48A-style rebate issues, round 1).
- **What kills it:** under 10% conversion from audit to install.

**90-day test (~A$25k).** Partner with 3 PM agencies (about 3,000 managed properties). Audit 200 rentals and get finance-partner term sheets.
- **Proceed if** ≥15% of audited landlords sign at ≥A$1,000 gross margin.
- **Kill if** <7%.

## Ranking and top pick

| Rank | Idea | EV (10-yr, A$) | Median | Start while employed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Private-credit loan valuation and risk layer** | **~5.8M** | 3.0M | Discovery yes (with consent) |
| 2 | Finish-the-job special-situations vehicle (bold) | ~6.3M | 3.0M | No |
| 3 | Support at Home data-led consolidator | ~5.2M | 3.5M | No |
| 4 | Rental electrification / compliance-as-a-service | ~3.5M | 3.0M | Pilot only |

**Top pick: idea 1, the independent valuation and risk layer for Australian real-estate private credit, held as an option to become idea 2.** Idea 2 has a slightly higher EV, but it needs a co-GP with a delivery record and a GP commitment beyond his risk budget, and it can't start while he is employed. Idea 1 is the same edge (pricing illiquid assets under uncertainty) sold to whoever owns the capital, which is exactly the pattern earlier rounds found works. It is driven by a regulatory and market shock happening *right now* (A$9B frozen, ASIC enforcement, a fund–auditor valuation dispute), in a A$213B+ market with no local data layer. It also produces the proprietary deal flow and pricing that would make idea 2 (or a co-investment in it) credible in 2028, which repeats round 3's lesson: capture the edge as equity and carry, not only as fees.

Its main uncertainties are whether managers will share loan data and whether ASIC turns its "independent quarterly valuation" expectation into a requirement. The 90-day test checks both cheaply.
