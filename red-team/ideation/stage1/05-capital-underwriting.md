# Stage 1: Capital, Underwriting & Fintech Strategist (agent 05)

3 Nov 2026. Lens: pricing risk under uncertainty plus CFO credibility. Web access was partial: several primary sites (Salter Brothers, ASIC, the Clean Energy Regulator) weren't reachable from the session, and much of the evidence below is secondary reporting. Labels: **[verified: source]** means at least one credible named source; **[estimate]** is mine; **[unverified]** means one weak or promotional source.

## Benchmark and wealth convention

- **Founder wealth at year 10** means cumulative after-tax income, plus the after-tax value of equity or carry, minus the capital he puts in. Every bucket below uses this convention.
- **Staying employed:** the brief puts this at ~A$2.0–2.4M over 7 years. Extended to 10 years, I use **~A$3.3M** as the bar **[estimate]**.
- **Fail outcomes** assume he spends 2–3 years, loses part of the A$0.75–1.2M he puts at risk, then goes back to a ~A$400–500k role.

## What I generated, and what I cut

I generated 14 ideas before cutting:

1. SaaS revenue-based finance
2. R&D tax incentive advance lending
3. Receivables or invoice finance
4. EV fleet and equipment leasing
5. Rent-roll lending
6. A lender for recurring-commission "books" of business
7. An SME succession acquisition-credit fund
8. A permanent-capital succession holdco
9. A battery and solar warranty MGA
10. An EV residual-value or battery-health cover MGA
11. A strata defect-remediation lender
12. A private-credit valuation and monitoring utility
13. A super-fund co-investment vehicle for SME buyouts
14. AI-underwritten tradie and SME credit

**Cut, and why:**

- **SaaS revenue-based finance and R&D tax lending: crowded, with capital already arrived.**
  - Players already in the market:
    - Fundbox entered Australia in Jan 2026 with an MA Financial-backed warehouse **[verified: medianet/Fundbox release]**.
    - Stripe Capital is launching in Australia **[verified: FF News]**.
    - Fundsquire reports growth in grant advances and RBF (Apr 2026) **[verified: FF News]**.
    - Outfund, Lighter Capital and Choco Up are also active **[verified: various]**.
  - His SaaS-CFO network gives him deal flow, but no pricing edge. The spread gets competed to the cost of funds.
- **AI-underwritten tradie and SME credit, and receivables finance:** Prospa, Judo, ScotPac/Business Fuel and Moula already do this, cheaply. The capital provider wins this game, not the model.
- **EV fleet leasing:** capital-heavy and commoditised. The only interesting part is residual-value risk, which I've folded into idea C.
- **Strata defect lending:** a real niche (Lannock already operates in it), but it's small and slow.
- **Permanent-capital succession holdco:** that's an equity and operator idea, and other lenses will likely cover it. Here it appears only as an equity kicker inside idea A.

## The four ideas kept

### A. "Succession Credit": an acquisition-finance platform for the boomer business transfer (an Australian Live Oak, without the SBA)

**One-liner.** A non-bank credit platform that funds owner-operator buyers, management buyouts and search funders acquiring profitable Australian businesses worth A$1–20M.

- **Product:**
  - 4–7-year amortising senior or stretch-senior loans of A$0.5–8M.
  - Standardised vendor-note documents.
  - A small equity kicker (warrants, or 2–5% of the business).
- **Underwriting:**
  - A model built on accounting-ledger and bank-feed data (Xero, MYOB).
  - A post-acquisition outcome dataset that grows with every deal.
- **Funding:** a wholesale credit fund.
- **Who pays:** borrowers pay interest of about 10.5–14% plus a 1–2% establishment fee. Limited partners pay management fees and carry.

**Why now (2026).**

- **Sellers are retiring.**
  - 48% of baby-boomer owners plan to exit within 1–5 years, and only 24% have an exit plan **[verified: MYOB Business Monitor via ITBrief / CA ANZ, Aug 2025]**.
  - More than 500,000 businesses are owned by people aged 60+ **[verified: SmartCompany]**.
- **Buyers can't easily borrow against goodwill.**
  - Banks are reluctant to lend unsecured against goodwill, and searchers report that banks and second-tier lenders show no appetite for true SME acquisition finance **[unverified: searchfunder.com forum posts; businessesforsale]**.
  - More than 40% of SME sales now involve vendor finance **[unverified: marketing source]**. That is the market quietly signalling unmet credit demand.
  - Accountants and business groups are lobbying for US-style SBA loan guarantees **[verified: SmartCompany, Dynamic Business]**. If a guarantee ever arrives, an incumbent specialist takes most of it.
- **Capital is available.**
  - Australian private credit is ~A$225B, growing ~9% a year **[verified: ASIC private debt review via Super Review; Tanarra]**.
  - Super funds' private-credit holdings are forecast to rise from ~A$30B to ~A$35.5B in FY26 **[verified: Tanarra/GSFM]**.
  - ASIC's main concern is real-estate concentration, about half the market **[verified: KWM on ASIC review]**. Non-property, cash-flow credit with clean governance is what allocators say they want **[estimate]**.
- **AI makes underwriting cheap.** Ledger-level underwriting of a A$3M business used to need a credit analyst for a week. With LLM agents it is a few hours plus human sign-off. That makes A$1–5M tickets economic for a non-bank **[estimate]**.
- **The US proof.**
  - Live Oak Bank is the largest SBA 7(a) lender: ~US$2.7–2.9B in FY25 approvals, with a dedicated search-fund team **[verified: SBA ranking via Live Oak IR]**.
  - Its 10-year average net charge-offs are ~0.27% against the SBA's ~0.65% **[unverified: Cobalt Intelligence]**.
  - The US market exists because of the SBA guarantee. Australia has no guarantee, so pricing must carry the loss, which means 11–14% coupons and lower leverage (≤3x EBITDA senior).

**Unfair inputs.**

- **Strong:**
  - Statistician plus CFO: he can build and defend a loss model to investment committees.
  - Ex-BCG and two exits: credible to family offices and investors from his prior exits as a first-time manager.
  - Has been on both sides of a business sale (Fulfilio to Australia Post).
  - A heavy AI user, so he can build the underwriting stack himself.
- **Medium:**
  - Networks with accountants and brokers through LocalAgentFinder and the real-estate industry.
  - Melbourne SME networks.
- **Weak spots:**
  - **No credit track record.** First-time credit managers struggle to raise. He needs a partner with 10+ years in workout and recovery, or a seasoned credit lead, and a borrowed track record via a platform: an established trustee or responsible entity, or a credit-manager seeder.
  - He has never run a loan book through a downturn.

**Market and model.**

- **Market size:**
  - Businesses changing hands in the A$1–20M EV band: perhaps 3,000–6,000 transactions a year nationally **[estimate; no official count]**.
  - Even 2% at A$2.5M average loans is ~A$150–300M a year of origination capacity **[estimate]**.
  - The average asking price on Bsale is A$673k (Mar 2026) **[verified: Bsale Q1 2026]**. That figure is dominated by micro deals that sit below this target band.
- **Fund economics:**

  | | |
  |---|---|
  | Fund size | A$100M year 2 → A$300M year 5 → A$600M year 8 **[estimate]** |
  | Management fee | 1.25–1.75% |
  | Carry | 15–20% over an 8% hurdle |
  | Target net LP return | 9–11% |
  | Fee revenue at A$300M | ~A$4.5M a year |
  | Platform cost | A$2–2.5M a year (team of 6–8, trustee, audit, compliance) |
  | Carry over 10 years | ~A$5–15M across vintages if net returns hold **[estimate]** |

- **Alternative structure:** become a balance-sheet lender with a bank warehouse. That captures the full spread but needs ~A$15–25M of equity.
- **Comparables:**
  - Live Oak (US).
  - Salter Brothers bought Causeway (A$600M of funds under management) to build out corporate credit to companies with A$5–300M revenue **[verified: Salter Brothers / Broker News]**. That shows big managers are moving toward this space, which is both an exit path and a competitive threat.
  - MA Financial, Metrics and Qualitas: listed AU credit managers that trade on fee-related earnings multiples **[unverified multiples; ~10–15x FRE typical]**.

**Capital and structure.**

- **Founder money:** A$0.4–0.6M in operating costs for the first 18 months, plus a 1–2% GP commitment (A$1–2M; part can be deferred or financed).
- **Seeding:** a family office or seeder anchors A$25–50M and takes 15–25% of GP economics.
- **Regulation:**
  - Business-purpose lending generally doesn't need an Australian Credit Licence (the NCCP's predominant-purpose test), but **unfair-contract-terms penalties** (since Nov 2023) apply to small-business loan contracts.
  - The fund needs an AFSL. Start as a corporate authorised representative under a professional trustee (Equity Trustees, One Investment Group, Perpetual), wholesale only.
  - APRA isn't involved unless he takes deposits.
- **Founder ownership:** after the seeder and a credit partner (~15–20%), about **55–65% of the GP**.

**Outcome distribution (10 years, founder wealth).**

| Outcome | P | Founder wealth | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail | 30% | A$1.6M | Can't raise past ~A$30M or early losses; wind down in year 3 and return to a CFO role |
| Modest | 35% | A$3.2M | A$100–150M boutique, A$400–550k salary, small carry |
| Good | 25% | A$7.0M | A$300–500M, carry from two vintages |
| Great | 10% | A$20M | A$1B+ platform; GP stake sold to a listed manager or bank, or a government guarantee arrives |

**EV ≈ A$5.3M; median ≈ A$3.2M** (in the modest bucket). The upside skew is real. The median only matches staying.

**Time to replace $500k.**

- About 3–4 years: fee income at ~A$100–150M of funds under management, around year 3.
- **Pre-work can start while employed:**
  - Outcome-data collection.
  - Underwriting model on historical deals.
  - LP soundings.
  - Recruiting a credit partner.
- These require **disclosure and consent under his employment contract** (outside-interests clause), and no lending until he leaves.
- Realistic quit point: when A$25M+ of anchor capital is soft-circled.

**Job satisfaction.** High.

- Building the underwriting model and the outcome dataset is exactly his statistics and CS work.
- IC meetings and LP raising use his CFO and BCG skills.
- Every deal is a different business, so curiosity stays fed.
- The downside is workouts: by year 4, enforcing against a failing family business takes ~20% of the time and is draining.

**Key risks.**

1. **Credit losses in a downturn.** Goodwill has little recovery value: maybe 20–40% recovery versus 60%+ for property **[estimate]**. A 2027–28 recession on vintage 1 could kill the franchise.
2. **First-time manager fundraising.** Super funds won't invest before a ~5-year record, so it's family offices and HNW for years. ASIC's scrutiny of private credit adds compliance cost.
3. **Adverse selection.** The buyers who come to a 12% lender are the ones banks declined. Mitigants: be the fast, specialist "yes" with more of the vendor note at risk, and win through brokers before the bank says no.

**What kills it:** fewer than ~A$30M raised by month 18, or vintage-1 losses above 4% a year.

**90-day test (~A$20–30k, while employed and with consent).**

- **Supply of deals:**
  - Interview 25 business brokers, M&A accountants and searchers (Link, Bsale, ABB, Vic SME advisory firms).
  - **Pass:** ≥30 live deals in the A$1–15M EV band with a documented financing gap in a quarter, of which ≥10 buyers say they would pay ≥11%.
- **Loss model:**
  - Score 20 historical deals (closed 2019–23) from advisers' files.
  - **Pass:** the model separates performers from failures. Even rough AUC above 0.7 counts.
- **Capital:**
  - Soundings with 15 family offices or HNW investors, plus 2 seeders.
  - **Pass:** ≥A$25M soft-circled, or one seeder term sheet.
- **Kill:** fewer than 15 qualified deals, or under A$10M of soft interest.

### B. "BookCo": a stretch-senior and acquisition-capital provider for recurring-commission books (rent rolls first)

**One-liner.** Capital for buyers of books of recurring commission: rent rolls, insurance-broker books, accounting fee bases, financial-adviser books and strata management.

- **Where it sits:** above bank senior debt (banks lend ~50–70% LVR) as a stretch or second-lien tranche to ~80–85%.
- **Products:** earn-out and holdback financing, and roll-up equity co-investment.
- **Pricing edge:** the round-3 retention-pricing engine.
- **Who pays:** acquirers, through 12–16% coupons and fees, plus equity upside on roll-ups.

**Why now.**

- These books change hands steadily as the boomer cohort leaves.
- UK and Australian roll-ups are active (round-3 synthesis).
- **NAB's rent-roll policy:** lends to 70% LVR (some pages say 80%), for terms up to 10 years, and only to agencies with 100+ residential properties under management **[verified: NAB rent-roll lending pages]**.
- The gap between the bank tranche and the price, and every buyer below NAB's size threshold, is now filled with vendor finance or not at all.
- The round-3 synthesis found the engine is worth **$50–100k per deal to the buyer**. The way to monetise that is as a capital provider, not a report vendor.

**Unfair inputs.**

- The engine and outcome dataset from round 3: unique if built.
- Real-estate agency networks through LocalAgentFinder.
- Statistician plus CFO credibility for the loss model.
- **Weak:**
  - Macquarie Business Banking dominates practice finance for accountants, advisers and brokers, and is very good at it.
  - Deal volume is thin: ~250–900 rent-roll deals a year (round 3). Broker books are similar.

**Market and model.**

- **Size:** across the four book types, perhaps A$1.5–3B of transaction value a year in Australia. The stretch tranche is 15–25% of that, so ~A$250–600M a year **[estimate]**.
- **Phases:**
  - **Years 1–2:** a balance sheet of A$5–15M from the founder, friends and a family office. Loans of A$0.3–2M.
  - **Year 3+:** A$100–200M fund.
- **Comparables:**
  - Oak Street Funding: US insurance-agency lender, bought by First Financial Bancorp **[unverified: price not confirmed]**.
  - Macquarie practice finance.
  - Live Oak's verticals: it began in veterinary and pharmacy practice lending.
- **Revenue:**
  - At A$80M deployed, ~A$5–6M net interest margin after funding.
  - Or, as a fund: ~A$1.5M in fees plus carry.

**Capital and structure.**

- **Phase 1:** founder A$0.5–0.8M first-loss equity, plus A$5–10M of co-lender money from family offices on a note programme.
- **Licensing:**
  - Wholesale notes need an AFSL or authorised-representative structure.
  - Business lending needs no ACL.
- **Founder ownership:** ~70–80%.
- **Later:** this becomes the first vertical "sleeve" of idea A, or is sold to a bank or credit manager.

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Outcome | P | Founder wealth | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail | 25% | A$2.3M | Cheap test while employed; abandon after year 2 with modest losses |
| Modest | 40% | A$3.6M | A$40–80M book, A$400–500k pay |
| Good | 25% | A$6.0M | A$150–250M across verticals; sale or merge into a larger manager |
| Great | 10% | A$12M | Becomes the category specialist; equity upside from roll-ups |

**EV ≈ A$4.7M; median ≈ A$3.6M.** It has the highest floor of the four. The ceiling is capped because the niche is thin and Macquarie is entrenched.

**Time to replace $500k.** About 3–5 years. It can start while employed, but only as data and engine work and lender soundings; lending is the jump point.

**Job satisfaction.** Good: underwriting with real data and close to deals. But the niche is narrower, so there's less novelty after year 3.

**Key risks.**

1. **Volume ceiling.** There may be only ~50–150 financeable deals a year nationally in the stretch-tranche band.
2. **Macquarie/NAB reaction.** Either bank can raise LVRs a notch and erase the gap.
3. **Retention shocks.** A buyer's poor integration (~66% versus ~80% retention, round 1) is larger than the pricing error the engine catches, and second-lien recovery is poor.

**What kills it:** banks lift LVR to 80% broadly, or fewer than 30 deals a year come to him.

**90-day test (~A$15k).**

- **Count the deals:**
  - Survey 10 rent-roll and insurance-book brokers.
  - **Pass:** ≥40 deals a year where the buyer had a gap above bank debt they'd pay ≥12% to fill.
- **Demand:** get 3 acquirers to sign non-binding indications for a stretch tranche.
- **Backtest:** run the engine on ≥10 historical rolls with outcomes.
- **Kill:** fewer than 20 qualified deals, or banks already stretching to 80%+.

### C. "VoltCover": an energy-transition warranty and residual-value MGA (the bold idea)

**One-liner.** An underwriting agency (MGA) that prices, with real data, risks no one in Australia prices well yet:

1. **Insurance-backed workmanship and installer-insolvency guarantees** for home batteries and solar.
   - **Who pays:** installers and retailers, ~A$120–250 per install, bundled into the quote.
2. **EV battery-health and residual-value gap cover** for novated-lease drivers and fleets.
   - **Who pays:** salary packagers and fleets, ~A$300–700 per vehicle.

The capital and claims risk sits with a carrier (Lloyd's syndicate or local insurer) under a binder. The MGA earns 25–35% commission plus a profit commission.

**Why now.**

- **Battery volumes are huge, and installation quality is poor.**
  - The Cheaper Home Batteries Program passed 250,000 installs by Mar 2026 **[verified: Energy-Storage.news]**. Reports say 500,000 by Aug 2026 **[unverified: single secondary source]**.
  - The Clean Energy Regulator reportedly found **more than 60% of inspected installations substandard** **[verified: secondary reporting of CER findings, RenewEconomy/Pulse]**.
  - A workmanship warranty becomes worthless when the installer closes **[verified: Energy Matters]**. Rebate step-downs (from 1 May 2026) will shake out installers **[verified: Energy Matters]**.
- **There is a regulatory template and an enforcement gap.**
  - The UK's Renewable Energy Consumer Code requires members to **insure the workmanship warranties they issue** **[verified: reonic, describing the UK RECC]**.
  - Australia's NETCC has no such requirement that I could find **[verified absence across CEC/Solar Victoria/NETCC sources; confirm with NETCC]**.
  - A retailer expelled from NETCC in Dec 2025 kept trading **[verified: search summary of NETCC enforcement]**.
  - Regulators need a lever, and an insurance-backed guarantee is one they can mandate. This is the "regulation as a channel" bet.
- **EV resale values are uncertain.**
  - The FBT exemption cost ~A$2B in 3 years against a A$205M forecast. It phases down from 1 Apr 2027 **[verified: NRMA, CarExpert, Savvy]**.
  - Novated EV leases grew to "over 100,000" against a forecast of 4,700 **[unverified]**.
  - Lessees carry the gap between the ATO residual and market value **[verified: Gridly; contract-dependent]**.
  - Australian owner confidence in resale value fell from 76% (2024) to 56% (2026) **[verified: Insurance Business AU, NRMA data]**.
  - A big wave of novated EVs reaches lease-end in 2027–29, into a market that also receives the post-FBT-phase-down supply.

**Unfair inputs.**

- **Strong:**
  - Statistics and pricing under uncertainty.
  - Ability to build telemetry and claims-data pipelines (CS).
  - CFO credibility with capacity providers.
  - Logistics and physical-asset background from Fulfilio (installers are a field-ops network).
- **Weak:**
  - **No insurance background or relationships with carriers and Lloyd's.** He needs an experienced underwriter or actuary co-founder.
  - Policy lobbying is slow.
  - Long-tail (10-year) risk means profit commission and credibility take years to arrive.

**Market and model.**

- **Battery and solar guarantees:**
  - ~150–250k battery installs and ~250–300k solar systems a year **[estimate]**.
  - 30% penetration × ~A$180 ≈ **A$25–30M a year in gross written premium**.
  - If it is mandated, A$70–100M.
- **EV gap cover:** ~40–60k novated EVs a year **[estimate]** × 25% × A$500 ≈ A$5–8M of premium, plus fleets.
- **MGA economics:**
  - Commission of ~30% on ~A$30M of premium = A$9M revenue.
  - EBITDA margin 35–45% → A$3–4M.
  - Valued at 10–15x EBITDA → A$35–60M **[estimate; multiples unverified]**.
- **Comparables:**
  - Steadfast's agencies place large premium volumes, and it consolidated seven MGAs into Miramar (which bought Sports Underwriting Australia, May 2026) **[verified: Insurance Asia News]**.
  - KKR, Dragoneer and Amwins proposed buying Steadfast at ~A$5.5B, with Amwins taking the agencies **[verified: Coverager/The Insurer]**. That shows strategic demand for AU underwriting agencies.

**Capital and structure.**

- **Funding:**
  - VC or insurtech seed of A$2–4M, plus founder money of A$0.5M.
  - The carrier provides risk capital. A fronting arrangement plus reinsurance is possible.
- **Regulation:**
  - Start as an authorised representative of an existing underwriting agency, then get his own AFSL (general insurance) in year 2.
  - Retail products need DDO target-market determinations.
  - EV gap cover sold alongside finance risks ASIC's **add-on insurance deferred-sales model**. Sell it B2B through fleets and packagers, or as embedded cover, to avoid that.
- **Founder ownership:** ~55–65% after seed plus a co-founder underwriter, then ~40–50% after a Series A if needed.

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Outcome | P | Founder wealth | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail | 45% | A$1.8M | No binder, adverse loss experience, or no mandate and slow uptake |
| Modest | 25% | A$3.2M | A$8–12M premium niche agency, A$300–400k pay, small sale |
| Good | 20% | A$8M | A$30M premium; sold to Steadfast/Miramar, Amwins or Insurance House at year 7–8 |
| Great | 10% | A$25M | Mandate arrives; becomes the national guarantee scheme operator; plus EV RV data business |

**EV ≈ A$5.4M; median ≈ A$3.2M** (cumulative 70% falls in modest). It has the biggest spread and the most binary outcome of the four.

**Time to replace $500k.** About 4–6 years. Little can start while employed beyond carrier and installer soundings.

**Job satisfaction.** High on intellect: new risk pools, telemetry data, regulation design. Lower day to day: carrier politics, claims disputes, and installer sales.

**Key risks.**

1. **No capacity.** Carriers fear correlated battery fire, model-defect and installer-collapse losses, and a 10-year tail.
2. **No mandate means a slow voluntary market.** Installers won't pay A$150 unless consumers or regulators demand it.
3. **Correlated losses.** A single battery-model recall or a wave of installer insolvencies after the rebate can wipe out a year's profit commission and the binder with it.

**What kills it:** no binder by month 9.

**90-day test (~A$25k).**

- **Capacity:**
  - Meet 6 capacity providers (Lloyd's via Sydney coverholders, and local insurers).
  - **Pass:** ≥2 written indications of appetite.
- **Demand:** ≥5 retailers or installers doing more than 1,000 installs a year sign letters of intent at ≥A$120 per install.
- **Regulator:** a meeting with Solar Victoria or the Clean Energy Regulator.
- **Data:** an installer failure-rate dataset assembled from ASIC insolvency records.
- **Kill:** zero carrier appetite.

### D. "Mark": an independent loan-level valuation and monitoring utility for private credit held by super funds

**One-liner.** An AI-plus-analyst service that independently values and monitors private-credit loans (non-property and property) for super funds, platforms and credit managers.

- **What it produces:**
  - Quarterly marks.
  - Early-warning covenant analytics.
  - ASIC- and APRA-ready reporting.
- **Who pays:** asset owners and managers, at ~1–4 bps on assets covered or ~A$40–250k per mandate a year.

**Why now.**

- **ASIC is scrutinising private credit.** Its 2025–26 private debt review flagged valuation, fee and concentration issues, and its 2026 priorities target funds lending to retail clients **[verified: KWM, HSF Kramer, savings.com.au]**.
- **APRA's valuation standard applies.** APRA's SPS 530 valuation expectations already apply to super funds' unlisted assets **[estimate: from knowledge, not re-verified]**.
- **The asset pool is large.** Private credit is ~A$225B; super funds hold ~A$30–35B **[verified]**.
- **AI changes the cost.** Reading loan files and management accounts at scale was the cost barrier, and LLMs remove most of it.
- **Pattern from earlier rounds:** this is a data layer sold to whoever owns the capital, the configuration the brief says works.

**Unfair inputs.**

- Statistics plus CS to build the engine.
- CFO credibility with investment teams.
- BCG networks into super funds.
- **Weak:**
  - Valuation is reputation-led: Kroll, Lincoln, Houlihan, the Big 4 and Evidentia already serve this.
  - Long procurement (CPS 230 vendor onboarding takes 6–18 months, round 3).
  - He has no valuation-firm brand.

**Market and model.**

- **Australian market:** perhaps A$20–50M a year of addressable fees **[estimate]**.
- **Path:** A$3–6M ARR by year 5 if he wins 10–20 mandates.
- **Exit:** sale to a global valuation or data firm at 4–8x revenue **[estimate]**.
- **Bigger version:** the international play is larger, but competes with 73 Strings and Chronograph **[unverified funding figures]**.

**Capital and structure.** Bootstrap to A$1M ARR, then raise A$3–5M of seed or Series A. Founder ownership ~60–70%.

**Outcome distribution (10 years).**

| Outcome | P | Founder wealth | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail | 40% | A$2.3M | Procurement stalls; return to work after year 2 |
| Modest | 35% | A$3.2M | A$2–3M ARR consultancy-like business |
| Good | 20% | A$6M | A$8M ARR, sold at 5x |
| Great | 5% | A$15M | Becomes the AU standard; international sale |

**EV ≈ A$3.9M; median ≈ A$3.2M.**

**Time to replace $500k.** About 5–6 years. Pilots could run while employed, but conflicts are likely if his employer has investors in the space.

**Job satisfaction.** Medium-high. It's model-building, but the work becomes repetitive and audit-like, and he advises on risk rather than taking it.

**Key risks.**

1. A trusted-brand incumbent adds AI and undercuts him.
2. Procurement cycles outlast his runway.
3. Liability for wrong marks; professional-indemnity insurance needed.

**What kills it:** fewer than 2 paid pilots in 9 months.

**90-day test.**

- Meet 12 super-fund and credit-manager CIOs and risk teams.
- **Pass:** ≥2 paid pilots at ≥A$40k, or a written RFP.
- **Kill:** "we use Kroll" from everyone.

## Top pick: A, Succession Credit, entered through B

**Recommendation:** build an acquisition-credit platform for the boomer business transfer, starting as BookCo (rent rolls and broker books priced with the round-3 engine), then widening into general SME succession lending as the fund grows.

**Why:**

1. **It sells capital to the demand that exists.** Hundreds of thousands of owners need buyers, and buyers can't borrow against goodwill. The brief's pattern says the founder wins when pricing risk, not running operations, is the job.
2. **The round-3 leading candidate becomes the first sleeve of a much larger market.**
   - The engine's value (~$50–100k per deal to the buyer) is captured as spread and carry, not report fees.
