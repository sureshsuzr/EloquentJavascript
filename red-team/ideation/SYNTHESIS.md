# Ideation synthesis: what to build

3 Nov 2026. Stage 1: 10 independent lenses generated 40 ideas (`stage1/`). Stage 2: 5 independent evaluators scored a merged shortlist of 9 candidates plus the rent-roll benchmark, head to head (`stage2/`, model in `stage2/wealth_model.py`). Many primary sources were blocked from the session; figures marked unverified in the reports should be checked.

## The recommendation

**Build a "pricing-led acquirer": a holding company that buys boomer-owned businesses cheaply and makes them worth more through statistical pricing, AI and CFO discipline.**

1. **Engine first (while employed).** Build the succession-capital scoring engine (C5): find, score and price SME acquisition targets from accounting and invoice data. The rent-roll pricing engine (C0) becomes one vertical inside it.
2. **First deal: a specialty B2B distributor** (C4), bought at ≤5x EBITDA, where pricing leakage of ≥150 bps is proven on its own invoice data. Fund it deal by deal from family offices as an independent sponsor: ~25–30% founder ownership, ~A$300–350k salary from close, debt secured on stock and debtors with **no personal guarantee**.
3. **Quit only when the deal is real:** a signed sale agreement at ≤5x EBITDA, a no-PG debt term sheet, and ≥A$3M of equity committed.
4. **Second vertical (years 3–4): ANZ vertical software** (C1), once you've left your SaaS employer (officer duties and target overlap make it hard to pursue while employed).
5. **Time-boxed alternative first vertical: SMSF compliance practices** (C3), only if its 90-day test (below) is strong. Division 296 and the auditor exodus give it the only hard deadline on the list.

Why this wins: it is the **only structure every evaluator ranked at or near the top** (four of five put C4 or the C5/C4 combination first), it uses the edge every earlier round kept finding (pricing under uncertainty, captured as equity rather than fees), it replaces $500k fastest (~18–30 months), and **it fails cheaply while you're still employed**.

## The honest wealth result (read this before deciding)

The wealth model (`stage2/01`) re-estimated every candidate on one consistent set of assumptions: base rates of ~5–6% for a great outcome (stage 1 used 10–12%), investor hurdles, illiquidity, salary, and tax under the proposed CGT change.

- **Quitting now for any candidate is roughly break-even or worse than staying on expected value, and worse on a risk-adjusted basis.**
- **The staged path (quit only on a signed deal) is clearly ahead on EV:** ~+A$0.46M for C4. But it still has only a **~32% chance of beating staying**, and it is slightly negative risk-adjusted (−A$0.40M).
- C0/C5 while employed is positive on both (+A$0.39M EV, +A$0.17M risk-adjusted), because you keep the salary.

**So pure wealth doesn't make the case for leaving. Job satisfaction and ambition do, and the staged structure is what keeps that choice cheap.** The three inputs that swing the answer most:
1. **The value of your current employer equity.** Value it first: if it's large, staying wins more clearly.
2. **The tax regime.** The May 2026 Budget proposal to replace the 50% CGT discount with indexation plus a 30% minimum from 1 Jul 2027 is reported in three separate rounds but unverified. Under the current regime, every candidate except C6 beats staying.
3. **Exit values,** and a one-year delay (costs C4-staged ~A$0.32M).

## What stage 2 corrected from stage 1

- **The AI accounting roll-up (stage-1 top at ~A$9.3M) is not open.** Multiplier (US$35M raised, 10 acquisitions), Grant Thornton US buying GT Australia, and Kelly Partners and Count keep buying (05). The A$9.3M was also pre-tax equity without salary, so it wasn't comparable (01).
- **Software is not as cheap as it looks.** Public SaaS multiples fell (SEG index 5.7x → 3.2x), but private deal multiples held at ~4.0x revenue. Valsoft, Constellation (via Vela/Libra) and Arcadea all bought in Australia in 2026. A software ETF rose ~41% from April to October 2026 (04, 05).
- **The private-markets valuation layer has incumbents:** Kroll Australia, MSCI and the Big 4. No 2026 APRA mandate was found, though ASIC's REP 841 (14 Oct 2026) and the fund freezes create pressure (05).
- **The credit fund is the wrong vintage:** private-credit stress (Metrics freezing >A$9B, ASIC stop orders) makes a 2027 first-time raise very hard (02, 05).
- **New risk for every roll-up:** mandatory ACCC merger control from 1 Jan 2026; serial acquisitions over 3 years may be aggregated (unverified) (05).

## Before anything else (by mid-December 2026)

1. **Employment:** review your contract and Corporations Act ss 181–183 duties if you're an officer. Get written consent for outside activity; build on your own devices and accounts. Don't source software targets while employed (04).
2. **Value your employer equity** realistically (vesting, liquidity, likely exit). It decides how high the bar is (01).
3. **Tax advice** on the proposed CGT change and on structuring a holdco and founder equity before 30 Jun 2027 (01; earlier rounds).
4. **A lender conversation** to test that a ~A$5–15M distributor can be financed with cash-flow and asset-based debt and **no personal guarantee**. This is the model's least-verified load-bearing assumption (01, 02, 03).

## Decision calendar

- **15 Dec 2026:** employment consent, employer-equity valuation, tax adviser, first lender call.
- **30 Apr 2027:** 90-day results. Choose the first vertical (distribution, or SMSF if its test clearly wins).
- **30 Jun 2027:** LOI on the first acquisition; holdco and tax structure in place.
- **By 31 Dec 2027: resign only with** a signed sale agreement (≤5x EBITDA), a no-PG debt term sheet and ≥A$3M equity committed.
- **If no LOI by 31 Dec 2027:** stay employed, keep the engine as a side asset (it still has positive value per the wealth model), and re-run this exercise.
- **Years 3–4:** add vertical software (C1) as the second vertical; consider the lending product (BookCo) once the platform has a track record.

**Personal limits:** ≤A$0.75–1.2M of your own cash, no personal guarantees, nothing secured on the family home.
