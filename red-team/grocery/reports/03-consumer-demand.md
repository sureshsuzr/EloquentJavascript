# Consumer Demand Researcher: red-team report

## Verdict
**As a consumer grocery marketplace for the mainstream, it is fatal. It survives only as a narrow niche with a low ceiling.** Australians are buying more groceries online, but nearly all of that growth goes to Woolworths and Coles. Both now deliver within 2 hours, run subscriptions, and are on Uber Eats or DoorDash. Quick commerce did not die after Milkrun and Voly closed: the major chains and the delivery apps absorbed it.

**The single biggest issue:** almost nobody needs one delivery that combines several independent shops, often enough and badly enough to pay for it. Melbourne already ran this exact experiment. YourGrocer ran a multi-shop basket from Brunswick independents for about nine years. It reached $9.2M in sales, still lost money, and its assets sold for $3,000 in December 2022.

## Verdict on the business model
From a demand lens, the **full consumer marketplace with delivery is not the business**. There is real demand, but it sits in three small pockets:
- premium fresh food (butcher, baker, greengrocer, deli) for affluent inner-city food lovers;
- specialty and ethnic grocery for people who live away from their community's shopping strip;
- apartment-dweller top-ups.

These pockets overlap less than the "one basket, many shops" idea assumes. Each one is already partly served:
- premium fresh by Harris Farm with Amazon (Sydney);
- top-ups by Milkrun, Uber Eats and DoorDash;
- ethnic grocery by Uber Eats and DoorDash listings and by in-person shopping-strip habits.

Consumers have already chosen where they gather, and it is Uber Eats, DoorDash and the majors' own apps. So the version with the most consumer pull is one that **feeds those apps**: shelf-scan cataloguing that gets small specialty merchants listed. It does not build a new consumer brand. If the founder insists on going direct to consumers, the only defensible version is **scheduled, batched delivery (not instant) of specialty and fresh baskets in 2–3 dense pockets of Melbourne**. Treat it as a test of the software, not as the business.

## Attacks on the thesis

**1. The direct precedent failed in the founder's own city (High).** YourGrocer was a Melbourne marketplace that delivered from several independent butchers, greengrocers and bakers in one consolidated drop. It used a once-a-day pickup run and no warehouse, which is structurally the founder's model.
- FY22: sales of $9.2M, gross profit of about $2.9M (31%), and a loss of about $680k **[verified: Startup Daily coverage of its Birchal offer]**.
- Its average order was $158 **[verified: same]**. That implies about 58k orders a year, roughly 1,100 a week across Melbourne **[estimate: $9.2M ÷ $158]**.
- It sought up to $1.5M through crowdfunding, raised only about $225k from 60 backers, and closed on 16 December 2022. Rewardle bought its assets for $3,000 **[verified: Startup Daily, Business News Australia]**.
- Sydney's Bundlfresh ran a similar model (60 retailers, about 3,000 customers, 47 suburbs, 2022) and has had no visible traction since **[verified for 2022: trade press; current status unverified]**.

So in practice, a large basket and loyal customers were not enough. About 1,100 orders a week after nine years is roughly the ceiling for unaided demand in one city. Shelf-scan software lowers merchant onboarding cost. It does not create more consumer demand.

**2. Online growth goes to the majors, which already offer speed (High).**
- Woolworths Australian Food online sales were about $8.7B in FY26, up about 19%, at 16.4% penetration **[verified: IGD, secondary]**. Q1 FY27 penetration was 18.2%, and 48% of delivery orders arrived within 2 hours **[verified: Woolworths Q1 FY27 trading update, 14 weeks to 4 Oct 2026, via secondary coverage]**.
- Coles Supermarkets online sales were $5.6B in FY26, up 26.4%, at 13.6% penetration **[verified: Coles FY26 ASX release, 25 Aug 2026]**.
- Together that is about $14.3B. Woolworths and Coles hold about 67% of supermarket sales overall, but probably more than 85% of online grocery **[estimate: the majors' online sales against the absence of Aldi and the small Metcash online footprint]**.

"Speed" and "convenience" are no longer gaps a start-up can own.

**3. Quick commerce was absorbed, not abandoned (High).**
- Milkrun is now Woolworths' own on-demand service: 628 stores in Q1 FY26 and a $5 delivery fee **[verified: Woolworths Q1 FY26 report; CHOICE Oct 2025]**.
- Woolworths joined DoorDash in December 2025. Coles went exclusive with Uber Eats and is expanding to up to 17,000 products **[verified: Inside FMCG, 11 Dec 2025]**.
- Aldi is on DoorDash: a Canberra trial in 2025, and Special Buys nationally from January 2026 **[verified: The New Daily, Retail Insight Network]**.
- IGA has been on DoorDash since 2021 (100+ stores), and so is Drakes **[verified: Inside Retail, Food Mag]**.

Independents that want an extra sales channel can already get one without the founder.

**4. Price: independent shelf prices plus a take rate plus a fee is a losing offer for anything but specialty items (High).**
- CHOICE (October 2025) found item prices on delivery apps about 11% above in-store. Whole baskets came in 31–39% above in-store once fees were included. Delivery fees ran from $5 (Milkrun) to $11.32 **[verified: CHOICE]**.
- Independents' shelf prices already sit above Woolworths and Coles on branded packaged goods **[estimate: 5–15%, based on ACCC inquiry commentary on independents losing share; unverified as a number]**.
- If the platform's 10–20% take is passed into prices, as Uber Eats and DoorDash do, a packaged-goods basket ends up roughly 25–45% dearer than Woolworths online.
- This comes during a cost-of-living squeeze: Woolworths' average prices have fallen for 11 straight quarters as it pushes value **[verified: Q1 FY27 coverage]**.

Only items with no supermarket equivalent can carry that premium.

**5. The multi-merchant basket is a supply-side idea, not a consumer need (High).** Shoppers buying from several shops get that in person on shopping strips (Sydney Rd, Box Hill, Footscray, Springvale, Victoria St) and enjoy it; they rarely want it delivered. Packaged goods go to the majors. That leaves weekly fresh produce from 2–3 specialists.
- The basket is about $60–160 **[estimate; YourGrocer's $158 is the top end]**.
- The order frequency is fortnightly or weekly for the most loyal customers and monthly for most **[estimate]**.
- Each extra merchant in a basket also raises the risk of a stockout or late handover. The aggregation consumers would value is the one that is hardest to run reliably.

**6. Trust in substitutions from small shops depends on how fresh the data is (Med-High).**
- Freshness and quality are the top worries for online grocery shoppers **[unverified: Statista survey, 2022]**.
- Small shops' stock moves quickly (meat cuts, bakery items that sell out by early afternoon), and shelf scans taken every few days will be stale.
- One wrong cut from a butcher feels worse to a shopper than a missing tin. Woolworths and Coles absorb substitutions with refunds and scale.
- A start-up needs an item fill rate above 95% on promised items, or repeat orders collapse **[estimate: industry rule of thumb]**.

**7. Shoppers are sticky (Med).** In a Canstar Blue survey, 55% of online grocery shoppers always use the same site **[verified: Canstar Blue, via Smartspotter; date uncertain]**. The majors' subscriptions (Delivery Unlimited and its Coles equivalent) lock in the weekly shop.

**8. Ethnic and specialty demand is real but split up (Med).** The communities are concentrated, each needs its own merchants and catalogue, and shoppers are often price-sensitive and loyal to the shop. Uber Eats and DoorDash already list many Asian grocers; HungryPanda serves Chinese-speaking customers **[unverified: no merchant counts found]**. The best customers are younger diaspora in CBD and inner-ring apartments who live away from their community's shopping strip. That is a niche of tens of thousands of households, not millions.

## Things the brief gets wrong, misses or understates
- **"Aldi absence online" is out of date.** Aldi delivers through DoorDash (Special Buys nationally from January 2026, plus a groceries trial) **[verified]**. It still has no own-brand online shop.
- **"Milkrun exit" is out of date.** Milkrun the start-up closed in April 2023. Woolworths bought the brand, rolled it out to more than 600 stores, and charges a $5 fee **[verified]**.
- **Amazon:** Amazon has launched fresh grocery in Sydney only, with Harris Farm, from 28 January 2026, across 80+ suburbs **[verified: Amazon AU release]**. It has no Melbourne fresh offer yet. **Time-sensitive:** a Melbourne roll-out would compete directly with the premium-fresh pocket.
- **Missed entirely:** YourGrocer, the closest prior attempt at this model, in Melbourne (see attack 1).
- **Understated:** the sales channel independents would get already exists through DoorDash and Uber Eats, which also bring the consumer demand.
- **"Global from day 1":** demand is hyper-local and catalogue-specific. Each suburb's merchant mix is the product. From a consumer lens this is a business built suburb by suburb.
- **Time-sensitive:** Woolworths holds an Investor Day on 18 November 2026, likely to cover eCommerce and Milkrun plans. Read it before committing.

## Improvements (ranked by impact)
1. **Don't build a consumer brand first. Sell into existing consumer demand.** Pitch the shelf-scan catalogue to Uber Eats, DoorDash and Mercato-style platforms as the way to list long-tail specialty merchants that lack point-of-sale integration. Consumer acquisition cost becomes their problem.
2. **If going direct to consumers, change the offer from "one basket from many local shops" to "Saturday-morning specialty fresh, delivered".** Use scheduled windows (for example Thursday evening and Saturday morning), pre-orders and batched routes. That pre-commits stock (fixing freshness of the scan data) and raises orders per route. Speed is a race against Woolworths that a start-up can't win.
3. **Exclude packaged goods.** List only items with no supermarket equivalent: butcher cuts, artisan bread, deli items, ethnic fresh and frozen food, specialty pantry. That removes the price comparison that kills the basket.
4. **Charge a visible delivery fee or membership, not a hidden markup.** Shoppers react worse to hidden markups than to visible fees (see CHOICE coverage). A $79–99/year membership tests real commitment.
5. **Pick suburbs for merchant density × apartment density × low car use.** In Melbourne: Brunswick/Coburg/Northcote (food lovers, YourGrocer's original base); the CBD, Southbank and Docklands (high-density apartments; about 70% of residents in the Melbourne SAL have both parents born overseas **[verified: ABS 2021 QuickStats]**); and Box Hill/Doncaster for Chinese-Australian households.
6. **Guarantee substitutions** ("we refund and credit $5 if we miss an item"). Publish fill rates.

## Tests & kill criteria (90-day demand test, Melbourne, about $25–40k)
| Phase | Test | Metric | Pass / kill threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–3 | Two landing pages (premium fresh in Brunswick/Northcote; specialty Asian in CBD/Southbank), $6k paid social, real prices and fees shown | Visitor-to-waitlist rate; cost per signup; deposit paid ($10 credit) | Pass: ≥8% signup, ≤$8 per signup, ≥25% of signups pay the deposit. Kill: <3% signup or <10% deposits |
| Weeks 3–12 | Concierge pre-orders: 10–15 merchants hand-catalogued, order form, two scheduled windows a week, a hired courier, real shelf prices + 12% markup + $9 fee | Paying customers; repeat rate within 4 weeks; orders per customer per month | Pass: ≥150 paying customers by day 60; ≥40% repeat; ≥2 orders/month. Kill: <75 customers or <25% repeat |
| Weeks 3–12 | Basket composition | Average order; share of orders from ≥2 merchants; share of items with no supermarket substitute | Pass: average ≥$85; ≥50% multi-merchant; ≥60% non-substitutable. Kill: average <$60 or <30% multi-merchant (if aggregation isn't valued, the core idea is dead) |
| Weeks 6–12 | Fee elasticity A/B: $6 vs $12 fee vs $89/year membership | Conversion and retention by arm | Pass: the $12 arm keeps ≥70% of the $6 arm's conversion |
| Weeks 3–12 | Fulfilment quality | Item fill rate; complaints; Net Promoter Score (NPS) | Pass: ≥95% fill, NPS ≥40. Kill: fill <88% after week 6 |
| Day 90 | Extrapolation | Projected orders a week per 10k households in the target area | Pass: ≥40 per 10k households a week (about 4× YourGrocer's city-wide density) **[estimate]** |

Run a parallel cheap test: offer the same merchants a free listing on DoorDash or Uber Eats via the scan tool. If their orders there beat the concierge orders, that confirms consumers don't need a new app.

## Answers to the brief's questions in my domain
**Q3: Will consumers choose it, and for which baskets?**
- **The weekly packaged-goods shop: no.** The majors win on price, speed, subscriptions and habit.
- **Convenience top-ups: no.** Milkrun, Uber Eats and DoorDash already deliver in under an hour.
- **Specialty, ethnic and premium fresh, scheduled: yes, for a small segment.** These shoppers are affluent and food-focused, or diaspora households in inner-city apartments. They order fortnightly to weekly at roughly $70–160 per basket **[estimate; YourGrocer $158]**.
- **Ranking of what these shoppers value:** fresh quality and specific items first, convenience second, "supporting local" third (a tiebreaker, not a driver), and price last within the niche.

**Q1 (demand inputs only):**
- Realistic average order is about $80 for specialty and top-up baskets and about $150 for premium fresh **[estimate]**.
- At a 15% take plus a $9 fee, revenue per order is about $21–31, against a likely multi-stop pickup and delivery cost of $18–28. The courier minimum of about $31.30/hour applies if the platform engages couriers on the same terms **[unverified]**.
- Customer acquisition cost for a niche grocery start-up is plausibly $40–80 **[estimate]**. Even YourGrocer's 31% gross margin on a $158 average order did not cover overheads.

**Q6:** Demand is built city by city, and within a city suburb by suburb.

**Q9 (wealth):** Beating a $500k salary needs roughly $150–300M a year in sales at a 10–15% take, or a $50M+ exit **[estimate]**. That is about 2–4M orders a year, roughly 20–40 times YourGrocer's peak. Consumer demand gives no evidence that this exists in Australia for a marketplace of independents.

**Q10:** The 90-day concierge test above. The decisive metrics are 4-week repeat rate and multi-merchant share.

## Questions I'd hand to other specialists
- **Unit economics:** What do a multi-stop pickup plus drop cost with 2–3 merchants within 1.5 km, at 4 vs 10 orders per route-hour? At what orders per suburb per day does it break even?
- **Competition:** Do Uber Eats and DoorDash already list Melbourne butchers, bakers and Asian grocers at scale? What do they charge them (take rate)? Would either of them pay for a shelf-scan catalogue tool?
- **Merchants:** Will butchers and bakers commit stock for scheduled pre-orders? Will they accept 12–15% when Uber Eats and DoorDash charge more but bring demand?
- **Technology:** Can shelf scans capture loose and weighed fresh goods (meat cuts, bread) and prices with enough accuracy to keep fill rates above 95%?
- **Legal:** Does the courier minimum standard apply to a grocery aggregator's couriers? What are the cold-chain rules for meat in multi-stop runs?
- **Founder fit:** Is a demand ceiling the size of YourGrocer compatible with the founder's goals at all? Would the software-only path suit his background better?
