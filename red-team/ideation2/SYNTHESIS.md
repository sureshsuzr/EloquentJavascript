# Ideation round 2: synthesis

4 Nov 2026. Objective: equity value from a cash-generating business, with a **high probability of a positive outcome** and low chance of total loss; salary can stay low. Two tracks:
- **Track A:** the founder's idea, an AI-native finance function (close, collections, payments, compliance, reporting) priced by outcome.
- **Track B:** Australia's resources and renewable-energy advantages.

Stage 1: 9 lenses (`stage1/`). Stage 2: 5 evaluators (`stage2/`, wealth model in `stage2/wealth_model.py`). Many primary sources were blocked; check labelled figures.

## Bottom line

**Build one company: an AI-native finance office with an outcome-based service contract, aimed at long-lived, deadline-driven clients. Start it while employed, with a chartered-accountant co-founder, and quit only on contracted revenue.**

- **This is the founder's own idea (Track A),** made viable by choosing the right customers and contract.
- **The first segment is chosen by a 90-day test** between three options; all share one platform:
  1. **Renewable and storage project companies (SPVs)**, served directly and as a white-label back office for asset managers. Lowest churn, 10–15-year assets, per-SPV fees, and the direct bridge to Track B.
  2. **Australian subsidiaries of foreign groups and VC-backed scaleups** ("Closewise"). The best wealth result in the model (01).
  3. **ASX explorers and juniors.** Needs a Perth CA/company-secretary co-founder bringing 5–10 boards (02).
- **Contract:** a fixed fee plus service-level credits capped at 10–15% of fees, gain-share only on cash outcomes you control, and a cap on the client's corporate costs.
  - **No ATO-penalty indemnity:** PI and tax-audit insurance generally exclude penalties and contractually assumed liability (04).
  - **No royalties:** boards, brokers and listing rules resist them. For cash-short juniors, take up to 30% of fees in options under the client's placement capacity (02).
- **Capital products** (refund advances against the critical-minerals production tax offset and R&D refunds, invoice finance) **start as referrals.** They move onto your balance sheet only with a licence (or authorised-representative arrangement) and 24 months of loss data (04).

Why this answer:
- **Three of five evaluators independently chose the resources-and-energy finance office** (AI competition 03, capital/licensing 04, resources insider 02).
- **The wealth model chose the same platform from the other end:** Closewise first, then adding the resources vertical (01).
- **Founder fit (05)** preferred the cold-chain energy option but ranked the resources finance office second.

## The honest numbers (read before deciding)

On one consistent set of assumptions (01), **no candidate exceeds a ~33% chance of beating staying employed**, against 40–65% claimed in stage 1.
- **Why:** after tax and lost employer equity, a "modest" outcome (a profitable A$3–5M-revenue services firm) almost never beats a A$500k job. So P(positive) is roughly the chance of a good or great outcome, ~25–30% for everything.
- **Expected gain over staying (dEV, after tax):**
  - Closewise entered as a hybrid: +A$0.59M, P(total loss) 9%.
  - Resources office with royalties, as a hybrid: +A$0.11M. Service-only: −A$0.43M.
  - SPV Office alone: −A$0.28M.
  - ColdCore + MeterProof: −A$0.61M.
  - Staying employed has the best floor (P10 ≈ A$4.07M).
- **The resources insider (02)** put the merged office at EV ~A$4.8M vs A$3.25M staying, P(positive) ~42%, P(total loss) ~12%. The AI-competition analyst (03): ~A$5.0M, ~47%.
- **The S1 dispute is resolved both ways (01):** generic SME outcome-priced accounting loses (−A$0.43M). The scaleup and foreign-subsidiary segment is the better one. Stage-1's A$11.8M was pre-tax, used a 9% great-outcome rate, 2.5x revenue exits for a services firm, and counted half of modest outcomes as wins.

**What this means:** on wealth alone, the founder paths beat staying only modestly and only about one time in three to four. **The hybrid entry is what makes the downside acceptable:** it cuts total-loss risk to ~9%. Job satisfaction and ambition, not expected wealth, are the reason to do it.

**Biggest swing inputs:** exit multiple, tax regime, the value of current employer equity, and a one-year slip (01).

## Before anything else (by mid-December 2026)

1. **Employment:** contract review, outside-interests consent, IP on your own devices. Check whether finance services for scaleups conflict with your SaaS employer's customers.
2. **Value your employer equity:** it is one of the biggest swing inputs (01).
3. **Tax advice:** the change from 1 Jul 2027 (cost-base indexation plus a 30% minimum tax) is reported by secondary sources as passed on 25 Jun 2026, but the Act wasn't read. The model puts a founder's effective rate at ~45% vs 23.5%, which shifts every dEV by A$0.4–1.0M. Also check the announced 50% active-asset reduction for businesses under A$10M turnover. Structure the company and founder equity accordingly (01).
4. **Co-founder search:** a chartered accountant and registered tax agent (TPB) is needed in every segment; for juniors, one with Perth boards.
5. **PI insurance quote** for capped service credits.

## Decision calendar

- **15 Dec 2026:** employer consent, equity valuation, tax adviser, co-founder search begun.
- **30 Apr 2027:** test results; choose the first segment.
- **30 Jun 2027:** company formed with co-founder; first paying pilots delivered by the co-founder while you stay employed.
- **Quit rule:** ≥A$0.5–1.0M contracted ARR, with ≥3-year terms for SPVs or ≥10 clients for scaleups/subsidiaries. Expected around H1 2028. The hybrid entry is what lowers P(total loss) to ~9% (01).
- **Years 2–4:** add the second segment; introduce capital products as referrals, then on balance sheet after licensing and 24 months of loss data.
- **If the tests fail:** stay employed. The model shows staying beats most quit-now paths, and you keep the option to back the co-founder or angel-invest.
