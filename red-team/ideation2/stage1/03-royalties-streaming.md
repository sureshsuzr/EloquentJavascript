# Stage 1, lens 03: mining royalties, streaming and critical-minerals finance (Track B)

Date: 4 Nov 2026. Lens: royalties and streams, outcome-linked mine finance, the Critical Minerals Production Tax Incentive (CMPTI), and Japan/Korea offtake as outcome-linked capital. Track B, with the Track A intersection kept in scope as the brief allows. 16 web searches. Many sources are summaries of filings rather than the filings themselves; labels show which.

## 1. What the market looks like in late 2026

**A royalty is the purest outcome-priced instrument in resources.** The holder is paid only when ore is mined and sold, as a percentage of revenue (gross revenue royalty, GRR) or of net smelter return (NSR). It carries no capex or opex exposure and no obligation to fund cost overruns. The risks are geology, the operator, commodity prices and timing. Pricing it is a statistics problem: P(production) × timing × grade/tonnage × price curve × operator quality, discounted. That is the founder's core skill.

**The scale players are consolidating and moving up-market.**
- **Deterra Royalties (ASX: DRR):** FY25 revenue A$263.4M, about A$219M of it from the BHP Mining Area C iron-ore royalty. Bought Trident Royalties for about £144M (≈A$276M) in Sep 2024 to get the Thacker Pass lithium royalty, then sold the non-core gold offtakes and royalties [verified: Deterra FY25 results via TipRanks/FilingReader summaries]. Deterra wants A$50M+ cheques, not prospector royalties.
- **Elemental Altus + EMX** merged on 13 Nov 2025 into Elemental Royalty Corp (≈US$1B, 200+ assets, with Tether as a US$100M financier). It also paid US$52M for a 2% GRR over Genesis Minerals' Laverton (WA) [verified: Newsfile, SEC 40-F]. EMX was the archetypal *prospect generator*, so that model now sits inside a mid-tier company.
- **Altius Minerals agreed to buy Lithium Royalty Corp** in Dec 2025, which holds a 2.5% GOR over Finniss (NT) [verified: Business Wire; reported deal value differs by source].
- **Franco-Nevada** bought Venus Metals' 1% NSR over Youanmi (WA) for $46M upfront plus $1M deferred (2026) [verified: Pulse/Discovery Alert; currency not confirmed]. One percent of one WA gold mine, owned by a junior, sold for ~$46M. **That is the upside a small royalty book is buying.**
- **Taurus** bought Anglo American's royalty package, including Onslow iron ore (WA), for up to US$195M (2024) [verified: Mining Weekly]. It co-invests with Gold Royalty Corp. **Sprott Resource Streaming and Royalty** writes US$20–50M deals, mostly in the Americas [verified: Fasken, Sprott].

**The small-ticket Australian niche is real, but it is not empty.**
- **Vox Royalty** holds ~70 royalties, **52 of them in Australia** (mid-2025 MD&A), with 2025 royalty revenue of ~US$12M. Its deals are small: a NSW producing royalty for up to A$650k, Wyloo North for A$1.5M, a private Australian group's portfolio for A$4.7M (2024), and Stockman (VIC) for A$5M plus up to A$10M deferred (Dec 2025) [verified: Vox SEC filings and press releases].
- **Red Hill Minerals (ASX: RHI)** runs off its 0.75% FOB Onslow royalty. It bought a 2% GRR over Sandstone for A$4M plus deferred payments, and a 1.5% NSR over Thomson (NSW) for **A$220k** [verified: RHI announcements via iTiger/Investorpa].
- Operators buy back royalties: Northern Star paid CuFe A$4M for the Crossroads royalty in Nov 2024 [verified]. **Arc Mineral Royalties** (Sep 2026) is a new TSX-V shell buying WA royalties [verified: Fasken].

**Read-through.** Exits exist and are deep: Franco, Elemental, Vox, Deterra, Altius, Taurus, operators. Buyers below A$1M are few, and the gap between A$0.2M and A$5M deals is a pricing problem as much as a capital one. The thousands of small royalties sitting with prospectors, families, deceased estates and juniors are unindexed. In WA a royalty is generally not a caveatable proprietary interest [verified: Clayton Utz 2019, citing Warden's Court authority; check current law]. So title and enforceability diligence is messy, which is a moat for whoever builds the data layer.

**Policy tailwinds.**
- **CMPTI:** a 10% *refundable* tax offset on eligible processing and refining costs for 31 critical minerals, for production 2027–28 to 2039–40, up to 10 years per project. Capex, financing and feedstock are excluded. It passed Parliament in Feb 2025; guidelines are to be finalised before 1 Jul 2027 [verified: industry.gov.au/CMPTI; ATO; Mining Weekly]. It was costed at ~A$7B [verified: Stockhead, 2024 Budget coverage]. Gilbert + Tobin note lenders may count it in cash flow available for debt service [verified: G+T]. **The CMPTI is itself an outcome-linked instrument: cash per unit of processing actually done.**
- **The US–Australia critical minerals framework** (20 Oct 2025) commits each side to ≥US$1B over six months against a US$8.5B pipeline. The **A$1.2B Critical Minerals Strategic Reserve** works through EFA offtake and stockpiling. The US stepped back from a price floor in Jan 2026, and Canberra says its "floor" will come through offtake [verified: HSF Kramer, IEA, Mining.com/Kitco].
- **Japan and Korea:** the Sojitz/JOGMEC A$200M Lynas offtake-linked investment (2023), POSCO prepayment and offtake structures, Korea Zinc's June 2026 talks with Canberra [verified: SMM, SmallCaps, Korea Times]. This is real capital, but it goes **to projects at FID scale (A$100M+)**. A founder with A$1M cannot be a principal there. He can be an arranger or a data provider.
- **Gold ≈ US$4,000–4,200/oz** in Oct 2026, off early-2026 highs [unverified: pricegold.net]. Gold royalty prices are rich, and buying at the top of the cycle is a known way to lose money in this sector.

## 3. The floor question (why this lens is attractive on the new objective)

Diversified royalties are among the few resource instruments that rarely go to zero. The holder has no capex calls, no cost-overrun exposure, and survives operator bankruptcy in most cases (the royalty runs with the tenement or the deed). The failure mode is **overpaying**, not wipe-out. This lens is therefore well matched to "avoid complete failure", **on two conditions**:
1. buy or receive royalties cheaply (Ideas 1, 2 and 4 all do, by different routes);
2. keep 50%+ of value in producing or near-production assets.

Streams and offtake prepayments carry more credit risk (they are closer to debt to a single developer) and need a far larger balance sheet. That is a place for Taurus, Sprott, EFA and JOGMEC, not a A$1M founder.

**Competition, honestly.** Above A$5M, the competitors are deep-pocketed and fast (Franco, Elemental, Deterra, Taurus). Below A$5M, Vox and Red Hill are active, but the long tail under A$1M is thinly served. The best structural edge is not "better capital" but **cheaper origination**, through AI-priced diligence (Idea 1) or privileged access as the client's finance function (Idea 2).

Its fatal-flaw test (will boards and auditors accept royalty-for-fees?) is cheap and can run while employed.
