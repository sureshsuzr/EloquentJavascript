# 12 · Cash, collections and working-capital outcomes (Track A)

Stage 1, round 2 · 4 Nov 2026 · Lens: the outcome easiest to measure in finance is cash. Can an outcome-priced AR/AP service be the wedge into the AI-native full finance function, and does the data it generates support a financing business underwritten with the founder's statistics edge?

## Summary

- **Collections are the best outcome to price, and the weakest business to sell on their own.** Cash collected and DSO are measurable to the dollar and day, and they're baselined from the client's ledger. But Australian SMEs are paid only about 6.6 days late on average (Xero Small Business Insights, Dec 2025 quarter) [verified: Xero via search], and AR software is consolidating: Sidetrade bought ezyCollect in Oct 2025 (1,100 clients, A$19bn of receivables, ~A$14M revenue) [verified: dealroom/techleap news feeds]. HighRadius now sells "$0 subscription, pay a fraction of realised gains" pricing [verified: Business Wire, Feb 2026]. Digits launched outcome pricing for accounting firms in Apr 2026 [verified: Accounting Today]. **Outcome pricing on its own is no longer a moat.**
- **The money is in the balance sheet, not the fee.** An agency that runs a client's ledger, AR and AP holds the best underwriting data there is: verified, real-time, dilution-visible receivables. That is what Waddle sold to Xero (A$31M upfront, up to A$80M) [verified: techpartner.news, smallcaps], but run as the operator, not as a data pipe. Specialist invoice financiers show the economics are real but modest. Earlypay's invoice finance funds-in-use were A$118.6M in FY25, down 10% [verified: Earlypay FY25 via search]. Butn's non-recoverable write-offs were 0.3% of originations, but its funding cost was ~10% [verified: Butn FY25 ASX; Banking Day].
- **Where it wins: approved invoices owed by strong debtors who pay slowly.** That points to the resources and energy supply chain. BHP's standard terms are 60 days [verified: BHP supplier guidance]. Labour-hire and maintenance contractors pay staff weekly [verified: TSBE/OptiPay]. Big-business payment times worsened to 64 days at the 95th percentile in H1 2025, and only 83 entities (2.3%) qualify as "fast payers" [verified: Dynamic Business, citing the Payment Times Reporting Regulator].

**Top pick: C, "Contractor Cash Desk".** It is an AI finance function for mining-services and renewable-build contractors, priced on days-to-cash and close quality, with a receivables-finance arm that funds only invoices already approved in the tier-1 debtor's portal.

## Idea A: Outcome-priced AR/AP agency as the wedge into the full finance function ("Cashflow-First Finance")

**One-liner.** Mid-market B2B companies (A$10–150M revenue) pay a base fee for close, BAS and reconciliations, plus an outcome fee on cash. One fee basis is days of DSO cut against a trailing-12-month ledger baseline. Another is cash collected on invoices more than 30 days overdue. A close-by-day-N SLA carries fee credits if missed. Over time the agency expands into the full finance function.

**Why now (2026–28).** Agentic AI can now do the dull middle of AR: triaging debtor emails, extracting promises to pay, cash application, and supplier-portal submission. Tesorio shipped a Supplier Portals Agent in 2025 [verified: PYMNTS]. That makes AR the first finance task where an agency can deliver at about 30% of the labour cost. The Payment Times Reporting Scheme is shining a light on slow large payers [verified]. Mid-market CFO and AR-clerk wage inflation helps the case.

**Why Australia / Melbourne.** Xero and MYOB penetration means ledgers are cloud-accessible from day one. Melbourne has a deep mid-market B2B base (manufacturing, wholesale, logistics). **Handicap:** the AU market is small and the incumbents (ezyCollect/Sidetrade, Chaser, CreditorWatch) are local.

**Unfair inputs.** He is a CFO in his current role, so he knows what a good close looks like. Statistics gives him baseline and counterfactual design: DSO is seasonal and mix-driven, so a naive baseline invites disputes, and he can build one that doesn't. CS/AI lets him build the agents. Fulfilio and LocalAgentFinder show he can do services and marketplace operations. **Gap:** a chartered-accountant operator and head of delivery (ex-Big 4 or mid-tier outsourcing manager, registered tax/BAS agent) who will run 20–40 staff.

**Market and model.** Australia has roughly 20,000–30,000 businesses with A$10–150M turnover [estimate]. The average ezyCollect client carries about A$17M of receivables (A$19bn across 1,100 clients) [derived from verified figures]. **Example client:** A$30M revenue, DSO 55. Cutting DSO by 10 days releases ~A$0.82M of cash once, worth ~A$80k a year at a 10% cost of capital. The outcome fee is 25–35% of that, ~A$25k a year. The finance function replaces 2.5–4 FTEs (A$300–450k loaded) at a price of A$150–220k [estimate]. 100 clients gives ~A$18M revenue; at a 30–40% EBITDA target with AI, that is A$5–7M EBITDA [estimate]. **Comparables:** HighRadius (US$3.1bn in 2021; outcome-priced since 2026) [verified]; Tesorio (US$17M Series B, 2022) [verified]; ezyCollect (sold to Sidetrade) [verified]; Digits [verified].

**Capital and structure.** Self-funded at A$0.3–0.5M while employed, then a seed round of A$1.5–3M. Founder keeps 55–65%.

**Floor and failure mode.** Recurring service contracts that are saleable to a mid-tier accounting firm at 0.8–1.5x revenue. **Failure mode:** clients churn after the one-off DSO gain ("you fixed it, now we'll keep the software"), or price competition from offshore BPO plus AI. P(total loss) ~20%.

**Outcome distribution (10 yrs, founder equity).** Loss 20% (<A$0.3M). Modest 42% (~A$1.5M). Good 28% (~A$7M). Great 10% (~A$25M). **EV ≈ A$5.1M. Median ≈ A$1.5–2M. P(positive) ≈ 42%.**

**Cash generation.** Cash-positive in months 12–20 at about 15 clients. It can fund its own organic growth; acquiring books of clients would need outside capital.

**AI leverage.** High. Agents do cash application, chasing and reconciliation, and model gains go straight to margin. **But** better models also lower the barrier to entry, and clients may do it in-house with Xero-native agents. The outcome stays measurable, but the moat erodes.

**Job satisfaction.** Building a delivery machine and a playbook for "good finance". Plenty of client contact. Moderate intellectual challenge once built.

**Key risks.** (1) Generic positioning in a crowded field. (2) Outcome-fee disputes over the baseline. (3) Thin AU mid-market willingness to pay for outcome fees on top of a base fee.

**90-day test (can start while employed).** Sign 3 paying pilot clients through his network at ≥A$8k a month base, with a written DSO baseline. **Kill** if fewer than 2 of 10 qualified CFO conversations will sign, or the gross margin model can't reach 55%.

## Idea B (bold): The operator-lender, a finance function that funds its own clients' receivables

**One-liner.** Same agency as A, plus a receivables-finance facility offered only to agency clients. The agency issues and chases the invoices, so it sees the debtor's history, credit notes and disputes in real time. The client pays a discount fee (1.5–2.5% for 30–60 days), and the outcome priced is cash in days, not weeks. The agency is the origination and servicing engine; the loan book is the profit pool.

**Why now.** Private credit is eager for short-dated, data-rich SME assets. Butn signed a A$100M syndicated facility with Northleaf (Jul 2025) and an MoU with a global bank for supply-chain finance (Apr 2026) [verified: Butn ASX via search]. The specialist incumbents look stuck: Earlypay's invoice finance book is shrinking, and Solvar took a 19.9% stake amid change-of-control talks [verified]. Bank appetite for small facilities remains poor.

**Why Australia.** Business-purpose credit generally sits outside the National Credit Code [estimate: standard reading of NCCP; confirm with counsel]. That keeps licensing light compared with consumer credit. The PPSA lets you register over receivables. Under PPSA s81, anti-assignment clauses are generally ineffective for accounts [unverified; legal check needed]. **Handicaps:** a small market and an ASIC focus on private-credit conduct.

**Unfair inputs.** This is the founder's strongest edge: pricing under uncertainty. Each invoice is scored for probability of payment, expected days and dilution, and the agency's own servicing improves the result. It is Fulfilio's physical-capacity arbitrage applied to balance sheets. **Gaps:** a credit and treasury co-founder (ex-Scottish Pacific / Earlypay / bank workout) and a warehouse funder relationship.

**Market and model.** Lending book of A$40–80M by year 6 [estimate]. Gross yield 14–18%, funding costs 8–10% (Butn's funding cost ~10%) [verified], losses 0.3–1.0%, plus servicing. Net margin is 4–6% of book: A$2.5–4M a year on a A$60M book. That sits on top of agency EBITDA, and cross-sell lowers client acquisition cost for both businesses. **Comparables:** Waddle (data-led, sold to Xero for up to A$80M) [verified]; Earlypay; Butn (market cap down sharply; share price A$0.008 at one point) [unverified as to date]. **Lesson from comparables:** standalone invoice financiers trade at low multiples. The value is in the integrated client relationship.

**Capital and structure.** Opco equity as in A, plus a lending SPV. The warehouse is 85–90% senior debt, with a 10–15% first loss of A$4–8M from family offices or a mezzanine fund. Founder ownership is ~45% of the opco and a promote or carry on the SPV.

**Floor and failure mode.** The receivables self-liquidate in 30–90 days, so the book can be run off in about 6 months and capital is largely recoverable. **Failure mode:** a fraud or a concentrated debtor default in a recession eats the first-loss layer. Fake invoices are the classic invoice-finance killer; running the client's ledger mitigates this but doesn't remove it. P(total loss) ~25%.

**Outcome distribution.** Loss 25%. Modest 37% (~A$1.8M). Good 27% (~A$9M). Great 11% (~A$30M). **EV ≈ A$6.4M. Median ≈ A$1.8–2.5M. P(positive) ≈ 45%.**

**Cash generation.** The lender is cash-positive at a ~A$15M book. The opco breaks even as in A. Growth needs outside first-loss capital.

**AI leverage.** Strong and compounding. Every serviced invoice is a labelled credit outcome, so the model improves with each client, and better models reduce fraud and dilution.

**Job satisfaction.** Very high for this founder: pricing risk, structuring capital, building data.

**Key risks.** (1) A fraud or concentration loss early on. (2) Funding cost: warehouse spreads widen in a credit cycle. (3) A conflict of interest, since the agency runs the books of the borrower it lends to. That needs governance and disclosure.

**90-day test.** Get indicative terms from 2 warehouse lenders. Back-test a default and dilution model on 3 years of anonymised ledgers from 5 friendly companies. **Kill** if indicative senior debt costs more than 11%, or if modelled dilution exceeds 3%.

## Idea C (top pick): Contractor Cash Desk, a finance function plus approved-invoice finance for resources and energy contractors

**One-liner.** Mining-services and renewable-construction contractors with A$10–200M revenue pay for an AI-native finance function: close, job costing, BAS, payroll, and the hard part, **invoice-to-portal-to-cash**. The hard part covers timesheet reconciliation, purchase-order matching, uploads to the miners' Ariba/Coupa portals, progress claims under the Security of Payment (SOP) Acts, and chasing retentions. Typical clients are labour hire, shutdown maintenance, haulage, drilling, electrical, and civil and BESS/solar EPC subcontractors.

- **Priced outcomes:** days from work done to cash; % of invoices approved first time; close by day 5.
- **Financing arm:** pays 90–95% of **invoices already approved in the tier-1 debtor's portal**. Credit risk then sits with BHP/Rio/Fortescue, a CIS-backed project SPV or a large EPC, not the SME.

**Why now (2026–28).**
- Tier-1 terms of ~60 days [verified: BHP] versus weekly payroll [verified: TSBE/OptiPay].
- Payment times are lengthening: 64 days at the 95th percentile in H1 2025 [verified: PTRR via Dynamic Business].
- A big renewables build-out: CIS tenders, REZs, Victoria's SEC and offshore wind [per brief].
- Construction insolvencies remain near records, ~3,400+ a year [verified], and that cascade comes through working-capital failure.
- Portal-submission agents just became feasible [verified: Tesorio supplier-portal agent, 2025].

**Why Australia / Melbourne.** Australia's resources and renewables supply chain is world-scale and dominated by a handful of strong, slow-paying debtors. That is the ideal approved-payables structure, and it is how the intersection in the brief becomes concrete. **Handicap:** mining services are concentrated in WA and Queensland, so Melbourne is a distance problem. It is partly offset by Victorian renewables and a Perth-based hire. Delivery is remote anyway.

**Unfair inputs.** All five edges apply:
- **Statistics:** debtor-specific payment-time distributions and dilution modelling.
- **CFO:** job-costing and WIP close, the hard part of contractor accounting.
- **CS/AI:** the portal and timesheet agents.
- **Marketplace:** a network effect, since many suppliers are paid by the same few debtors and per-debtor approval behaviour is learned once and reused.
- **Logistics and physical operations:** Fulfilio credibility with blue-collar operators.

**Gaps:** a co-founder from mining-services finance (ex-CFO of a contractor or ex-Scottish Pacific/Westpac trade finance in Perth) and a construction-law adviser for SOP and set-off.

**Market and model.**
- **Market:** several thousand mining-services and renewable-construction SMEs [estimate; METS sector size needs verifying]. Mining supply terms run 60–75 days [verified: TSBE].
- **Agency:** A$120–250k per client per year [estimate].
- **Finance:** the approved-invoice book turns ~6–8 times a year. Discount of 1.2–1.8% per 30 days (cheaper than generic factoring because the debtor is investment-grade), funded at 7–9%. Net margin ~4–5%; losses should sit well below Butn's 0.3%-of-originations figure if only approved invoices are funded [estimate].
- **Scale:** 60 agency clients (~A$10M revenue) plus a A$50M average book (~A$2.5M net margin). EBITDA ~A$5–6M by year 7 [estimate].
- **Strategic exits:** a bank (supply-chain finance distribution), a contractor-ERP vendor, or Sidetrade/HighRadius buying vertical capability.

**Capital and structure.** Bootstrap the agency while employed (A$0.3M). Then a seed round of A$2–3M from resources-linked family offices (WA has many). The lending SPV uses a senior warehouse plus 10% first loss; approved-invoice risk on tier-1 debtors should command a cheaper warehouse than generic SME factoring [estimate]. Alternatively, start **off balance sheet**: refer approved invoices to a bank SCF programme for a fee, and keep the book only once loss data proves the case. Founder ~50%.

**Floor and failure mode.** Contracted recurring service revenue. A self-liquidating book whose credit risk sits on tier-1 debtors. Sticky clients, because the agency becomes their portal and payroll operator. **Failure modes:**
1. A commodity downturn (lithium 2024-style) cuts contractor volumes and causes client failures, but the receivables owed by miners still pay.
2. Disputed or set-off progress claims in construction. Mitigation: fund only portal-approved mining invoices, and fund construction only after SOP adjudication or certification.
3. Debtors launch their own SCF programmes. BHP has had its own supplier payment policies [verified].

P(total loss) ~22%.

**Outcome distribution.** Loss 22%. Modest 35% (~A$1.8M). Good 30% (~A$9M). Great 13% (~A$30M, a vertical champion sold to a bank or software strategic). **EV ≈ A$7.2M. Median ≈ A$2–3M. P(positive) ≈ 50%.**

**Cash generation.** Agency is cash-positive in months 12–18. Lending fee income from month 9 if run as referral, positive from year 2 on balance sheet. Largely self-funding apart from the first-loss capital.

**AI leverage.** The outcome is measurable to the day, because portal approval and payment timestamps come from the debtor's own system. Agents get better at portal formats and timesheet matching. Per-debtor payment curves get sharper with every client. **Stronger as models improve**, and the domain specificity protects margin from horizontal tools.

**Job satisfaction.** High. Real operators, real assets, and a pricing and underwriting puzzle. It echoes Fulfilio: arbitraging a mismatch (weekly wages against 60-day terms) with data.

**Key risks.**
1. Sector concentration and the commodity cycle.
2. Debtor-side resistance or own programmes, plus legal complexity: SOP, set-off, anti-assignment.
3. Distance from WA/Qld clients, and the need for a co-founder with sector credibility.

**90-day test (while employed).**
- 20 interviews with mining-services and renewable-construction CFOs and owners, sourced through METS networks and the Victorian renewables supply chain.
- Collect 12 months of invoice-to-cash data from 5 of them.
- **Pass if:** median days from work done to cash ≥55; ≥30% of delay is administrative (portal rejections, missing POs, late timesheets) rather than terms; ≥3 owners commit to a paid pilot at ≥A$6k a month; one bank SCF or warehouse lender gives indicative appetite for approved tier-1 receivables at ≤9%.
- **Kill if:** median days to cash is <40, or approved invoices are already financed cheaply through debtor programmes.

## Idea D: AP payment integrity plus a dynamic-discounting fund ("PayRight")

**One-liner.** Mid-market and ASX-listed companies (including juniors and project SPVs) pay a share of savings: duplicate and erroneous payments recovered, payment-redirection fraud prevented (a verified-bank-detail layer), and early-payment discounts captured. The firm's own fund pays suppliers early in exchange for the discount, earning 12–20% annualised on short-dated, buyer-approved payables.

**Why now.** Payment-redirection and business email compromise losses are rising [unverified: needs ACSC/Scamwatch figures]. Payments licensing is being modernised: the Tranche 1 exposure draft of Mar 2026 brings "payment products" into the AFSL regime [verified: Ashurst/GTLaw]. **The design must not hold client funds**, or it becomes a licensed payment service provider (PSP).

**Why Australia.** Weak. Eftsure and other local players already sell AP fraud controls [unverified scale]. No location edge.

**Unfair inputs.** Statistics for anomaly detection; a CFO's credibility with other CFOs. **Gap:** AP/treasury sales.

**Market and model.** Recovery audits are a niche; savings-share of 20–35% is typical [estimate]. The discounting fund needs supplier uptake, which is historically low in AU. A$3–6M revenue at maturity [estimate].

**Capital.** Light for the audit; the fund needs A$5–20M. Founder ~60%.

**Floor and failure mode.** Audit fees are episodic, not recurring. Fraud prevention is a SaaS-like feature competing with banks' confirmation-of-payee. P(total loss) ~30%.

**Outcome distribution.** Loss 30%. Modest 45% (~A$1M). Good 20% (~A$5M). Great 5% (~A$15M). **EV ≈ A$2.2M. P(positive) ≈ 25%.**

**Cash generation.** Fast (month 6), but small.

**AI leverage.** Good for detection, but banks and ERPs will build it in.

**Job satisfaction.** Moderate.

**Key risks.** Episodic revenue; bank features commoditise it; licensing scope.

**90-day test.** Run free audits for 5 companies. **Pass** if recoverable duplicates exceed 0.05% of AP spend in at least 3 of them.

**Role.** Best used as a **module inside A, B or C**, not a company.

## Cross-cutting regulatory notes

- **Credit:** business-purpose lending is mostly outside the National Credit Code [estimate: confirm]. The ACL/ASIC Act unconscionability provisions and the unfair contract terms regime apply to small-business contracts. Raising first-loss capital from wholesale investors needs an AFSL or authorised-representative arrangement for the fund.
- **Collections conduct:** the ACCC/ASIC debt collection guideline (2021 edition is the latest I could confirm) is written for individual debtors but "relevant" to business debts [verified: ABA submission citing the guideline]. B2B-only scope keeps exposure low. Avoid sole traders, or treat them as consumers.
- **Payments:** under the payments modernisation drafts (Tranche 1a Oct 2025; Tranche 1 exposure draft Mar 2026) [verified], **never hold or route client money**. Initiate payments from the client's own bank under dual approval, or use a licensed PSP partner.
- **Tax:** BAS/tax work requires Tax Practitioners Board registration.
- **Privacy:** the Privacy Act 2024 amendments and contracts limit pooling client data. Get explicit consent for cross-client debtor models.

## Ranking and top pick

1. **C: Contractor Cash Desk.** EV ≈ A$7.2M, P(positive) ≈ 50%. It is the best floor (tier-1 debtor risk, self-liquidating book, sticky operational lock-in), it is the brief's intersection made concrete, and it uses all five founder edges.
2. **B: Operator-lender (generalist).** EV ≈ A$6.4M, P(positive) ≈ 45%. The same thesis without the debtor-quality advantage. Fallback if C's interviews fail.
3. **A: Outcome-priced AR wedge.** EV ≈ A$5.1M, P(positive) ≈ 42%. The safest start, but generic. **Treat it as the operating chassis inside C or B, not as the strategy.**
4. **D: AP integrity plus discounting fund.** EV ≈ A$2.2M, P(positive) ≈ 25%. A module, not a company.

**Calibration caveat.** These stage-1 estimates use ~10–13% "great" probabilities. With round-1 stage-2 base rates (~5–6%), each EV falls by ~A$1.5–2M and P(positive) by ~5 points. On that basis, C and B remain ahead of staying only if the 90-day tests pass.

**Main conclusion for the founder:** the AI finance agency is worth building only as the origination and servicing engine for a working-capital balance sheet, in a vertical where the debtors are strong. Outcome pricing on its own is now table stakes, not an edge.
