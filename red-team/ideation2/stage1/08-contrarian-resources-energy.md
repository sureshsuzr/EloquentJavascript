# Stage 1, Lens 08: Contrarian Wildcard (Resources × Energy × AI)

4 Nov 2026. Track B lens, read against the 4 Nov correction: I score Track B ideas and the Track A × B intersection on the same rubric. 16 web searches. Labels: **[verified: source]**, **[estimate]**, **[unverified]**. "Founder equity" means the founder's share of equity value in year 10, pre-tax, in A$. P(positive) means ending ahead of staying employed (~A$3.0–3.5M over 10 years), counting the modest salary the business pays.

## What changed my mind while researching

1. **Merchant battery revenue has collapsed.** NEM battery revenue fell to A$29k/MW/yr in May 2026, the lowest since Modo's index began in July 2022. It recovered to A$44k in July but was still 72% below July 2025. Winter 2026 averaged A$42k/MW/yr, down 76% year on year. Grid-scale fleet: 8.8 GW (2.6x a year earlier), plus ~8.8 GW of home batteries. FCAS is "structurally saturated" at ~9% of revenue **[verified: Modo Energy NEM revenue notes, Feb–Jul 2026; BNEF via ESS News, 20 Aug 2026]**. Battery-trading software is also crowded: OptiGrid (Vena 408 MW, Ampyr), Habitat (Octopus Australia), Tesla Autobidder, Fluence and Evergen **[verified: Energy-Storage.news, Sep 2026]**. A "battery-trading desk" pitched as software is therefore dead. The only interesting version left is the contrarian *asset* play (Idea B).
2. **AI exploration-for-royalty already exists in Australia.** Earth AI drills its own AI targets and takes perpetual royalties: US$20M Series B in Jan 2025, palladium with Legacy Minerals and gold at Willow Glen NSW **[verified: Mining.com, TechCrunch Mar 2025]**. It is also binary, so it fails the floor test. Dropped.
3. **The southern gas shortfall moved out again,** to peak-day risk from 2029 and a supply need from ~2030. AEMO credits batteries and electrification **[verified: AEMO 2026 GSOO media release; Bloomberg 26 Mar 2026]**. This weakens the urgency of process-heat electrification (Idea D), but does not kill it.
4. **The CMPTI is law.** It is a 10% refundable, uncapped offset on eligible processing costs (labour, reagents, utilities; not capex, feedstock or financing) for 2027–28 to 2039–40, with up to 10 years per registration and annual compliance **[verified: ATO, industry.gov.au]**. This is a new, recurring, measurement-heavy compliance stream that lands squarely on the Track A × B intersection.

## The best 4

### A. "Assay": an outcome-priced AI finance function for ASX resources companies and energy SPVs, with incentive monetisation (intersection; **top pick**)

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| One-liner | An AI-native agency runs the whole finance and compliance function for ASX junior explorers, developers, processors and renewable project SPVs: close, AP/AR, Appendix 5B quarterlies, half-year review and audit files, lender covenant reporting, BAS and payroll. Clients pay a **fixed fee per outcome** (5B lodged on time and board-ready by day 10, a clean audit with no material adjustments, covenant certificates on time) plus a **contingent fee on incentives recovered**: R&DTI, the Junior Minerals Exploration Incentive (JMEI), CMPTI from 2027–28, and HPTI where relevant. Later, advance the incentive receivable, financed off-balance-sheet with a partner. |
| Why now (2026–28) | CMPTI claims start in 2027–28, with an annual registration and compliance cycle. Every processor needs cost allocation to "eligible processing activity", which is a measurement problem **[verified: ATO]**. AI now makes reconciliations, 5B preparation and audit files cheap to produce. The junior sector is cash-starved, so it wants lower fixed costs and pay-on-result. R&D finance has grown fast: Radium Capital passed A$1B of cumulative advances to more than 1,000 companies by Nov 2025 and planned A$400M of lending in 12 months **[verified: Capital Brief / FinTech Australia]**. That proves people will pay for incentive-receivable finance. |
| Why Australia / Melbourne | It is an Australia-only regulatory product (ASX 5B, JMEI, CMPTI), so offshore AI players can't easily replicate it. **Handicap:** the junior-miner corporate-services cluster is in Perth (company secretaries, outsourced CFOs such as Integrated CFO Solutions and Eleven Corporate) **[verified: company sites, search results]**. Melbourne is strong for renewable SPVs and their sponsors (Vic SEC, offshore wind, the CIS pipeline). Plan for a Perth partner or acqui-hire. |
| Unfair inputs | This is the founder's current job (a CFO running finance ops), plus CS/AI to build the automation, statistics to defend cost allocations to the ATO, and a marketplace background for distribution. **Gaps:** a mining-tax specialist (an ex-Big 4 R&DTI/CMPTI director) as co-founder or early partner, and a Perth-based company secretary partner. |
| Market & model | ~600 ASX exploration companies **[unverified: Mining News, undated ASX profile]**. Typical outsourced finance plus company secretarial costs ~A$80–180k/yr per junior **[estimate]**, so ~A$50–100M/yr across explorers alone, plus producers, processors and SPVs. Incentive layer: a processor with A$150M of eligible opex earns a ~A$15M/yr CMPTI offset. A 3–5% success fee is A$0.45–0.75M per client per year **[estimate]**. Target: 60 juniors at A$110k + 8 incentive clients at A$400k = ~A$10M revenue by year 5, at a 30–40% EBITDA margin with an AI-leveraged team of ~25 **[estimate]**. Comparables: Radium (incentive finance), Perth outsourced-CFO firms, and Big 4 R&D practices (10–20% contingent fees are common for R&DTI **[unverified]**). |
| Capital & structure | Bootstrap-able: A$0.3–0.6M of founder capital, or a small angel round from mining financiers. The receivable-finance layer uses a partner's balance sheet (revenue share) or a warehouse facility in year 3+. **Founder ownership 60–80%.** |
| Floor and failure mode | Recurring service contracts, sticky because switching mid-audit is painful. If the AI edge disappoints, it is still a profitable boutique (A$2–4M revenue) that can be sold to a Perth firm or a Big 4/mid-tier. Failure modes: a junior cash crunch (commodity cycle) causes churn and bad debts; CMPTI claims get contested; professional-liability exposure. **P(total loss) ~8%.** |
| Outcome distribution (10 yrs) | Total loss 8% (A$0) · Modest 42% (A$1.5M: a boutique sold at ~1x revenue) · Good 40% (A$7M: A$10M revenue at 6–8x EBITDA, ~70% owned) · Great 10% (A$25M: a national resources and energy finance-ops platform plus a finance book). **EV ≈ A$5.9M; median ≈ A$3M; P(positive) ≈ 50%.** Salary of A$150–250k from year 2 adds ~A$1.5M. |
| Cash generation | Cash-positive in month 9–15 (services are paid monthly). Self-funds growth; the finance book is the only part that needs outside capital. |
| AI leverage | AI closes the books, drafts 5Bs and audit files, and classifies every cost line to CMPTI/R&DTI eligibility with an audit trail. That is the "outcome" the ATO tests. It gets stronger as models improve: margin rises and claims become more defensible. Statistics price the receivable (probability of ATO adjustment, timing). |
| Job satisfaction | High on building and selling, with real exposure to the resources sector. Risk: compliance work can feel like an accounting practice if the product layer stalls. |
| Key risks | (1) Perth incumbents and Big 4 bundle AI cheaply and defend relationships; (2) the junior funding cycle (2023–24-style droughts) drives churn; (3) liability for CMPTI/R&DTI errors (needs PI insurance and a tax-agent registration, via the partner). |
| 90-day test (while employed, subject to employer conflict policy) | Interview 20 junior CFOs or company secretaries and 5 processor CFOs (Alpha HPA-type, rare-earths and lithium refiners). **Proceed** if ≥5 sign LOIs at ≥A$6k/month for outcome-priced service and ≥2 processors agree to a paid CMPTI eligibility diagnostic (≥A$25k). Build the 5B/close automation on 3 public juniors' data as a demo. **Kill** if fewer than 3 LOIs, or processors say "our Big 4 auditor does it". |

### B. "Trough Capital": buy merchant batteries at the bottom and trade them in-house (bold, contrarian)

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| One-liner | Buy or complete 5–50 MW merchant batteries (distribution-connected, stalled projects, or developer sell-downs) at prices that reflect 2026's trough revenue. Run them with an in-house AI trading desk. Once there is a 2-year track record, sell revenue floors and tolls to smaller owners (councils, C&I, community batteries) as a second line. Investors pay through a fund structure. The outcome priced is $/MW/yr revenue against the floor the founder underwrites. |
| Why now (2026–28) | Revenue is down 72–76% year on year **[verified: Modo]**, so sellers' expectations are reset. Coal exits are clustered in 2027–29 (Yallourn ~2028, Eraring extended **[unverified dates]**). The southern gas peak-day shortfall arrives from 2029 **[verified: AEMO 2026 GSOO]**. This is a classic "buy capacity when nobody wants it" trade, the Fulfilio idea (physical capacity arbitrage) in a new asset class. |
| Why Australia / Melbourne | The NEM is the world's most volatile, data-transparent wholesale market (5-minute settlement, public bids). Victoria has the steepest coal-exit exposure. Melbourne is fine. |
| Unfair inputs | Statistics and forecasting under fat tails, CS/AI, and CFO structuring of floor/toll contracts. **Gaps:** an experienced NEM trader or registered-participant operator (ex-Shell Energy, Snowy, Akaysha) as co-founder, and an infrastructure fund partner for equity. |
| Market & model | Arbitrage spreads have fallen sharply (BNEF: Q2 2026 intraday arbitrage down 79–84% year on year) **[verified: ESS News]**. Battery capex has also fallen. A 20 MW/80 MWh asset costs ~A$25–35M **[estimate]**. At A$60–90k/MW/yr through-cycle revenue, it earns A$1.2–1.8M/yr. Buy at ≤A$1M/MW and it yields 7–10% unlevered; at 2025 prices it would not. Floors for third parties: fee 15–25% of upside above the floor **[estimate]**; precedent: Tesla's revenue floor for Genex Bouldercombe, ZEN and Pacific Green tolls **[verified: Energy-Storage.news, Modo]**. |
| Capital & structure | A$50–150M fund or club deal with super/infra investors, plus a CEFC co-investment angle. Founder takes GP economics: 1–2% management fee plus 15–20% carry, plus a 5–10% co-invest from personal capital (A$0.5M). **Founder ownership:** 30–50% of the GP/trading company and ~1% of assets. |
| Floor and failure mode | Hard assets with resale value, connection rights that are scarce, and recoverable capital. Failure mode: saturation keeps going (30 GW pipeline, 8.8 GW of home batteries), so revenue stays at A$30–50k/MW/yr; assets are written down; the fund raise fails. **P(total loss of founder capital and time) ~30%.** |
| Outcome distribution (10 yrs) | Loss 30% (A$0) · Modest 35% (A$1M) · Good 25% (A$8M: a A$300M AUM platform with carry) · Great 10% (A$30M: a named NEM storage and trading house). **EV ≈ A$5.4M; median ≈ A$1M; P(positive) ≈ 35%.** |
| Cash generation | Management fees from the first close (~year 2). Carry only after ~year 5–7. Not self-funding early. |
| AI leverage | Strong: bidding optimisation, degradation-aware dispatch, and pricing floors from simulated revenue distributions. It improves with models, but so does everyone else's bidding; the edge is in buying, not trading. |
| Job satisfaction | Very high intellectual intensity (markets, statistics, deals). It is a fund-raising job for 18 months. |
| Key risks | (1) Structural saturation (batteries cannibalise themselves); (2) first-time fund raise in a stressed private-markets vintage; (3) trading losses and operational outages (e.g. the Waratah transformer). |
| 90-day test | Build a backtested dispatch model on public AEMO data for 10 existing small batteries. Approach 10 owners or developers of 5–50 MW sites. **Proceed** if ≥2 assets are offered at ≤A$1.0M/MW with a credible ≥8% yield in the model, and 2 LPs give soft circles of ≥A$20M. **Kill** if asking prices still embed 2024–25 revenue. Can start while employed (it is research). |

### C. "Closure Co": buy mine-closure liabilities and rehabilitate to an outcome (bold, non-obvious)

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| One-liner | Take over small closed or care-and-maintenance mine sites, tailings dams and quarries in exchange for a cash payment from the owner below its book provision, plus release of its bonds. Deliver rehabilitation to regulator closure criteria using AI-measured outcomes (satellite and drone vegetation, erosion, groundwater). Monetise the residual: tailings reprocessing for critical minerals (processing costs may be CMPTI-eligible), solar or storage on disturbed land, and existing grid connections. The outcome priced is relinquishment achieved; the liability is underwritten statistically. |
| Why now (2026–28) | Queensland's estimated rehabilitation cost is ~A$14.3B (FY2025–26), 70% of it coal **[verified: IEEFA submission, Oct 2026, citing the FPS annual report; secondary]**. Queensland's Financial Provisioning Scheme is under review, and the surety guideline changed on 1 Oct 2025 **[verified: Qld Treasury]**. Banks and insurers are retreating from coal surety **[verified: McCullough Robertson 2023]**. Cobalt Blue notes that **liability ownership is the main barrier to reprocessing tailings** **[verified: Engineering News 2024]**, and that is precisely the barrier a liability buyer removes. In the US, environmental liability transfer is an established niche (firms buy contaminated sites with the remediation obligation) **[unverified: no specific firm checked]**. |
| Why Australia / Melbourne | Australia has the world's biggest per-capita mine-closure backlog and regulator-driven assurance. Victoria has legacy goldfields sites (e.g. former Benambra, a state-managed site) **[verified: resources.vic.gov.au]**. Melbourne is a mild handicap; the sites are in Qld, NSW and WA. |
| Unfair inputs | Pricing and underwriting under uncertainty (the core edge), CFO structuring of liability-transfer deals, AI and remote sensing to measure outcomes, and Fulfilio-style physical-asset arbitrage. **Gaps:** a mine-closure engineer or hydrogeologist (ex-Rio or BHP closure team) as co-founder, an environmental-surety insurer (Assetinsure wrote Australia's first mining rehabilitation insurance bond **[verified: Insurance Business]**), and a resources lawyer. |
| Market & model | A$14B in Qld alone, plus NSW, WA (MRF) and NT **[verified Qld; others estimate]**. Target the small end (A$2–30M liabilities), where majors' teams are absent. The deal earns the gap between the provision paid and the actual cost (aim for a 20–35% margin), plus residual value. Example: take A$10M of liability for an A$8M payment, rehabilitate for A$6M over 4 years, keep A$2M plus the land and connection option **[estimate]**. |
| Capital & structure | One SPV per site, with a surety from the insurer and equity from family offices or a resources fund. Founder in the opco with 40–60% and a promote on each SPV. A$0.3–0.5M of personal capital. |
| Floor and failure mode | Cash is paid upfront, so the opco is cash-funded. Downside: perpetual water treatment or legacy contamination that blows up the cost (the tail risk), and the regulator refusing to transfer the authority or to relinquish. Structure to cap liability per SPV (ring-fencing, insurance), but regulators may resist ring-fencing. **P(total loss) ~25%.** |
| Outcome distribution (10 yrs) | Loss 25% · Modest 30% (A$1.5M) · Good 35% (A$8M: 15–20 sites, a reprocessing JV) · Great 10% (A$30M: the default closure counterparty in Australia). **EV ≈ A$6.3M; median ≈ A$1.5–3M; P(positive) ≈ 40%.** |
| Cash generation | Positive cash from the first deal (upfront payment), but the cash is a liability. Economic profit comes at the milestones. It self-funds once 3–4 sites are running. |
| AI leverage | Very high and improving: satellite, LiDAR and hyperspectral monitoring turn "rehabilitation achieved" into a measurable, auditable outcome. Statistical cost-to-close models price each deal. Better models mean tighter pricing and lower monitoring cost per hectare. |
| Job satisfaction | Very high for a curious builder: a new asset class, field work, regulators and deals. Reputational and ESG upside. |
| Key risks | (1) Regulators won't accept transfer of liability to a new entity without heavy assurance; (2) an unknown contamination tail; (3) deal flow: majors prefer to keep closure in-house for reputational reasons. |
| 90-day test | Meet the Qld Mine Rehabilitation Commissioner's office, the Vic ERR, 2 surety insurers and 10 mid-tier or junior owners holding care-and-maintenance sites. **Proceed** if ≥2 owners will share a closure estimate and say they would pay ≥70% of the provision to transfer it, and 1 insurer will price surety in principle. **Kill** if regulators say transfer to a non-producer is unworkable. Can run while employed. |

### D. "HeatCo": process heat-as-a-service for Victorian food and beverage manufacturers

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| One-liner | Own and operate heat pumps, heat recovery and thermal storage at manufacturers' sites. Sell heat per GJ under 10–15 year contracts priced below the client's gas-equivalent cost, with a savings share. AI dispatches heat production into low-price (midday solar) intervals and stores it thermally. The outcome priced is GJ delivered and $ saved, verified by M&V. |
| Why now (2026–28) | VEU activity 44 (commercial and industrial heat pumps replacing gas boilers) was revised on 31 Mar 2026, and DEECA consulted on adding industrial-heat activities (closed 28 Apr 2026). The Large Energy User Electrification Support program funds feasibility studies up to A$60k for 10–100 TJ/yr gas users **[verified: ESC, energy.vic.gov.au]**. ARENA has funded Victorian demos (Hardwick 1 MWth heat pump, A$838k; Blackmores A$724k) **[verified: ARENA via Australian Manufacturing]**. The contrarian point: battery saturation keeps midday power cheap, and **thermal storage is the cheapest battery**. **Headwind:** the gas shortfall has been deferred to 2029+ **[verified: AEMO]**. |
| Why Australia / Melbourne | Victoria is the most gas-dependent manufacturing state, with a dense food and beverage belt (Ballarat, Kyneton, the SE suburbs). Melbourne is an advantage. |
| Unfair inputs | M&V statistics, CFO structuring of energy-as-a-service contracts, CS/AI dispatch, and operations. **Gap:** a thermal and refrigeration engineer co-founder. |
| Market & model | Unit economics for 1 MWth, ~6,000 h/yr: displaces ~25 TJ of gas, ~A$330k/yr at ~A$13/GJ **[estimate]**. At COP 3 and A$130/MWh it saves only ~A$70k/yr. With heat recovery (COP 4–5) and storage shifting to ~A$50/MWh midday power, it saves ~A$200–250k/yr. Capex A$1.5–2.5M **[estimate]**, so a 7–10 year payback; it works only with VEU certificates, ARENA/CEFC money and cheap debt. **Thin, site-specific margins.** |
| Capital & structure | Project-level CEFC or green-bank debt plus equity from an infrastructure partner. Founder 40–60% of the opco. |
| Floor and failure mode | Hard assets on contracts, but with single-site credit risk (manufacturers close) and re-deployment that is costly. **P(total loss) ~12%.** |
| Outcome distribution (10 yrs) | Loss 12% · Modest 45% (A$1.5M) · Good 33% (A$6M: ~40 sites) · Great 10% (A$20M). **EV ≈ A$4.7M; median ≈ A$1.5M; P(positive) ≈ 40%.** |
| Cash generation | Cash-positive per site from year 1, but growth needs project capital. Not self-funding for growth. |
| AI leverage | Moderate: dispatch optimisation and automated M&V. Improves slowly. |
| Job satisfaction | Engineering and sales grind; tangible. Lower ceiling. |
| Key risks | (1) Gas stays cheaper than expected (shortfall deferred), so savings evaporate; (2) manufacturer credit and closures; (3) site-by-site engineering costs kill scale. |
| 90-day test | Run 5 paid LEUES-funded feasibility studies (the grant pays). **Proceed** if ≥2 sites show ≥A$150k/yr savings at ≤A$2M capex and sign a heads of terms. Possible while employed only with a partner engineer. |

## Top pick and ranking

**Top pick: A, "Assay": an outcome-priced AI finance function for ASX resources companies and energy SPVs, with CMPTI/R&DTI/JMEI incentive monetisation.** It is the only idea here that satisfies the floor requirement *and* uses Australia's resources advantage *and* is the founder's day job. It also connects the corrected Track A to Track B. The CMPTI's 2027–28 start gives it a hard window.

Ranked:
1. **A. Assay** (EV A$5.9M, P(positive) ~50%): best floor, fastest cash, highest ownership.
2. **C. Closure Co** (EV A$6.3M, P(positive) ~40%): the most original and the strongest use of underwriting plus AI measurement. Highest EV, but there is regulator and tail risk. Its 90-day test is worth running in parallel with A's because it is cheap.
3. **B. Trough Capital** (EV A$5.4M, P(positive) ~35%): the right contrarian trade *if* assets can be bought at ≤A$1M/MW. It is a fund-raise business with a weak floor for the founder.
4. **D. HeatCo** (EV A$4.7M, P(positive) ~40%): solid but thin economics, gas-price dependent, and capital-hungry.

**One synthesis to consider:** A can be the cash engine and the deal-flow source for C. A finance function that sits inside dozens of juniors sees every care-and-maintenance site and its closure provision first.
