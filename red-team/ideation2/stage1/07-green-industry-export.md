## 1. What changed in 2025–26 (the facts the ideas rest on)

- **The Critical Minerals Production Tax Incentive (CMPTI) is law.** It passed Parliament on 11 Feb 2025. It is a **refundable, uncapped 10% offset on eligible processing costs** for 31 critical minerals, payable for 2027–28 to 2039–40 and for up to 10 years per project. Registration opens 1 Jul 2027 with DISR, and keeping it requires annual reporting [verified: ATO "critical minerals and hydrogen production incentives now law"; industry.gov.au/CMPTI]. The Budget costing is **~A$7B over a decade** [verified: KWM/GTLaw summaries of the 2024–25 Budget]. Feedstock, depreciation and financing are excluded, so the offset covers only part of a processor's costs [verified: KWM]. Draft guidance on the Community Benefit Principles that apply to FMIA support was released in Dec 2025 [verified: CPD submission to DISR, Feb 2026]. The result is a **new, recurring and measurable compliance claim on the Commonwealth, paid once a year in arrears.** It cannot be transferred (unlike US IRA credits) [estimate, based on its design as a refundable tax offset], so the only ways to monetise it early are borrowing against it or a structured advance.
- **The Critical Minerals Strategic Reserve (A$1.2B) has legal powers.** The EFIC Amendment (Strategic Reserve) passed on 31 Mar 2026. It lets Export Finance Australia sign fixed- or floating-price offtakes, trade forward offtakes and aggregate supply and demand [verified: Minister King media release; mining.com, 1 Apr 2026]. Its first focus is antimony, gallium and rare earths. Eligible projects need at least a pre-feasibility study and **≥A$20M of support** [verified: industry.gov.au CMSR page]. The minister says a floor price is "no doubt" an element [verified: Mining Weekly, 25 Mar 2026]. Industry has proposed a **price collar**: government pays below the floor and shares revenue above a ceiling [verified: S&P Global, Jan 2026]. No first offtake had been reported in my searches [unverified either way].
- **Japan already pays for floors.** On 10 Mar 2026 Lynas signed a 12-year deal with JARE (JOGMEC/Sojitz): at least 5,000 t/yr of NdPr at a **US$110/kg floor until 2038** [verified: Argus/TXF]. Mitsui signed an offtake-plus-funding letter of interest with VHM (Goschen, Victoria) for 40% of its heavy mineral concentrate [verified: Creamer Media, Jul 2025].
- **US–Australia framework (20 Oct 2025).** Each country is to invest ≥US$1B toward a pipeline of up to **US$8.5B**. It includes Alcoa–Sojitz gallium (up to US$200M of concessional equity with offtake rights), US$100M of Australian equity in Arafura Nolans, and **US EXIM letters of interest worth >US$2.2B** (Arafura, Northern Minerals, Sunrise) [verified: pm.gov.au; industry.gov.au].
- **Lithium is back, and capacity is stranded.** Albemarle idled Kemerton's last train in Feb 2026 and cancelled its expansion [verified: Mining Weekly, 12 Feb 2026]. Spodumene prices then roughly tripled from Dec 2025. Core Lithium restarted Finniss, MinRes is restarting Bald Hill, and PLS is weighing a Ngungaju restart [verified: Northern Miner; mining.com, 2026]. The country has idle refining assets, restarting mines and volatile prices at the same time.
- **Green iron is policy-led, not demand-led yet.** The Green Iron Investment Fund's ≥A$500M National Development Stream (applications opened 28 Oct 2025) [verified: business.gov.au]. POSCO's Port Hedland HBI plant was approved, and it runs on gas first [verified: GMK Center]. IEEFA (Mar 2026) found **no green-iron offtakes yet**, only HBI offtakes by traders [verified: IEEFA briefing note].
- **Certification is slow.** The Guarantee of Origin scheme went live in Nov 2025 with hydrogen first. Green metals come later [verified: CER, Nov 2025]. The date for the EU CBAM's financial obligation is in flux (1 Jan 2026 vs Feb 2027 under the omnibus) [verified: conflicting, Clayton Utz vs PwC].
- **Victorian offshore wind is slow.** The 2 GW auction RFP opened 26 Aug 2026, and **contracts are expected in 2028** [verified: Energy Global, 26 Aug 2026]. Port of Hastings construction can start in 2028 at best [verified: RenewEconomy].
- **The customer base for the Track A intersection:** ~**680 ASX explorers** reporting cash in Appendix 5B at Q1 2026 [verified: Austex/Noosa, Jul 2026], with a record A$13.0B of cash entering 2026 [verified: BDO].

**Implication for the lens.** The money is in three places, and none is a megaproject:
1. the **compliance and claim layer** around new production subsidies and government offtake;
2. **price-risk transfer** (floors and collars), which governments and Japanese buyers are now explicitly pricing;
3. **physical-capacity matching**: idle processing plant, restart volumes, and aggregation for the Reserve and allied buyers.

## 3. The four ideas

### Idea 1 (top pick): PTI Desk, an outcome-priced AI finance and incentive function for critical-minerals companies

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | Runs the full finance and compliance function for ASX juniors, developers and processors: 5B quarterlies, half-year and annual close to audit, AP/AR, payroll, BAS, R&D Tax Incentive, Junior Minerals Exploration Incentive, grant milestones, CMSR/EXIM/EFA lender reporting, and from FY28 **CMPTI registration, claims and annual reporting**. Clients pay a fixed base, plus outcome fees: 5B and close lodged by day N with no ASX query, audit with no adjustments, and a **% of incentive dollars secured and defended**. Phase 2 adds a **refund bridge**: a partner credit fund advances against the processor's audited CMPTI/R&D refund, and the Desk earns origination and servicing fees plus a carried share. |
| **Why now (2026–28)** | CMPTI registration opens 1 Jul 2027 with annual reporting obligations and new Community Benefit Principles. The first refunds arrive only after FY28 returns (late 2028–29) [estimate], creating a 6–18-month cash gap during ramp-up, when processors are most cash-starved. CMSR, EXIM and the US framework add lender-grade reporting. The 2026 lithium rebound puts restarts and new processors back into play. AI now makes a two-person team able to run a junior's close. |
| **Why Australia / Melbourne** | The incentive exists only in Australian law, so being in Australia is an advantage, not merely compatible. Melbourne hosts many ASX-listed junior head offices and their accountants, and Perth is a 4-hour flight [estimate]. Handicap: the processor cluster is in WA (Kwinana/Kemerton) and QLD/NT, so a Perth presence is needed by year 2. |
| **Unfair inputs** | A CFO who has run finance in a SaaS company, a statistician (claim estimation, audit sampling, variance), CS/AI (agentic close), and marketplace experience (bundling services). **Gaps:** (1) a resources-tax specialist (an ex-Big 4 mining-tax manager or R&D/CMPTI adviser) as co-founder or senior hire; (2) company-secretarial and ASX Listing Rules expertise; (3) for phase 2, a private-credit partner (e.g. an R&D-advance lender) [unverified that any would lend against CMPTI yet]. |
| **Market & model** | **Juniors:** ~680 explorers [verified]. If 300 are addressable, at A$120–240k/yr for full outsourced finance and company secretarial [estimate], the pool is A$36–72M/yr. **Processors and developers:** CMPTI ≈ A$700M/yr of offsets at maturity [verified total A$7B/10 yrs]. A 3–6% claim and defence fee [estimate; R&D contingency fees run 15–20% on small claims, verified only from adviser marketing pages] is a pool of **A$20–40M/yr**, concentrated in ~20–40 facilities [estimate]. **Refund bridge:** a processor with A$400M of eligible costs gets ~A$40M/yr. Advancing 70% for ~12 months at ~12–14% [estimate] generates ~A$3.5M of interest per client; the Desk takes 1–2% origination plus 20% carry. **Unit economics:** a junior client at A$15k/month with ~0.25 FTE plus AI gives a 50–60% gross margin [estimate]. **Comparables:** outsourced CFO/company-secretarial firms serving juniors (Perth boutiques, Automic, Boardroom) bill by hours, not outcomes [unverified on pricing]. R&D advisers bill 15–20% contingency [indicative]. |
| **Capital & structure** | Equity-light: A$0.3–0.5M of founder capital to reach about 15 clients, optionally A$1–2M of angel money. Phase 2 uses a **partner's balance sheet** (private credit or bank), not the Desk's. Founder keeps **60–80%** [estimate]. |
| **Floor and failure mode** | Recurring contracted service revenue and a client book saleable to an accounting group even at 1.0–1.5x revenue. Worst case: CMPTI uptake is thin (few processors reach production) and the business is "just" a good AI-native finance bureau for juniors, which still pays a salary. **P(total loss) ≈ 12%.** |
| **Outcome distribution (10 yrs)** | **Total loss 12%** (A$0); **modest 45%** (~A$1.5M: a A$3–6M-revenue bureau); **good 33%** (~A$6M: A$12–15M revenue, sold at 1.5–2x); **great 10%** (~A$25M: the default finance-and-incentive platform for Australian processing, plus a carried interest in a bridge-lending book). **EV ≈ A$5.2M; median ≈ A$2.5–3M; P(positive) ≈ 45%** [all estimates]. |
| **Cash generation** | Cash-positive at ~10–12 juniors (month 9–15). It funds growth from its own cash. CMPTI fees start in FY28 and are lumpy (annual claims). |
| **AI leverage** | Strong and improving. Agentic close, 5B drafting, invoice and contract extraction, and **tagging eligible processing costs line by line**, with a statistical audit trail that defends claims. Each model generation cuts the hours per client while outcome prices hold. Pooled anonymised cost data across clients becomes an underwriting asset for the bridge (and for Idea 2). |
| **Job satisfaction** | High on building and structuring, lower on the grind of the bureau. CFO-of-many work, site visits, Canberra/DISR policy work, and credit structuring. |
| **Key risks** | (1) Big 4 and mid-tier firms (KPMG, BDO, PwC already publish CMPTI material) dominate processor claims [verified: KPMG 2026 CMPTI PDF exists]. (2) Few processors reach production before 2030, so the CMPTI pool is back-loaded. (3) Liability for incorrect claims, which needs professional-indemnity cover and a registered tax agent. |
| **90-day test** | **Can start while employed** (no conflict with a SaaS employer; nights and weekends plus one contractor). Thresholds: (a) **3 paying juniors** at ≥A$8k/month on outcome terms; (b) **2 of 10** CMPTI-candidate processors or developers agree to a paid readiness review (≥A$25k); (c) **1 lender** gives an indicative term sheet for a refund advance. Kill if fewer than 2 juniors convert from 25 conversations, or all 10 processors say "our Big 4 auditor handles it". |

### Idea 2 (bold): Collar Capital, revenue floors and collars for critical-minerals producers

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | A specialist vehicle that writes **price floors or collars** to junior and mid-tier critical-minerals producers (rare earths, antimony, gallium, graphite, lithium restarts). The producer gets a bankable revenue floor that unlocks project debt or CMSR/EXIM support. The vehicle takes an **upside share above a ceiling** (a royalty-like participation) plus an upfront premium. Its risk is laid off partly to offtakers (Japanese buyers already pay for floors) and partly to the Reserve, or sits alongside it. The outcome priced is **realised price relative to a band**. |
| **Why now** | Floors are now the market norm for strategic minerals: the US MP Materials NdPr floor (Jul 2025) [unverified detail], JARE's US$110/kg floor with Lynas [verified], and the Australian Reserve committed to a floor element [verified]. Lenders want floors to finance juniors. The US stepping back from blanket floors [verified: mining.com, Jan 2026] leaves a gap for private capacity. 2026 lithium volatility shows how mispriced the tails are. |
| **Why Australia / Melbourne** | Australia is the allied-supply jurisdiction of choice (US framework, Japan JARE), and superannuation funds want transition exposure uncorrelated with equities. Melbourne is the super-fund capital (AustralianSuper, ART, UniSuper, HESTA). Handicap: commodity derivatives talent sits in London, Singapore and Perth. |
| **Unfair inputs** | Statistics (tail modelling of thin, manipulated markets is the core skill), CFO structuring, and capital-raising credibility. **Gaps:** a commodity-structuring co-founder (ex-Macquarie Commodities, Traxys or Orion), an AFSL/wholesale fund wrapper, and legal structuring (whether this is a derivative, an offtake or a stream). |
| **Market & model** | Pipeline: CMSR projects need ≥A$20M of support [verified]; EXIM LOIs >US$2.2B [verified]; US framework pipeline US$8.5B [verified]. Writing collars for 10–15 projects with A$30–100M notional each gives A$0.5–1B of notional exposure [estimate]. Economics: premium of 3–6% of notional per year, plus 10–25% upside share above the ceiling [estimate]. The GP earns 1.5% management fee plus 20% carry on a A$150–300M fund [estimate]. **Comparables:** Orion Resource Partners and Appian (streams and offtake finance), JARE (sovereign floor buyer), and government collars (the industry proposal in the CMSR) [verified for JARE/CMSR; the rest is general knowledge]. |
| **Capital & structure** | Phase 0: an advisory or "structuring agent" mandate for 2–3 producers negotiating floors with JARE, CMSR or EXIM (fee-based, no balance sheet). Phase 1: a A$150–300M closed-end fund (anchors: one super fund, one Japanese trading house, and possibly CEFC/NRF co-investment), GP owned 40–60% by the founder. Founder capital: A$0.5–1M into the GP and GP commitment. |
| **Floor and failure mode** | If the fund doesn't close, the founder has spent ~A$0.3–0.5M and 12–18 months, and keeps advisory fees. If it closes and tails are mispriced (as with prices in 2023–25), LP capital is lost and the GP's reputation goes with it. Founder P(total loss of the invested ~A$0.75M) **≈ 35%**, mostly from the fund not closing. |
| **Outcome distribution** | **Loss 35%**; **modest 30%** (~A$1M: an advisory boutique); **good 22%** (~A$8M: one fund raised with fees and carry); **great 13%** (~A$40M: a franchise across funds II and III, the allied-minerals price-risk house). **EV ≈ A$7.3M; median ≈ A$1M; P(positive) ≈ 30%** [estimates]. |
| **Cash generation** | Advisory fees from year 1. Management fees once the fund closes (year 2–3). Carry only after year 6–8. It cannot self-fund the balance-sheet business; the fund structure solves that. |
| **AI leverage** | Medium-high. Price-tail models for opaque markets (antimony, gallium) built from customs data, Chinese export-control signals and news flow. Per-project production-risk models using operator data (feeding from Idea 1). Better models raise the edge but don't remove the need for capital. |
| **Job satisfaction** | Very high for this founder: pricing under uncertainty, deal-making with governments and Japanese buyers, ambitious. |
| **Key risks** | (1) The government crowds it out: the CMSR's own collars set prices below commercial levels. (2) A first-time manager fails to raise in a niche asset class. (3) Correlated tail events (China floods or withholds supply) hit every position at once. |
| **90-day test** | Partly possible while employed (conversations only, with no fiduciary conflict). Thresholds: **≥5 of 15** juniors or developers say a floor is the binding constraint on financing and would pay ≥2%/yr; **≥1 super fund or trading house** expresses interest in a ≥A$25M anchor commitment; EFA confirms private co-participation is possible. Kill if the CMSR states it will provide floors directly to all eligible projects on concessional terms. |

### Idea 3: Offtake and toll desk ("Fulfilio for concentrates")

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | A physical-capacity arbitrage platform. It aggregates small-producer output (restart spodumene, mineral-sands HMC, antimony, rare-earth intermediates), books **toll-processing capacity** at under-used Australian plants, and sells back-to-back to Japanese, Korean and US buyers and to the CMSR's aggregation window. It earns a per-tonne margin and fees, **paid only on tonnes delivered to spec**. Trade finance and prepayments come from trading-house and EFA balance sheets, not the desk's. |
| **Why now** | Stranded processing capacity (Kemerton idled Feb 2026) [verified] while lithium prices tripled and mines restart [verified]. The CMSR is legally empowered to aggregate supply [verified]. Japanese houses are signing offtake-plus-funding with juniors (Mitsui–VHM) [verified]. The CMPTI makes Australian processing 10% cheaper from FY28 [verified], which improves tolling economics. |
| **Why Australia / Melbourne** | The resource and the plants are here, and so are Victoria's mineral-sands juniors (VHM Goschen, Astron Donald) [verified VHM; Astron unverified]. Handicap: WA is the physical centre. |
| **Unfair inputs** | Fulfilio was exactly this pattern (pooling spare physical capacity), plus statistics for grade and recovery variance and logistics. **Gaps:** a metallurgist or ex-trader co-founder with Japanese and Korean relationships; working-capital lines. |
| **Market & model** | Australian critical-mineral exports run to tens of billions of A$ a year [estimate]. The addressable "orphan" volume (small producers without offtake) is perhaps A$1–3B/yr [estimate]. A 1.5–3% margin on A$300M of throughput by year 6 is A$5–9M of gross profit [estimate]. **Comparables:** Traxys, Wogen, Glencore and Sojitz/Mitsui/Hanwa are the incumbents and potential partners. |
| **Capital & structure** | A$0.5–1M founder capital, then A$3–5M of equity plus an uncommitted borrowing-base facility. Founder 50–70%. |
| **Floor and failure mode** | Contracts and relationships have some resale value to a trader, but there are few hard assets. The failure mode is a counterparty or quality dispute on one cargo that wipes out the equity. **P(total loss) ≈ 30%.** |
| **Outcome distribution** | Loss 30%; modest 35% (~A$1.2M); good 25% (~A$7M); great 10% (~A$30M). **EV ≈ A$5.2M; median ≈ A$1.2M; P(positive) ≈ 35%.** |
| **Cash generation** | Deal by deal from year 1–2, but lumpy and cyclical. It can self-fund once it has 3–4 repeat flows. |
| **AI leverage** | Medium. Assay, spec and recovery prediction, freight optimisation, and counterparty risk scoring. Commodity trading is relationship-bound, so models help less than in Idea 1. |
| **Job satisfaction** | High: physical, international (Tokyo, Seoul), deal-driven. |
| **Key risks** | (1) Incumbent traders and the CMSR itself squeeze margins. (2) The price cycle (2023–25 style collapse). (3) Plant owners refuse third-party tolling (technical and liability concerns). |
| **90-day test** | Can start while employed (conversations only). **Thresholds:** 1 plant owner willing to quote a toll; 2 producers with unsold volume ≥10kt/yr signing LOIs; 1 Japanese or Korean buyer indicating price and spec. Kill if no plant owner will toll. |

### Idea 4: Product carbon passport, priced on the green premium captured

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **One-liner** | Product-level embedded-emissions measurement, reporting and verification for Australian exporters of aluminium, HBI and pellets, lithium hydroxide and nickel. It produces CBAM, Product GO and Japan/Korea buyer declarations. It charges a base fee plus a **share of the green premium or carbon cost avoided** on verified tonnes. |
| **Why now** | The GO scheme went live Nov 2025 (green metals later) [verified]. CBAM is moving to financial obligations (date contested) [verified, conflicting]. The Carbon Leakage Review recommended an Australian border adjustment starting with cement and expanding to steel and aluminium [verified via search summary]. |
| **Why Australia / Melbourne** | Australian renewable-powered processing is the source of the premium. Handicap: green-metals Product GO is years away, and Japan and Korea don't yet pay premiums at scale (IEEFA: no green-iron offtakes) [verified]. |
| **Unfair inputs** | Statistics for measurement and verification, and data pipelines. **Gaps:** an LCA engineer and an accredited verifier relationship. |
| **Market & model** | Narrow: Minviro raised only ~US$2.6M (2023) [verified: CB Insights], which suggests a small software market. Premium-share pricing works only once premiums exist. |
| **Capital & structure** | A$0.3–0.5M, founder 70%+. |
| **Floor and failure mode** | Becomes a consultancy. P(total loss) ≈ 25%. |
| **Outcome distribution** | Loss 25%; modest 45% (~A$1M); good 22% (~A$5M); great 8% (~A$20M). **EV ≈ A$3.2M; median ≈ A$1M; P(positive) ≈ 28%.** |
| **Cash generation** | Year 1–2 from consulting work. Premium share comes late (2029+). |
| **AI leverage** | High (data ingestion, process models), but it is a commoditising capability. |
| **Job satisfaction** | Moderate: technical and regulatory. |
| **Key risks** | Regulatory delay (CBAM omnibus, GO timing); Big 4 and Sphera/Minviro competition; no premiums emerge. |
| **90-day test** | 2 exporters paying ≥A$40k for a CBAM- or GO-ready passport pilot. **Better pursued as a module of Idea 1** (the same cost ledger feeds both the CMPTI claim and the emissions intensity). |

## 4. How the ideas fit together

Idea 1 is the wedge and the data layer. Running the books of juniors and processors yields line-item cost, production and cash data that nobody else holds in one place. That data is what makes the CMPTI refund bridge underwritable (Idea 1, phase 2), lets floors and collars be priced on production risk rather than price alone (Idea 2), and powers the emissions passport (Idea 4) almost for free. The staged path:

1. **2026–27, while employed:** sign the first junior clients and build CMPTI readiness reviews before registration opens on 1 Jul 2027.
2. **2027–28:** quit when the Desk reaches ~A$1.5M ARR (about 10 juniors plus 2 processors) [threshold estimate]. Launch the refund bridge with a credit partner once the first FY28 claims are lodged.
3. **2028–30:** if Idea 2's 90-day test passes, use the Desk's data and relationships to raise Collar Capital fund I. That raise is the bold upside on top of a cash-generating floor.
