# Founder-fit, satisfaction and first-10-customers: stage-2 evaluation

Lens 05. 4 Nov 2026. Australian English, AUD. 11 web searches. Labels: **[verified: source]**, **[estimate]**, **[unverified]**.

## Assumptions

All candidates are scored on the same basis.

- **Stay-employed baseline (B0).** A package of about A$500k a year creates **about A$3.25M of net value over 10 years** (midpoint of the A$3.0–3.5M figure from earlier rounds). That is the bar. **P(positive)** means P(founder's after-tax equity plus after-tax salary, minus capital, exceeds about A$3.25M).
- **Founder salary.** A$0 from the venture while he is still employed and testing (up to 12 months). A$150k from quitting until the business is cash-positive, then A$250k. That comes to about A$1.1–1.3M after tax over the decade. The salary covers only about a third of the bar, so equity has to supply the other A$2–2.2M after tax.
- **Tax.** Salary is taxed at marginal rates. Equity is taxed at exit under the current 50% CGT discount (about 23.5% effective). As a stress test I use about 30%, the reported indexation-plus-minimum proposal [unverified, as in round 1].
- **Dilution.** Services and asset-light businesses: 15–25% (co-founder plus ESOP), so the founder keeps about 60–70%. Capital-heavy businesses (royalty book, asset SPVs, closure SPVs): the founder ends with 20–40% of the operating company plus a promote.
- **Time.** Year-10 values are not discounted. Instead I score time to first revenue and time to 10 paying customers explicitly. Every 6 months he is off salary without revenue costs about A$0.13M of net value against B0.
- **Network assumed.**
  - *Warm:* SaaS and VC-backed CFOs and investors; the Melbourne logistics and warehousing community (Fulfilio); BCG alumni; real-estate agents (LocalAgentFinder).
  - *Cold:* resources, mining services, utility-scale energy, insurance.
  - *Fulfilio caveat:* it was incubated by Australia Post in 2018 and later described as "Australia Post-owned", with sites in Melbourne, Sydney, Brisbane and Perth [verified: TechPartner News 2018; Power Retail; undated PPTI]. I found no 2025–26 news, so his warehouse relationships may be 5–8 years old. **They need to be refreshed and tested, not assumed.**
- **Employer.** A SaaS company, no restraint clause. I assume he needs written consent for any side venture, and that a conflict arises only if the venture sells to his employer's customers, investors' portfolio companies or peers, or competes with the employer's product.

## Candidate notes

**B0, stay employed.** It is the safe baseline, but on this lens it scores badly on satisfaction by year 7. A curious builder who has already sold a company tends to decay in a third CFO decade. Staying also has an option value the stage-1 agents ignored: every test below can be run while employed, so B0 is not the alternative to the candidates. It is the platform for testing them. The real decision is which 90-day test to run inside B0.

**B1, distribution holdco.** He has already rejected it, so it fails on satisfaction before anything else. Go-to-market means deal sourcing through business brokers and advisers, a crowded channel with adverse selection. The first "revenue" arrives only at acquisition close (12–18 months). Score it as a benchmark only.

**S1, Closewise.** It has the best founder-network fit of any candidate: he *is* the buyer, and his peers are the first 10 customers.
- *First 10 channel:*
  - VC platform teams at Blackbird, Square Peg, AirTree, OneVentures and Rampersand, which keep "preferred provider" lists for portfolio finance.
  - Startup-side partners at Gilbert + Tobin, LegalVision and Hall & Wilcox.
  - Audit partners at Pitcher Partners, William Buck and RSM, who see failed closes during audits.
  - For foreign subsidiaries, Invest Victoria/Austrade landing-pad referrals and US/UK accounting firms that need an Australian partner.
- *Timing:* 5 design partners at A$6k/month within 3–6 months is realistic [estimate].
- *What stage 1 missed:*
  - **Employer conflict is highest here.** His customers are his employer's peers, and possibly its investors' portfolio companies. He needs a co-founder to front sales from day one, and consent is not assured.
  - **Year-7 satisfaction is poor.** At A$12–28M of revenue this is a 60–150-person accountable-services firm, "his current job multiplied" (stage 1 said so itself).
  - Skeptic 14's crowding evidence (Basis, Rillet, Xero JAX, Multiplier) means fast first revenue does not translate into P(positive).
- *Verdict:* best distribution, weakest moat.

**S2, Resources Finance Office.** The founder's skills fit (5B automation, data layer, path to Runway Capital and royalties), but he has **no network at all** in this vertical, and the gravity is Perth.
- *Perth's weight:* Perth is home to almost one third of all ASX-listed companies [verified: WA DEED "key facts"], and a larger share of junior explorers [estimate]. Automic, under Advent since its A$725M acquisition, now owns Endeavour and Grange and sells outsourced CFO and CoSec services to its registry base. Its AI work is visible on the registry side, not yet in the CFO line [verified: Automic site; mergr].
- *First 10 channel:*
  - **Free 5B benchmark.** Parse 8 quarters of about 750 5Bs, which the founder can build alone, evenings, with zero employer conflict. Use it as the door-opener to managing directors and chairs.
  - **Mid-tier audit partners for juniors.** HLB Mann Judd, Hall Chadwick, RSM, BDO, Stantons and William Buck see which clients have messy closes and could refer them.
  - **Small-cap brokers.** Euroz Hartleys, Canaccord, Argonaut, Taylor Collison and Bell Potter want raise-ready clients.
  - **Events.** AMEC and AusIMM membership; the Melbourne Mining Club, a Minerals Council–AusIMM joint venture whose Victorian Critical Minerals Showcase drew about 300 small and mid-caps [verified: MCA; Vic Resources]; Diggers & Dealers (Kalgoorlie, 3–5 Aug 2026) [verified: AHK calendar, single source]; IMARC, now in Sydney (27–29 Oct 2026), not Melbourne [verified: NSW Mining, Austrade].
- *What stage 1 underplayed:*
  - **The co-founder is the business.** A Perth CA/CoSec with 10–20 listed clients is an incumbent's employee, and their book is "director-linked". Recruiting one at 15–25% equity is the critical-path risk, not the AI.
  - **Pilots run to the reporting calendar.** The first realistic pilot is the March-quarter 5B, due 30 Apr 2027. Ten customers takes 18–24 months.
- *Satisfaction rises over time* (data, then capital, then royalties), which is the opposite of S1.

**S3, Contractor Cash Desk.** It has a good conceptual fit: Fulfilio-style arbitrage of weekly wages against 60-day terms. The go-to-market is the weakest of the Track A options.
- *Where the buyers are:* mining-services contractors sit in WA and Queensland and are reached through CCI WA supplier programs, METS Ignited, the Civil Contractors Federation and tier-1 supplier days. None of these is in his network.
- *A Victorian variant:* Victorian renewable-EPC subcontractors can be reached through All-Energy Australia (MCEC, 28–29 Oct 2026 [verified: MCEC]) and CEC events.
- *Two separate sales:* the service needs a contractor commercial manager, and the finance arm needs a bank or warehouse partner.
- *Starting while employed:* he can do interviews only, because delivery needs an operator.
- *Verdict:* this is a module for S2 or S4 later, not a lead.

**S4, SPV Office.** It has the best Melbourne fit of the finance-function candidates.
- *Where the buyers are:*
  - Sponsors, CEFC, infrastructure funds and super funds sit in Melbourne and Sydney.
  - All-Energy Australia and Australian Energy Week (MCEC, June) are both in Melbourne [verified: MCEC; ESD News].
  - CIS award lists are public, so the target list is free.
- *First 10:* realistically **3–4 sponsors holding 10 SPVs**. The routes in:
  - lender agency and project-finance teams (NAB, ANZ, MUFG, SMBC, CEFC), which receive covenant certificates and know which sponsors deliver them late;
  - technical-only O&M firms that want a financial partner to bid full-service mandates;
  - the Clean Energy Investor Group.
- *Timing:* sales cycles of 9–18 months mean first revenue at month 9–15, so this needs the most patience while employed.
- *Satisfaction:* Y1 is low because of slow selling. Y3 and Y7 are good if the performance data becomes underwriting.
- *Co-founder:* an ex-RES/OX2/Tilt commercial asset manager is findable in Melbourne.

**S5, MeterProof → ColdCore.** It has the strongest founder fit:
- statistics in its purest form (counterfactual baselines);
- CFO structuring (SPVs and CEFC-backed debt);
- Fulfilio's warehouse world;
- a local market: Truganina, Laverton and Derrimut, plus the ESC in Melbourne.

*First 10 channel (MeterProof, asset-light):*
- **(a) An existing VEU accredited person** on a revenue share. This avoids 6–12 months of accreditation and gives immediate access to the ESC process.
- **(b) Industrial refrigeration contractors** (ammonia/CO2 service firms). They see every plant's inefficiency and want a certificate partner.
- **(c) RWTA.** The cold-storage and transport association is based in South Melbourne, and its members include NewCold, Oxford Cold Storage and Swire [verified: rwta.com.au; conference programmes]. Speaking at its conference is a one-to-many entry.
- **(d) Landlords with NABERS warehouse ratings** (Goodman, ESR, Charter Hall, Dexus).
- **(e) His old Fulfilio and Australia Post contacts**, to be re-verified.

*Timing:*
- Paid feasibility and M&V studies (A$10–25k) can start at month 5–9.
- Certificate revenue lags by the measurement period, so it arrives at month 9–15.
- Ten paying sites takes 15–24 months.

*What stage 1 missed:*
- **Certificate price risk is real and close.**
  - VEECs were about A$82.50 in January 2026, and the regulator's own modelling expects 2026–27 prices to fall [verified: ESC/SolarQuotes, Jan 2026; DEECA 2026–27 targets decision].
  - The 2026 target is 4.4M certificates, and the shortfall penalty rises to A$100, which gives a soft cap [verified: DEECA].
  - The VEET Act was amended in 2025 [verified: ESC].
  - So MeterProof alone has a modest ceiling. Its value is as **ColdCore's underwriting engine and lead generator**.
- **ColdCore cannot start while employed beyond a term sheet.** The first site needs A$1–2M of capex and a family-office or CEFC-backed lender.

*Satisfaction:* the highest and most stable across Y1, Y3 and Y7: physical sites, models and capital structuring, much like Fulfilio with a balance sheet.

**S6, PassMark → VoltCover 2.0.**
- *Fit:* a good fit on AI vision and statistics, and LocalAgentFinder's two-sided marketplace logic applies to installers.
- *First 10:* the buyers are concentrated:
  - STC registered agents (most system owners assign certificates to an agent [verified: CER]);
  - large retailers and OEM distributors;
  - green lenders (Brighte, Plenti).
  - So 10 customers in 9–15 months is plausible.
- *Market risk stage 1 underweighted:* the Cheaper Home Batteries rebate was cut from 1 May 2026, when the STC factor fell from 8.4 to 6.8 and larger systems moved to a tiered rate. Step-downs are now **every six months, the next on 1 Jan 2027** [verified: SolarQuotes, EWON, installer sources]. The program budget was expanded [reported, unverified primary]. Volume and installer margins are both under pressure exactly when PassMark needs installers or agents to pay A$60–120 a job.
- *Satisfaction:* lower by Y7 because it is "less finance". VoltCover also needs an underwriting co-founder and a carrier, both cold.

**S7, Long Tail Royalties.**
- *Fit:* an excellent intellectual fit ("find sellers who don't know what their asset is worth" is LocalAgentFinder's logic). Its floor is the best in the set (P(total loss) about 7% in stage 1).
- *Network:* zero. Deal flow comes through Kalgoorlie and Perth brokers, WA probate lawyers and prospectors, all relationship-led.
- *What stage 1 missed:*
  - **It can start while employed with his own money.** He can buy one producing A$0.2–0.4M royalty personally in year 1 (no AFSL needed for his own account). That gives a track record before asking family offices for A$15–25M.
  - **First-time manager risk.** Melbourne family offices are reachable through BCG and VC contacts, but raising A$15–25M as a first-time resources manager is a 12–24-month project.
- *Satisfaction:* high, but the Y7 job (a 3–5-person royalty company) may be too small for his ambition.

**S8, Closure Co.** It needs a closure engineer, a surety insurer, regulator acceptance and a first site owner. None of these is in his network, and none can be tested beyond conversations while he is employed. First revenue arrives in 24–36 months. Satisfaction is excellent in the long run, but the probability of reaching that point is low. **Demote.**

## Promotions/demotions

1. **Merge S2 + S7 (with 14's "Fees for Royalties" and 03's "Royalty-for-Finance").** In the merged business the finance office *is* the royalty origination channel. It solves S7's missing deal flow and gives S2 a Y7 upside beyond a services multiple. The Perth co-founder must then be someone who can also judge tenements, or be paired with a geologist adviser.
2. **Merge S4 into S2 as its Melbourne leg ("Resources & Energy Finance Office").** Energy SPVs are Melbourne and Sydney buyers with 10–15-year contracts. That reduces S2's Perth dependence and its cyclicality. Run one product, two segments, and one 5B/covenant agent stack.
3. **Promote MeterProof above ColdCore as the entry, but don't treat it as the destination.** Treat ColdCore as an option exercised at 3 signed sites plus a lender term sheet.
4. **Demote S8 and S3** to modules or later options. S3 becomes the contractor collections module of S2 or S4.
5. **Not promoted:** 11's Exit-Ready (warrant fees). It runs through the same VC channel as S1 and carries S1's employer conflict.

## Ranking and recommendation

**Top 3 from this lens:**
1. **S5 MeterProof → ColdCore (7.5).** It is the only candidate where skills, warm network, Melbourne location, satisfaction and starting while employed all score well at once, and it has a hard-asset floor if ColdCore triggers.
2. **S2 Resources Finance Office, merged with S7 royalties and the S4 energy leg (7).** It has the best long-run satisfaction and data-to-capital path. It is gated on one hire: the Perth co-founder.
3. **S1 Closewise (6.5).** It has the fastest route to 10 customers and the highest skill fit, but crowding and employer conflict keep P(positive) down, and Y7 satisfaction is poor.

**Melbourne vs Perth.**
- **S5 and S4** are Melbourne-native.
- **S2, S3, S7 and S8** need Perth. The answer is a **Perth co-founder with equity, not a move**. The founder stays in Melbourne, which serves Victorian gold and critical-minerals juniors, the Melbourne Mining Club, energy SPVs and family-office capital. He is in Perth about one week a month during quarterly reporting peaks, and at Diggers & Dealers and RIU events.
- If no Perth co-founder signs within 6 months, S2 should be cut back to its Melbourne and Sydney energy leg (S4).

## Sequencing

1. **Nov–Dec 2026 (employed).**
   - Get written employer consent for an energy-efficiency side venture. This is the lowest-conflict candidate for a SaaS employer.
   - Re-activate 20 Melbourne cold-store, 3PL and landlord contacts.
   - Approach 2 VEU accredited persons and 3 refrigeration contractors.
   - Build the AI baseline engine on interval data from 3–5 sites.
2. **Jan–Apr 2027 (employed).**
   - Sell 2–3 paid M&V and feasibility studies (A$10–25k), which is the first revenue.
   - Sign an AP revenue-share pilot.
   - Recruit the refrigeration co-founder on a sweat-equity vest.
   - In parallel, on evenings, parse the 5B corpus and publish a free "overhead & runway benchmark". This costs under A$10k and tests S2's pull (pass: 40 or more requests) with no conflict.
3. **Gate, around Jun–Sep 2027.** Quit only if:
   - the first metered certificates are approved by the ESC;
   - at least 3 ColdCore sites have non-binding savings-share term sheets;
   - one CEFC-backed lender has given indicative terms;
   - seed of A$1.5–3M is soft-circled (founder A$0.5M).
   - If MeterProof works but no one wants off-balance-sheet finance, keep MeterProof as a side business and stay employed. **That is still a positive outcome.**
4. **If S5's gate fails but the 5B benchmark pulls**, pivot the energy into S2 and hire the Perth co-founder. The royalty option (S7) is first exercised with his own money on one producing royalty.
5. **S1** is a fallback only if he leaves his employer for other reasons, because that removes the conflict.

**How they combine.** Every path shares one engine: statistical baselines and outcome measurement. In S5 it measures energy savings; in S2/S4 it measures runway, covenant headroom and P(production). That shared engine is the founder's real moat across candidates, and he should build it once, while employed.
