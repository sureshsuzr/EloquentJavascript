# Resources & Energy Industry Insider (Skeptic): stage-2 evaluation

Lens: a Perth/Melbourne resources-sector CFO and company secretary who has served ASX juniors, mining-services contractors and renewable SPVs. The question I ask of each candidate is whether it works the way this industry actually buys, pays, appoints and litigates. 4 Nov 2026. 16 web searches. Labels: **[verified: source]**, **[estimate]**, **[unverified]**, and **[industry knowledge]** for practitioner norms I could not find published.

## Assumptions (applied to every candidate)

- **Stay-employed baseline (B0):** A$3.0–3.5M of value over 10 years, midpoint A$3.25M (after-tax salary saved plus employer equity at face value), as in earlier rounds. "P(positive)" means the founder's 10-year value beats A$3.25M.
- **Founder value** = cumulative after-tax salary taken + after-tax founder equity at year 10. Venture salary is A$150–200k from year 2 (about A$1.0–1.2M after tax over the period). Personal capital at risk is A$0.4–0.8M.
- **Tax:** current regime, with a 50% CGT discount on exit. The proposed indexation change would cut every venture EV by about 10–15% and B0 by less. I flag it but don't model it.
- **Dilution:** 15–25% to an operating co-founder (essential in every resources candidate) and 10% ESOP. Capital-heavy models (S5, S7, S8) dilute to 15–40% founder ownership.
- **Time discount:** 8% real on year-10 equity. This is already embedded in my EVs, which is why they sit below the stage-1 figures.
- **Base rates:** "great" outcomes are 5–7%, not 10–14%. Services exits are 0.8–1.5x revenue for boutiques and 6–10x EBITDA for scaled firms [estimate, consistent with round 1].
- **Total loss** means the founder exits the venture with negligible equity, has lost his capital, and ends ≥A$1M behind B0.

## Candidate notes

### B0: stay employed (6.0)
In this lens, B0 is also an *option-preserving* position. Every resources candidate below has a 90-day test that can run while he is employed, and none needs him to quit before a paid pilot exists. The real cost of B0 is forgone learning, not forgone EV.

### B1: staged distribution holdco (5.0)
Outside my lens, and the founder has rejected it. One insider point: a resources-flavoured version ("buy one West Perth CoSec/CFO book") is the only acquisition I would endorse, and only as a *distribution* step inside S2. It is not a strategy.

### S1: Closewise, generic scaleups and subsidiaries (4.0)
I side with stage-1 report 14 over report 11. Nothing in a generic scaleup's finance function is specialised enough to resist Xero/MYOB agents and offshore bookkeepers. The foreign-subsidiary niche is real, but the Big 4, TMF, Vistra and Azets already own it through group mandates signed overseas. The buyer is a regional CFO in Singapore or London who renews by default. Score held down by the channel, not the product.

### S2: Resources Finance Office (6.5; 7.0 when merged with S4 and without royalties)
**How juniors actually buy.** The CoSec is usually a *named person* who sits on 5–12 boards, often from a West Perth firm associated with a director. The CFO role is either the same firm or a part-timer [industry knowledge; consistent with stage-1 13/14 and firm directories]. Typical spend is A$4–8k a month for CoSec, A$5–12k a month for finance/CFO, plus audit [estimate; no published tariff found after a targeted search]. All-in A$100–200k a year is right. **Switching does not happen on price or SLA.** It happens at a *board event*: a recapitalisation or reverse takeover, a new chair after a big raise, the CoSec resigning or retiring, an ASX aware/query letter that embarrasses the board, or a mid-cap merger. I estimate 10–15% of juniors pass through such an event each year [estimate]. That is the sales calendar, so the business needs a broker and lawyer referral engine that fires at those moments.

**Outcome pricing is mostly mis-specified.** "5B lodged by day 20" is not a pain point. Every junior lodges in the last week of the month and nobody pays a premium for day 20. What boards do pay for is (a) not getting an ASX query, (b) a clean, cheap half-year review and audit, (c) capital-raising paperwork done right and fast (Appendix 2A/3B, cleansing notices, LR 7.1/7.1A capacity tracking, notices of meeting), and (d) a visibly lower "admin and corporate" line in the 5B, which activists and retail holders read. Price the guarantee as a **fixed quarterly fee with an all-in corporate-cost cap and SLA credits**, not as a success fee.

**Royalty-for-fees doesn't work in practice.** Here is why a board, broker or auditor will push back:
1. **Value transfer and royalty stacking.** A 0.25–0.5% NSR is permanent. Future financiers, acquirers and royalty buyers price every royalty on a project, and lead managers will tell the board not to encumber the asset for back-office fees.
2. **Conflicted gatekeeper.** The provider drafts the disclosures about the royalty it holds. Where the CoSec firm is director-associated, which is common, a royalty or tenement interest moving to it engages related-party rules (Corporations Act Ch 2E; ASX LR 10.1 for substantial assets), with an independent expert report and a shareholder vote that cost more than the fees saved [verified for the LR 10.1 framework and IER practice: ASX notices and RG 111 references; its application to a royalty is **unverified**, because whether a royalty counts as a "substantial asset" depends on value].
3. **Auditors** won't raise an independence issue (the provider isn't the auditor). They will, however, require the grant to be valued and accounted for as a part-disposal of an exploration asset under AASB 6. That is more audit work, and it lands on the provider's own file.
4. **Many tenements have JV partners with pre-emption rights, or existing royalty holders with first-refusal rights.** Vox's 2026 Sylvania deal depended on a third party waiving a right of first refusal [verified: Vox release, Aug 2026].

**Options, by contrast, are normal.** Consultants and lead managers routinely take options issued within the LR 7.1 15% capacity, with no shareholder vote [industry knowledge]. If he wants an asset kicker, take **options (not royalties) as up to 20–30% of fees for cash-poor clients**. Options are liquid, marked to market, and a recognised conflict.

**The data asset is weaker than stage 1 claims.** 5B aggregation already exists: BDO's quarterly update, and an unofficial "Resource Explorer" chart pack covering 440 explorers [verified: BDO; search result, low-authority]. The free benchmark is a good lead magnet but not a moat.

**The incentive layer is thin for explorers.** JMEI has had no confirmed extension since FY25 [verified: no 2026 bill or budget measure found; AMEC still lobbying]. Exploration is rarely R&DTI-eligible [industry knowledge]. CMPTI applies only to a few dozen processors, who will use the Big 4. Treat incentives as a module for developers and processors, not as core economics.

**Timing.** Explorer cash is at a record A$13.04B [verified: BDO Jun-2026]. That is good for selling now, but it means he would be building into a peak. The 2012–16 pattern (client count and fees falling together) will recur within his 10-year window.

**Melbourne.** A real handicap for Perth juniors, partly offset by a Collins St cluster of VIC/NSW gold and critical-minerals juniors. A Perth CA/CoSec co-founder who brings 5–10 boards is non-negotiable.

The stage-1 P(positive) of 55–60% assumed easy switching and a royalty book. I give it **~38%**, rising to ~42% when it is merged with S4 to dilute the commodity cycle.

### S3: Contractor Cash Desk (4.0)
**The premise about tier-1 debtors is out of date.** BHP pays small, local and Indigenous suppliers in **7 days** (since Jul 2021), and in 30 days or less under its standard exemption. Its standard terms are 60 days, and it has run its **own supply-chain finance programme** at a funding rate SMEs "would not normally access" [verified: BHP fact sheets and media release; BHP Treasury submission, c.2018–19, current status unverified]. Rio moved small Australian suppliers to **20 days** [verified: Mining Weekly 2020]. Fortescue signed the BCA 30-day small-supplier code [verified: search results; current terms unverified]. So "finance only portal-approved BHP/Rio invoices" competes with the debtor's own bank-funded SCF, priced at the debtor's credit spread, which a start-up cannot beat.

**Where the real contractor cash pain sits:**
- **Before approval:** service-entry sheets, PO mismatches, timesheet disputes and unpriced variations. Days lost before the invoice is "approved" exceed the terms themselves [industry knowledge].
- **Tier-2 debtors:** juniors, mid-tier miners, developers and EPCs. Contractors die on disputes with these debtors, not on BHP terms. Examples are Aerison's collapse after a payment dispute with Roy Hill, and Pilbara Resource Group after its miner client scaled back [verified: AMSJ; Business News]. Financing that is variation and dispute credit risk, not "approved invoice" risk.

**The buyer is wrong for a full outsourced function.** A$50–200M contractors run JDE, Pronto or Viewpoint and employ in-house commercial teams. Owners distrust outsiders touching claims. The salvageable piece is a **"work-to-approved-invoice" agent** (portal and SES submission, timesheet-to-PO matching), sold as software plus service to contractors' existing finance teams. That is a product company with a modest ceiling, not a finance function. P(positive) ~28%.

### S4: SPV Office (6.0)
**Real buyers, but the work is bundled elsewhere.** Asset-level accounting is commonly outsourced: an Octopus Energy role describes day-to-day SPV accounting as "outsourced at the asset (SPV) level" with in-house review [verified: job listing]. Fund-level administration goes to Apex (Octopus Australia, Federation's SARA) [verified: Apex releases]. Commercial asset managers (RES, OX2, Blueshore, CWP AM) bundle finance into 10–15-year AM contracts [verified via stage-1 13].

**Insider realities:**
- Lenders want covenant certificates signed by the borrower's director or CFO, so the provider cannot carry the certificate liability. "Outcome" means on-time and accepted, with credits capped at fees.
- Big IPPs (Neoen, Akaysha, Tilt, ACEN, Squadron) keep finance in-house past about 1GW.
- The best buyers are **foreign entrants and funds without an Australian back office** (Japanese, Korean and European sponsors, infrastructure and super-fund platforms buying operating assets), community and C&I portfolios, and technical AMs that lack a finance arm. Melbourne is an advantage here.
- **The fastest channel is white-labelling to technical asset managers and O&M firms.** They own the 10–15-year relationship and want a finance module they don't have to staff. Taking the client directly invites a fight with RES and OX2.

The cold start is still 12–24 months per mandate. Contracts are acyclical and survive asset sales more often than not [estimate]. A better floor than S2, slower cash. P(positive) ~36%.

### S5: ColdCore + MeterProof (5.0)
Not my core lens, but three things an insider sees:
1. **VEEC economics are trending down.** Government modelling expects 2026–27 prices to fall from recent years, the 2021–24 average was A$78, and the scheme is extended to 2045 [verified: DEECA/ESC; energy.vic.gov.au]. Long tenor helps; price per certificate hurts M&V margins.
2. **The large cold-store operators** (Americold, NewCold, Swire, Oxford, Emergent) have their own capex and energy-procurement teams. Independent cold stores are credit-weak, which is the counterparty risk in a 7–12-year savings-share contract [industry knowledge].
3. **Savings-share disputes** over throughput and product-mix baselines are the historic failure mode of Australian energy performance contracts.

The Fulfilio network is a genuine edge for origination. Asset-backed floor, but capital-hungry and dilutive. P(positive) ~32%.

### S6: PassMark → VoltCover 2.0 (4.5)
The defect problem is real: CER inspections find **>60% of inspected Cheaper Home Batteries installs substandard and 1.2% unsafe**, mostly labelling [verified: UTS/CER, Apr 2026]. CER now requires geotagged, timestamped photos [verified: CER installer obligations].

The defect economics, however, undermine willingness to pay:
- Only ~3,400 inspections against 500k+ installs (~0.7%) [verified, opposition-sourced]. An installer's expected cost of a failed inspection is a few dollars per job, so a A$60–120 guarantee is overpriced *from the buyer's point of view*.
- Most defects are cheap labelling call-outs [estimate A$150–400].
- STC registered agents (the Formbay/Greenbank class) already collect and check this photo evidence as part of certificate creation [industry knowledge]. They are the natural owner of an AI audit and could bundle it at near-zero price.

The value is in selling the **defect and failure dataset to insurers and OEMs**, and that loops back to the MGA's insurer-capacity problem. P(positive) ~32%. Low capital loss if run while employed.

### S7: Long Tail Royalties (5.0)
**The niche is not unindexed.** Vox is the incumbent of exactly this tail and runs a proprietary Australian royalty database [industry knowledge; Vox's public positioning]. Its 2026 tickets are A$650k (Feb), up to A$3.4M (Kalman/Sylvania, Aug) and ~A$8.4M (three royalties, Sep) [verified: Vox releases; Crux Investor]. It also sold Federation for A$8M and raised receipts guidance to US$32–37M [verified]. Red Hill buys down to A$220k, and Arc Mineral Royalties is a new WA buyer.

**Title and enforceability bite in practice.** Northern Star is disputing the assignment to Vox of a 4% GRR in the WA Supreme Court [verified: Vox disclosure]. Rights of first refusal gate deals [verified: Sylvania].

Deterra wants larger, near-production assets and holds A$300M+ of "dry powder" [verified: Deterra H1-FY26 summary], so it is an exit, not a competitor. That exit only exists for royalties that are already de-risked.

For a newcomer, the edge has to be **sourcing from estates, prospectors and syndicates before they reach Vox**, and that is a Kalgoorlie relationship game, not an AI one. The floor is good (producing royalties rarely go to zero), but founder ownership falls to 15–25% after raising money, and buying gold royalties near record prices is the classic loss mode. P(positive) ~30%; P(total loss) low (~10%) because most of the capital is other people's money.

### S8: Closure Co (3.0)
**The model exists, but at a scale and with balance sheets he doesn't have.** MMG transferred the closed Century zinc mine and its rehabilitation obligations to Century Bull (Raging Bull Group), which plans to fund closure by reprocessing tailings. Mercuria backed it in Oct 2026. Heritage Minerals at Mount Morgan is another case [verified: MMG release; Engineering News, 15 Oct 2026].

**Insider realities:**
- Relinquishment is rare and has no clear legislative pathway [verified: ACG closure paper; Allens].
- Regulators make the transferee post assurance based on a recalculated liability. A new entity without a balance sheet pays high surety premiums or cash-backs them.
- Juniors holding care-and-maintenance sites have no cash to pay a transferee. Majors keep closure in-house for reputational reasons.
- The tail (acid drainage, perpetual water treatment) is uncapped, and ring-fencing in an SPV is what regulators distrust.

The upfront cash is a liability, not revenue. This is a **trader or private-equity balance-sheet play**. P(positive) ~20%, P(total loss) ~35%. Keep only the asset-light piece: a closure-provision estimation service for juniors and their auditors, as an S2 module.

## Promotions/demotions

- **Merge S2 + S4 into one "Resources & Energy Office", without royalties.** Same agent stack, same CA/CoSec reviewers, same outcome contract (fixed fee + SLA credits + corporate-cost cap). Juniors give faster sales and cash; SPVs give acyclical 10–15-year revenue and play to Melbourne. Mix target by year 5: ~60% juniors/developers, ~40% SPVs, which damps the commodity cycle.
- **Demote S2(a) royalty-for-fees and S2(b) "Runway Capital".** Replace royalties with LR 7.1 options (≤30% of fees, cash-poor clients only). Refund advances are a thin product for explorers while JMEI is lapsed, and Radium Capital already does R&D advances at scale [verified via stage-1 08].
- **Promote one "book purchase" as distribution, not as a strategy.** Buy one small West Perth CoSec/CFO book (10–20 listed clients, ~1x revenue, vendor finance plus earn-out) once the stack is proven on 5–10 clients. It solves the director-relationship barrier at a stroke.
- **Promote "capital-raise back office" (stage-1 13, idea 9) into the S2 core.** Appendix 2A/3B, cleansing notices, LR 7.1 capacity, placement reconciliation. This is where juniors actually suffer deadline pain.
- **Demote S3 to a product module**, the work-to-approved-invoice agent. Drop the tier-1 approved-invoice financing arm.
- **Demote S8 to a module**, closure-provision estimation for S2 clients and their auditors, as a source of deal flow if a balance-sheet partner ever appears.
- **Keep S7 as a year-5+ option** funded by S2 cash and relationships, not as a start.

4. S5 (5.0), S7 (5.0), B1 (5.0), S6 (4.5), S1 (4.0), S3 (4.0), S8 (3.0).

**My one choice: the merged Resources & Energy Office (S2+S4), priced as fixed fee + SLA credits + a corporate-cost cap, with options rather than royalties as an optional kicker.** It is the only candidate where a buyer exists today, the founder's CFO craft is the product, the floor is a saleable practice (A$2–5M revenue at ~1x), and cash arrives within 12–18 months. Its upside is modest (a good case is A$10–15M revenue sold to Automic/Advent, Computershare, a mid-tier firm or an infrastructure-services group). Accept that. On this lens the stage-1 EVs of A$5–9M are optimistic by about a third.

## Sequencing (while employed, then combined)

1. **Months 0–3 (employed, with employer disclosure):**
   - Recruit the Perth CA/CoSec co-founder who brings 5–10 boards. **Without this person, stop.**
   - Run 25 junior interviews (chairs and MDs, not CFOs, because chairs make the appointment) and 15 SPV interviews (foreign sponsors, funds, technical AMs).
   - **Pass for juniors:** ≥5 boards say they would move at their next board event at ≤current cost, *and* ≥2 pilots on a half-year review pack or a capital-raise.
   - **Pass for SPVs:** ≥1 technical AM agrees a white-label pilot for one SPV.
   - **Kill the royalty idea outright** if more than half the chairs and both brokers you ask object. Expect them to.
2. **Months 3–12:** deliver pilots through the contract CA under the co-founder's registration and PI cover. Build the agent stack on real client files. Leave only when ≥A$400k of signed annual fees exist.
3. **Years 1–3:** reach 25–40 junior clients plus 10–20 SPVs. Buy one West Perth book once the stack works. Add the capital-raise and closure-provision modules.
4. **Years 3–5:** for the downturn, shift the mix towards SPVs and developers. If cash and relationships allow, seed S7 (small royalties, sourced from client and estate networks) with outside capital as a separate vehicle, with information barriers. **Never buy royalties over clients' tenements.**
5. **Keep S5/S6 out.** They are separate companies with separate capital needs and would dilute focus.
