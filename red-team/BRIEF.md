# Project brief: AI-native residential property-management agency (Melbourne)

Source: founder's brief, 2 Nov 2026. This file is the single source of truth every red-team agent reads.

## About the founder
Former CEO of LocalAgentFinder (LAF), the agent-comparison site. Founded Fulfilio (warehouse/fulfilment platform, acquired by Australia Post); its real edge was a physical arbitrage, consolidating spare warehouse space into local delivery hubs, not the software. Ex-BCG. Currently a senior finance and operations executive in SaaS. Heavy hands-on AI user. Melbourne-based. Success = replacing a ~$500k income; international scale is a bonus, not a requirement. Has not run a property-management operation and does not yet have a licensed operator partner.

## The idea (current form)
An own-brand, AI-native residential property-management agency in one Melbourne corridor, operator model (founder holds the management authorities), not a software vendor.
- **Staffing:** well-paid career relationship PMs own landlords and judgment calls; AI agents do the transactional work; cheaper, densely routed field staff handle inspections and condition reports. PMs run small pre-qualified viewing tours at lease-up.
- **Positioning:** quality-led, not price-led. Percentage fee at or slightly under market, with guarantees on days to lease, response times, arrears and maintenance cost.
- **Distribution:** a free LAF-style comparison of local PMs (listing data, rent achieved, mystery-shopped response times), with the founder's agency disclosed as an option, plus buying sub-scale rent rolls with debt. International precedent suggests acquisition is the main growth channel and the funnel is the supplement.
- **Earnings beyond the management fee:** when landlords sell, run their agent selection and take a disclosed referral fee (LAF embedded in a relationship already owned). No sales team.
- **The arbitrage:** rent rolls are priced as low-status admin annuities. Sub-scale rolls inside sales agencies are neglected, so buy them at multiples set by the incumbent's margin and run them at the founder's margin. Relationship value at the point of sale is also under-captured.

## Established so far
- The harness is a weak moat alone. Durable assets: contracts, suburb density, trades network, career PMs, review history, licences — all products of volume. Not first-mover duopoly territory.
- Incumbent AI (PropertyMe AiMe drafts replies for PM approval; MRI + Merlo AI in strata) speeds tasks but doesn't redesign the role. A 3–5 year window, not a land grab. PropertyMe exposes live data to external AI assistants, so building on the system of record incrementally is possible.
- :Different (AU, 2017–2023, administration): flat $100/month, ~200 staff vs thousands of properties, 4–6 cities, three distribution pivots, venture equity funding an agency. Lessons: productivity before price cuts, density, debt for the roll, one channel.
- Crossub (AU/UK) white-label PM back office at 30% of agency fee, no lock-in; inspectors ~$150/inspection. Make-or-buy benchmark ≈ $560/property/yr. Landlord-owner keeps ~70%.
- International operators (Dwelly UK ~15k properties, $95M equity + $75M debt; PURE HomeRiver US 40k+ homes, PGIM-lent; Proper US ~20k units) scaled by acquisition and debt, kept local brands/teams, earn across the lifecycle. None has published proof of AI cost-out; Dwelly growing headcount ~300 → ~1,500.
- An owned operator doesn't scale abroad through software. US crowded; Asia weak except Japan (needs licensing partner). A platform for independent career PMs (founder's brand, licence, back office, leads; PM owns the book) could cross borders like a franchise. NZ and UK nearest. Jurisdiction as configuration; adapters over the system of record.
- Victoria: rental bidding banned (Nov 2025); standard application form and no fees on rent payments (Mar 2026). Fee-free payment is law, not a differentiator; comparison tool needs clear disclosure (ACCC).
- Strata attractive on timing: insurance commissions being phased out/banned (NSW industry phase-out from Jan 2026; NSW review recommends ban; Victoria consulting), forcing incumbents to reprice. Victorian manager contracts capped at 3 years, removable by simple majority. Against: incumbent-embedded tools, roll-up capital (PICA), slow committees. Strata contracts deliver investor-owner density for rentals.

## Rough numbers (to model and verify)
- VIC average management fee ~5.1% (FY22); revenue ~$2.0k/property/yr. Rent roll multiple ~$3.70 per $1 management income (VIC FY22); bank lending up to ~70% LVR; 10–20% of price retained ~3 months against lost managements.
- Steady-state margin 30–35% → ~$600–700/property. Income replacement ≈ 750–850 properties unlevered, ~1,100–1,200 if half debt-funded. 1,000 properties ≈ $5M+ roll value.
- Wage cost/property: incumbent PM ~$75k/130 properties ≈ $580; founder's at $110k needs 300+ properties per PM, with role split, to beat it.
- Field cost ~$300–450/property/yr.
- Risks: attrition from investor sell-offs (Victorian active bonds fell ~25k in year to Sep 2024), fee deflation lowering roll values, LVR covenants tied to roll value.
- The roll is a liquid floor (brokers, parcel sales, bank finance). Platform and brand are option value only.

## Load-bearing assumptions
1. **Cost to serve:** 3x+ properties per relationship PM, measured as exceptions per 100 properties per month.
2. **Durability:** the cost gap lasts long enough before incumbents redesign the role.
3. **Acquisition:** comparison-funnel cost per signed management well under roll purchase cost (~$5–7k per management).
4. **Retention:** acquired managements survive the move to an AI-led service.

## Gates before going all in
1. Funnel cost per signed management under ~$2k in one corridor.
2. Back office beats Crossub's ~$560/property, with a relationship PM carrying 300+.
3. At least half of selling landlords sell through the founder.
Gates 1 and 2 testable on first 50–100 properties, using Crossub as back office if needed.
