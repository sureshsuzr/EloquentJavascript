# Capital Strategist & Founder-Fit Skeptic: red-team report

## Verdict (3–5 lines: survives / survives-if / fatal, and the single biggest issue)
**Survives only if** the entry route changes from greenfield to acquiring a licensed agency, and the founder stays employed until a staged gate passes. The biggest issue is that **"income replacement" in the brief is pre-tax operating profit, not cash the founder can take out**. Once a debt-funded roll pays interest, amortisation (from after-tax dollars, because a rent roll is not depreciable) and a licensed partner's equity share, the plan that is "half debt-funded at 1,100–1,200 properties" pays the founder close to nothing for 5–8 years. Unlevered, it ties up ~$4–5M of personal capital that would earn ~$200k a year doing nothing. Founder fit is fine for capital raising, roll sourcing and the funnel, and weak for the part that decides the outcome: PM operations, plus a statutory licence he cannot hold for at least ~18–24 months.

## Attacks on the load-bearing assumptions (only those in your domain; evidence, severity High/Med/Low)

**A1. The income-replacement maths leaves out debt service, tax and dilution. (High)**
The brief treats ~$600–700 a property as founder income. Here is the levered case the brief itself proposes:
- **Roll:** 1,150 properties at ~$5.5k each ≈ $6.3M, half of it debt ≈ $3.2M.
- **Profit:** EBITDA at $650 a property ≈ $750k. Interest at ~8.5% ≈ $270k, leaving ≈ $480k pre-tax.
- **Tax:** company tax at 25% (base-rate entity) leaves ≈ $360k.
- **Principal:** a 10-year amortisation is ≈ $320k a year, which leaves **≈ $40k distributable**.

Macquarie-style terms that amortise to 60% LVR within 3 years make the early years cash-negative. Then take out a 10–20% licensed-partner stake. [estimate: my arithmetic on brief inputs; rate assumption 7.5–9.5% for business term debt is unverified; rent roll as a non-depreciable intangible is my understanding of ATO treatment, so confirm with a tax adviser]

The founder gets paid only once the debt is paid down, or by refinancing a growing roll. That makes this a **wealth-building play (equity in the roll), not income replacement**. The success criterion should be restated in those terms.

**A2. Unlevered is no better on opportunity cost. (High)**
About 800 properties × ~$5.5k ≈ $4.4M of founder equity. At ~4.5% passive yield that capital earns ≈ $200k a year with no work. The agency's *incremental* return for full-time work and operating risk is therefore ≈ $300k pre-tax, below the salary being given up. [estimate]

**A3. The founder cannot "hold the management authorities". (High, timing)**
A Victorian company licence needs an officer in effective control (OIEC) who holds a current individual estate agent's licence, and a company may appoint only one OIEC [verified: CAV company licence page via search summary]. An individual licence needs the prescribed course within 5 years plus **one year of full-time experience as an agent's representative in Victoria within the last 3 years** [verified: CAV individual licence page via search summary].

The founder therefore cannot be the licensee before roughly early 2028–29 even if he starts now. The OIEC carries personal statutory liability (trust account, staff compliance) and VCAT can discipline them [verified: CAV]. A single OIEC is a **hold-up and key-person risk**: if they walk, the business cannot lawfully trade until a replacement is appointed.

**A4. Bank roll finance is not available to a startup. (High)**
NAB's rent roll policy needs a minimum of 100 residential properties under management. Search snippets show both 70% and 80% LVR and terms up to 10 years [verified: NAB rent-roll page title/snippet; the conflict in figures is unresolved]. Macquarie (via a broker) lends up to 70% of its own "multiplier" valuation and amortises to 60% within 3 years [unverified: broker page]. Banks also want a licensed operator with a track record and a personal guarantee with a GSA.

So the first acquisition must be funded with **vendor finance plus founder equity**, not bank debt. The brief's "debt for the roll" is a Stage 3 tool, not a Stage 1 tool.

**A5. Higher margin does not raise exit value. (Med–High)**
Rolls are priced as a multiple of *management income*, not EBITDA [verified: industry range $2.00–3.60 per $1 of rental commission revenue, eliteagent via search]. The founder's margin advantage shows up as cash yield, not as a higher "liquid floor".

The arbitrage pays only if one of two things happens: he holds for yield, or he re-rates the business as a platform sold on an EBITDA multiple to a strategic buyer or PE. The second has no Australian precedent I could find for PM-only books. Do not count re-rating in the base case.

**A6. VC is the wrong capital for the core agency. (High)**
1,000 properties ≈ $2M revenue. That is a good private business and not a venture outcome. :Different raised venture money (reported seed $1.3M and a $25M Series B [unverified: Entrepreneur/SmartCompany snippets, undated]) and went into administration with KPMG after failing to raise again [verified-ish: SmartCompany tag page]. Venture money forced it to push for scale before productivity. Taking seed money for the operator recreates that misalignment.

VC fits only the **platform for independent PMs** (franchise-like, cross-border), which is a different company with a different cap table.

## Things the brief gets wrong, misses or understates
1. **Revenue per property is overstated against the roll-pricing base.** Victoria FY22 average annual management income (AAMI) was **$1,132 per property**, the lowest of the eastern states. The average fee fell from 5.44% in the September quarter to 4.85% in the June quarter and ended at 5.14% [verified: Real Estate Dynamics FY22 report via Elite Agent search snippet].
   - **The multiple applies to AAMI, not to the brief's ~$2.0k.** $3.70 × $1,132 ≈ **$4.2k per management** in FY22. With ~25–35% rent growth since then, perhaps $5.0–5.6k now [estimate].
   - **The $2.0k includes letting and ancillary fees, which are less durable.** Any model that applies the multiple to $2.0k overprices rolls by ~40%. Any model that assumes $2.0k is all recurring overstates the yield.
   - **The FY22 multiple and fee are 4 years stale.** No 2025–26 Victorian multiple was found [unverified]. The brief should get a current broker read (Pendium, Wollermann, bsale).
2. **"Founder holds the authorities" is legally imprecise.** The licensed company holds them, under a licensed OIEC.
3. **Personal guarantee exposure is not stated.** It should be: likely a full PG plus a GSA, and possibly a charge over the family home [estimate: standard SME lending practice].
4. **Retention holdback works in the buyer's favour, but vendor finance is a better tool.** Holding back 10–20% for ~3 months is short. Victorian deals can be negotiated as 20–40% deferred over 12–24 months against retained managements, which shifts transition risk to the seller.
5. **The founder's LAF history cuts both ways.** It is the best roll-sourcing network in the plan, since LAF knows which agencies have sub-scale PM books. It also raises a possible restraint or confidentiality conflict, and a reputational one: a comparison site run by someone who now owns a competitor. Check his LAF exit deed.
6. **No personal-capital ceiling or family-risk statement is set.** Without a maximum loss, the kill criteria have nothing to enforce.
7. **The objective conflates two businesses.** The 1,000-property operator (cash yield plus equity) and the international PM platform (venture-scale) need different capital, governance and possibly founders. Choose one as the base case.

## Improvements (concrete changes to the plan, ranked by impact)

1. **Enter by acquiring a licensed PM business, not by building from zero.**
   - **Target:** a Melbourne corridor PM-only or PM-heavy agency with 250–500 properties whose principal wants to retire within 2–3 years.
   - **Structure:** the principal stays as OIEC for 18–24 months on an earn-out. 25–40% of the price is vendor-financed and paid against 12- and 24-month retention.
   - **What it delivers on day one:** the licence, trust account, staff, bank history (which clears NAB's 100-property floor), a contract base for the AI cost-out experiment, and a successor-OIEC pipeline.
   - **It also solves gates 2 and 4** (cost to serve and retention) on real books rather than a 50–100 property pilot. LAF's network is the sourcing edge.

2. **Restate the objective and the capital stack in three tranches.**
   - **Tranche A (founder cash, ≤ $600k):** licensed-partner salary, funnel build and mystery-shopping, AI/back office, legal and licence costs. Spent only in the pre-sell and first-acquisition phase.
   - **Tranche B (first roll, ~$1.5–2.5M):** ~35% founder equity, ~30% vendor finance, ~35% bank debt after settlement history.
   - **Tranche C (roll-up equity, $5–15M):** from family offices or wholesale (s708) investors as holdco preferred equity (8–10% pref, founder carry) or a co-invest SPV per acquisition. Raise only after Gate G3 below.
   - **No priced venture seed for the operator.** If the platform-for-independent-PMs idea survives, spin it out later with its own cap table.

3. **Set a hard personal-risk envelope now.** Maximum cash at risk is $1.2M. Maximum personal guarantee is $1.5M, never secured over the family home. A written family agreement sets the line at which the founder returns to employment. Every kill criterion below exists to protect this envelope.

4. **Licensed-partner structure** (if the acquisition route stalls, or as a second licensee).
   - **Profiles, in order of preference:**
     1. A retiring principal who comes with their own roll.
     2. Head of PM or BDM at a Melbourne franchise group or independent agency, 10+ years, holding the full licence and the CPP51122 diploma, who wants equity.
     3. The owner of a sub-scale roll (100–300 properties) who contributes it for equity.
   - **Terms:**
     - Salary $150–180k [estimate].
     - 10–20% equity vesting over 4 years with a 1-year cliff, half time-based and half on property count and 12-month retention.
     - The OIEC has a veto limited to compliance and the trust account. The founder holds board control and reserved matters (debt, M&A, issuing equity).
     - Good-leaver and bad-leaver buyback at roll-multiple valuation, plus a 12-month non-solicit.
     - A second licensed agent hired by month 12 as backup OIEC.

5. **The founder starts the agent's-representative course now and works a regular shift in the business once it exists.** This makes him licensable by about 2028–29, removes the long-term single-OIEC dependency, and fills the PM-operations gap with real exposure.

6. **Keep the job until G1 passes.** The pre-sell test, partner search and first LOI can run on evenings, weekends and leave. Resigning before G1 converts an option into a sunk cost.

7. **Rank the alternative paths on capital at risk.**
   - **Funnel only (lowest capital):** an LAF-style PM comparison with disclosed referral fees. It uses his proven edge, but income is uncertain.
   - **Crossub-backed lite agency (low capital):** tests gates 1 and 3 without building ops.
   - **Buy one licensed business (medium capital):** recommended.
   - **Strata-first (medium capital, slow committees):** a sound option, but it needs a second licence domain (owners corporation manager registration) [unverified details].
   - **Greenfield AI agency with debt-funded roll-up:** the brief's current plan, highest capital at risk and slowest to pay out.

## Tests & kill criteria (cheap experiments, metric, threshold, timeframe)
The decision calendar runs from today, 2 Nov 2026. Gates are hard: miss one and you stop or pivot down to a lower-capital path.

|---|---|---|---|

Run it as a normal agency for yield or sell the roll back into the market (liquid floor) |

Running metrics, reviewed monthly by a two-person board (the founder plus one independent with agency M&A experience):
- exceptions per 100 properties per month
- properties per relationship PM
- 90-day and 12-month retention of acquired managements
- cost per signed management by channel
- debt service cover and LVR headroom against covenant

## Answers to open items in my domain
- **Licensed operator / co-founder: who.** First choice is the retiring principal of the business acquired. Second is a head of PM or BDM from a Melbourne franchise group, on 10–20% vesting equity with a compliance-only veto. Hire a second licensee within 12 months. The founder becomes licensed himself by about 2029.
- **Capital plan.** Self-fund Tranche A (≤$600k) and the equity in Tranche B. Use vendor finance before bank debt. Bank debt becomes available once there is a settled book of ≥100 properties and a licensed operator. Raise no venture money for the operator.
- **Milestone that makes a raise credible, and to whom.** Two integrated acquisitions, ≥500 properties, ≥92% 12-month retention, documented ≥250 properties per PM, and margin ≥40%. Pitch this to family offices, wholesale/HNW co-investors and private-credit lenders, which suit a yield asset. Proptech venture funds fit only a separately incorporated PM platform. A strategic partnership with a franchise group's capital arm is worth trying [unverified that any is active in VIC].
- **Kill criteria.** As tabled. The governing principle: do not resign before G1 passes and the first acquisition is ready to settle, and never break the personal-risk envelope.
- **Unit economics (capital view).** Model income as *distributable cash after tax and amortisation*, set the multiple on AAMI not total revenue, and add a scenario for the AI thesis failing at normal incumbent margins. The roll's resale value is the floor.

1. **Tax/legal:** Is a purchased rent roll deductible or depreciable in any structure (e.g. a management-rights contract versus goodwill)? Which holdco and opco structure suits preferred equity and an OIEC with equity?
2. **Licensing/regulatory:** What does the Estate Agents Act require if the OIEC resigns (timeframe to replace, interim trading)? Can an unlicensed director-shareholder hold control lawfully, and what fit-and-proper checks apply to him?

5. **Comparison/ACCC:** Can the founder use LAF-style data or methods without breaching his LAF exit terms, and what disclosure makes the funnel defensible when he owns one of the options?
6. **Lending:** What are NAB's current LVR (70% or 80%), covenants and PG requirements for a first-time acquirer whose licensed operator is a hired OIEC?
