# Melbourne Corridor & Market Analyst: red-team report

## Verdict
**Survives if**: the corridor is chosen for *managed-stock density × fragmentation × roll supply*, not for brand fit. On 2021 Census tenure shares and land area, the inner north (Brunswick–Coburg–Northcote–Thornbury–Preston) and the inner south-east (St Kilda/Elwood–Prahran/Windsor–Caulfield/Carnegie) each hold roughly 40–60k private rentals within 25–40 km of the CBD. In either one, 1,000 properties is a 2–4% share, which is achievable. **The biggest issue is that the brief treats the Victorian investor exodus as background risk, when it actually sets the attrition rate of the roll.** Metro Melbourne lost about 4% of rental stock in one year (2024). If sell-driven management losses run at 7–9% a year rather than 4–5%, then (a) roll multiples and covenants must be priced off Victorian loss rates and (b) Gate 3 (capturing the sale) stops being upside and becomes the main hedge. Neither appears in the current model.

Data caveat: dffh.vic.gov.au, data.vic.gov.au, vic.gov.au, abs.gov.au and id.com.au are blocked by this session's network. LGA counts below are therefore estimates built from verified tenure shares. DFFH released the June-quarter-2026 Rental Report around 29 October 2026. Pulling its LGA bond tables is task one.

## Attacks on the load-bearing assumptions

**A1. Retention (assumption 4): High.** The brief has one risk line on attrition, citing ~25k fewer bonds. That figure is correct: active bonds fell from 677,492 to 652,766 between Sep 2023 and Sep 2024, down 3.6% [verified: Homes Victoria Rental Report via Domain/REIV]. Metro Melbourne fell 4.2% against 1.3% in regional Victoria [verified: DFFH via ABC, Jan 2025], and every metro LGA except Melton lost bonds [verified: trade press reporting DFFH]. The point the brief misses is that a 4% *net* fall in stock means a much higher *gross* rate of investor sales. Some buyers re-let, and new supply offsets part of the loss. My estimate is that 7–9% of a typical Melbourne book was sold in 2024 versus a 4–5% long-run norm [estimate: the net 4% stock fall plus normal churn of 3–5%; corroborated by survey claims that roughly one in four Melbourne landlords sold a property in the past year, (unverified, PIPA)]. On a 1,000 book that means 70–90 lost managements a year to sales alone, before any losses to competitors. A roll bought with 10–20% retention held for 3 months covers about one quarter of that.

**A2. The TAM is falling but stabilising, and the brief is stale on direction: Medium.** RTBA held 736,352 bonds at 30 Jun 2025, up 0.6% year on year [verified: RTBA Annual Report 2024–25]. That figure measures RTBA holdings rather than the DFFH active-residential series, so it is not comparable in level, but it points to stabilisation in FY25. Against that, a 2026 trade article reports that more bonds were refunded than lodged for the first time in about 20 years [unverified: date unclear]. Melbourne vacancy was 1.5% in Apr 2026 [verified: SQM via Property Update] and rental listings sat about 21% below the five-year average in May 2026 [verified: Longview]. So the stock is smaller and tight. That is good for days-to-lease guarantees and bad for growth in book size.

**A3. Fee per property (~$2.0k): Low–Med, roughly right but derived the wrong way.** LAF's own 2026 guide puts Melbourne's average management fee at ~5.8% and Victoria's at ~5.9% [verified: localagentfinder.com.au 2026 guide]. The brief uses 5.1% (FY22). Quoted fees overstate achieved fees: a Footscray roll for sale shows a realised **5.41%** [verified: bsale listing]. Rent is also overstated if you use the new-lettings median ($570/wk metro, June quarter 2025 [verified: DFFH]), because sitting tenants pay below new-letting rents. Working figure: in-place rent of ~$540 × 52 × 5.4% ≈ $1,520, plus letting fees (about 1.5 weeks' rent × ~30% annual turnover ≈ $240) plus admin and inspection fees (~$150) gives about **$1.9k** in the inner north and inner south-east, and about $1.7k in the west and growth areas [estimate]. Model $1.9k, not $2.0k+.

**A4. Acquisition (assumption 3): Medium.** Funnel economics depend on the volume of management decisions in play in the corridor, not on the size of the stock. Rough flow for a 35k managed pool: new investor purchases (~4%) + agency switchers (~8%) + accidental landlords (~1–2%) ≈ **4,500–5,000 decisions a year** [estimate]. Reaching 200 organic signings a year needs a 4–5% share of that flow. LAF-type conversion can plausibly do this. Gate 1 should be stated as *share of flow* as well as *cost per signing*.

**A5. Field cost (~$300–450/property): Low, overstated at density.** Victoria limits general inspections to one per 6 months [unverified: RTA 1997 s86, check]. With DFFH quarterly turnover of ~8.2% [verified: DFFH June quarter 2025], about 33% of properties turn over each year. Visits per property per year ≈ 2 routine + 0.33 × (entry + exit + lease-up) ≈ **3.0**. A salaried inspector on $90k loaded cost doing 8 visits a day over 225 days does 1,800 visits a year, which is $50 per visit and **~$150 per property per year**. That holds only if properties are dense, roughly ≥15 managed per km² so transit stays under 10 minutes. Below that, Crossub's ~$150 per inspection (~$450 a year) is the right number. Density is therefore worth about $300 per property per year, around half the target margin. That is the real case for choosing one tight corridor.

## Things the brief gets wrong, misses or understates
1. **The exodus also creates the referral revenue.** Each sale loses ~$1.9k a year in fees but generates a disclosed referral. Inner-north houses at ~$900k–1.1m × ~2% commission ≈ $18–22k, and a 20–25% referral ≈ **$4–5k**, which is 2–2.5 years of management fee [estimate; check referral norms and Estate Agents Act disclosure]. If Gate 3 is met, a sell-off market *raises* lifetime value per landlord. If it is missed, the roll decays at 7–9% a year. Price rolls and covenants on the missed case.
2. **New rental minimum energy standards from 1 Mar 2027.** These require R5.0 ceiling insulation at new agreements (unless some insulation already exists), with draught-proofing from 1 Jul 2027 [verified: CAV]. The burden falls on old detached and semi-detached stock (Edwardian and Victorian houses in Brunswick, Northcote and Footscray). It is a likely trigger for another round of sales in 2027, and it is also a service opportunity: a compliance-capex project-management product. Apartments are largely unaffected. That favours strata-dense corridors.
3. **Land tax tail.** The tax-free threshold fell from $300k to $50k from 1 Jan 2024 under a temporary COVID debt levy, with +0.1pp rates and the absentee-owner surcharge doubled to 4% [verified: Vic Budget 2023–24 coverage, Pitcher/BDO]. Vacant residential land tax was extended state-wide from 2025 [verified: William Buck]. This hits small single-property investors (inner north) and absentee or foreign owners (Box Hill, CBD) hardest. The levy is temporary (10 years), so the 2033 expiry is a long-dated tailwind.
4. **Portable bonds (live 1 Jul 2026)** and a reported requirement for evidence on bond claims from Oct 2026 [verified: CAV and Premier for the scheme; unverified: evidence requirement] make condition-report quality a cost and compliance item. That supports investing in field staff.
5. **BTR competes for tenants, not landlords.** Melbourne is reported to hold ~58% of the national BTR pipeline, with advanced projects of ~8,000 units in 21 schemes [verified: Feasly, Jun 2026; methodology unclear]. These are concentrated in the CBD, Southbank, Docklands and Cremorne, with some in Brunswick and Footscray. BTR caps apartment rent growth and lengthens lease-up for investor apartments nearby, which matters for a days-to-lease guarantee. It is not addressable by the agency.
6. **Agency penetration.** Not all private rentals are agent-managed. I assume 80–85% in inner Melbourne [estimate]. The addressable pool is therefore about 15% smaller than the bond count.

**Why inner north ranks first despite higher sale risk:** it has the founder's best fit for a quality-led pitch (high-information, review-reading landlords). Its fragmentation favours a comparison funnel. Turnover is high, which means many decisions in play each year. Six postcodes give enough density to hit ≥15 managed per km² by roughly 400 properties. Sale propensity is high, but these are the most valuable referral events in the list. **The inner south-east ranks second, not first,** for two reasons: premium brands compete hard for the best landlords, and a funnel finds it harder to dislodge sticky owners. Its walk-up strata density does make it the best corridor if the strata wedge in the brief is pursued; the two strategies point to the same streets. **The west** is the low-competition, low-fee fallback and the cheapest place to buy rolls.

## Improvements (ranked by impact)
1. **Model retention on Victorian 2024 loss rates (7–9% sale-driven + 3–5% competitive).** Make Gate 3 a P&L line: referral revenue per lost management, weighted by probability. Price acquired rolls on the vendor's 3-year actual loss rate, not on brokers' multiples.
2. **Negotiate retention terms to the market:** 15–25% retained for 6–12 months with pro-rata clawback, plus a separate clawback for sale-driven losses in the first 6 months. A falling-stock market gives the buyer leverage.
3. **Offer vendor agencies a referral-back clause.** When a sales agency sells its roll, give it first right to pitch on landlord sales from that roll for 2–3 years. This lowers price resistance and widens roll supply. Accept the reduced Gate 3 capture on acquired books and keep full capture on funnel books.
4. **Commit to one six-postcode core and set density as an operating KPI:** managed properties per km² and field minutes per visit. Do not sign outside the core until it reaches 15/km².
5. **Build a 2027 minimum-standards product** (insulation and draught-proofing audit, quotes, VEU discounts, compliance pack). It is a landlord-acquisition hook and a reason for landlords to stay rather than sell, aimed at the exact stock (old houses) most likely to leave.
6. **Use $1.9k fee per property and 5.4% achieved fee** in the model. Track the fee mix of acquired rolls.

## Answers to open items in my domain
- **Corridor:** inner north (Brunswick, Brunswick East/West, Coburg, Northcote, Thornbury, Preston), with the inner SE as strata-led alternative and the west as low-cost fallback.
- **Pre-sell test design:** six postcodes. Run an LAF-style local PM comparison with disclosed own option (ACCC), backed by mystery-shopped response-time data. Seed it with ~2,000 investor-owned addresses inferred from rental listings history. Measure cost per signing and share of flow.
- **Unit economics inputs from my side:** fee $1.9k a year; attrition base 10%, stress 14%; field $150 (dense) vs $450 (outsourced); referral $4–5k × capture rate.

## Questions I'd hand to other specialists
- **Rent-roll broker/valuer:** what are current Victorian multiples and retention terms versus FY22 $3.70, and do lenders now haircut Victorian rolls for attrition?
- **Lender:** do LVR covenants test against roll *value* quarterly? A sale-driven 8% annual decay could breach covenants in year 2.
- **Regulatory/legal:** referral-fee disclosure and rebate rules under the Estate Agents Act; whether the bond-claim evidence requirement (Oct 2026) is in force; the s86 inspection frequency.
- **Strata specialist:** lot counts and OC manager fragmentation in Port Phillip/Glen Eira walk-ups, which would decide whether the inner SE should be corridor 1.
- **Finance/modeller:** a sensitivity of income replacement to attrition (8/10/14%) × Gate 3 capture (0/25/50%).
- **Founder (LAF data):** historic LAF PM-comparison request volumes and conversion by postcode, which would give the funnel's share of flow directly.
