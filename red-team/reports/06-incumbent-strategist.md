# Competitive Strategist (Incumbents & Software Vendors): red-team report

## Verdict
**Survives only if the plan is repositioned.** The cost gap is real against the brief's benchmark, but the brief compares itself with the wrong incumbent. The relevant competitor is an incumbent with offshore VAs that is about to receive agentic AI from its software vendor at no development cost. That leaves a gap of roughly $50–150 per property, and it closes in 12–24 months, not 3–5 years. **The biggest issue:** the one durable advantage, independence from sales commission, is treated as a side revenue line ("referral fee"). It should be the core of the business. A franchise or boutique sales agency cannot copy it without cutting into its own sales commissions. Cost-out should be a hygiene factor, not the moat.

## Attacks on the load-bearing assumptions

**A1. Cost to serve (3x+ per PM): the benchmark is stale. Severity: High.**
The brief uses ~$75k for ~130 properties, about $580 per property. That describes a PM with no support. Melbourne agencies already use Philippine VAs, and vendors pitch it to them directly: Outsource Workers was founded by a Ray White Broadbeach agent, and Cloudstaff and Affordable Staff are listed in PropertyMe's partner directory. Agency rates for a pre-vetted VA run about A$6.50–15/hr [verified: VA Masters / Outsource Workers vendor pages, 2026; vendor claims].
- Steelmanned incumbent pod: one PM at $75k plus ~12% super and on-costs ≈ $88k, plus two full-time VAs at ~$20k each ≈ $128k, carrying 220–250 properties. That is **≈ $510–580 per property in wages, but at 1.7–1.9x the brief's ratio** [estimate: SG 12% from 1 July 2025; 1,824 hrs × ~$11/hr per VA].
- Add AiMe-style drafting (already shipped) and agentic workflows (announced), and the incumbent plausibly reaches 250–300 per PM by 2028. That would be about $430–510 per property [estimate].
- Founder's model: a $110k PM (~$128k loaded) at 300 properties is $427, plus AI and platform costs of maybe $50–100 per property. **Net advantage: ~$0–150 per property.** On 1,000 properties that is at most ~$150k a year. It matters, but it is not a moat, and it disappears if the founder's PMs plateau at 250.
- Even the brief's make-or-buy benchmark, Crossub at ~$560, is itself an offshore back office. Gate 2 ("beat Crossub") therefore tests "beat an offshore incumbent", which is the right test. The ratio target should then be 350+ per PM, not 300.

**A2. Durability: the tooling window is 12–24 months. The operating-model window is longer, but it is not a cost window. Severity: High.**
Incumbents do not need to redesign their own roles. Their system-of-record vendors ship the redesign to the whole installed base at once:
- **PropertyMe:** 6,000+ agencies, ~1.9M properties, EQT-backed [verified: EQT investment release]. Shipped Reply with AiMe (drafts) and AiMe Comply. AiMe Connect lets Claude or ChatGPT read live PropertyMe data, for 35,000+ users. An "agentic AiMe" is announced, with no date [verified: PropertyMe pages; Elite Agent / ProptechConnect, 2026].
- **MRI:** acquired Proptech Labs in Oct 2025 to add AI maintenance, inspections and invoicing to Property Tree and Palace [verified: AAP/Cision release, 6 Oct 2025]. In 2026 it launched a CommBank co-funded "Pro AI" bundle for Property Tree agencies [verified: MRI press release via PR Newswire Asia, 2026]. That is a bank subsidising AI adoption by incumbents.
- **Ailo:** the platform for Ray White (co-founded by Ben White) and LJ Hooker (partnership March 2025) [verified: Elite Agent 2025; REB]. The two biggest franchise networks now sit on a platform whose pitch is more doors per PM.
- **Jellis Craig:** has run a 24/7 AI renter concierge (Rental Heroes) across ~30 offices since 2022 [verified: REB, 24 Jun 2022].

The longer window that does exist is organisational, not technical. Franchisors cannot mandate role design in independently owned offices. In a sales-led agency, the PM department exists to feed listings and to buffer revenue. Principals will add tools and VAs, but they will not pay $110k career PMs or put guarantees in writing. That gap persists, but it is a quality and positioning gap, and the plan has to monetise it as one.

**A3. Acquisition arbitrage: the marginal roll buyer is not an "incumbent-margin" buyer. Severity: High.**
The brief assumes rolls are priced off the incumbent's margin. In a broker-run sale, the clearing price is set by the highest bidder. That bidder is usually a local sales agency that:
- absorbs 100 properties into existing PMs and VAs at low marginal cost, and
- values the landlord-to-vendor pipeline at the **full** sales commission, not a referral fee.

On a $1M Melbourne sale at ~2% commission, the sales agency books ~$20k gross. The founder books a disclosed referral fee of maybe 20–30% of that [estimate]. Sales agencies therefore outbid the founder on exactly the rolls with good seller pipelines. Melbourne multiples are cited at **$2.80–3.50** [unverified: Acumentis, undated]. One live North Melbourne listing asks about 3.5x [verified: SEEK Business listing, asking price]. Both are below the brief's $3.70. Either rolls are cheaper than modelled (good) or the $3.70 is stale or overstated (flag it). The arbitrage survives only on rolls that sales-agency buyers do not want: PM-only principals retiring, compliance-heavy or arrears-heavy rolls, outer suburbs, and rolls stuck inside franchise offices being restructured.

**A4. Retention through the transition: incumbents will attack for the first 90 days. Severity: Medium.**
On any announced roll sale, nearby sales agencies cold-call the landlords, often using the departing PM. That PM may also leave with relationships informally. The usual 10–20% retention holdback covers about three months [brief]. The real churn arrives at the first fee change or the first PM change.

## Things the brief gets wrong, misses or understates
1. **Past-disruptor list.** I could find nothing linking **Leasey** to Australian residential PM. Leasey.AI is a Vancouver company focused on US leasing [verified: CanadianSME; Mammoth Times release Dec 2025]. **Picket** is a Nashville US platform [verified: CB Insights]. I could not verify a **Cubbi/Kolmeo** failure: Kolmeo appears to operate, and Cubbi's status is unknown. **Rent.com.au** is still ASX-listed, with RentPay growing slowly [verified: Jan 2025 quarterly coverage]. The real AU precedents are **:Different** and **Yabonza**:
   - :Different was Sydney-founded in 2017 and entered voluntary administration in June 2023 after a $25M Series B led by CBA's x15ventures. KPMG sold the assets [verified: SmartCompany; Business News Australia; ASIC notice 29 Jun 2023].
   - Yabonza went into administration with the rent roll as its main asset [verified: Startup Daily; date unverified].
   - The brief's "flat $100/month, ~200 staff" for :Different: [unverified].

   **Pattern:** AU operator-disruptors either die or become vendors to incumbents (Ailo, Rental Heroes, Agentsy). None became a durable own-brand agency.
2. **Who is building "AI-native" in AU right now (2025–26):** vendors, not operators. Agentsy raised a $700k pre-seed (Feb 2026, Sydney, tools sold to agencies) [verified: SmartCompany]. RentBetter raised **A$5M in Sept 2026** to build AI self-management for landlords [verified: Startup Daily / Startup Researcher]. Collings markets an AI-native self-management platform [unverified, vendor claim]. I found **no own-brand AI-native PM agency in Melbourne** [verified as absence in search; not proof]. Read that as an open lane, and also as a warning that VCs no longer fund operators after :Different.
3. **The substitute threat is understated.** RentBetter-style self-management caps the fee for cost-sensitive landlords. The founder's quality-led positioning is the right side of that split, but it shrinks the addressable pool to landlords who value outcomes over fee.
4. **The comparison site's data supply is hostile territory:**
   - REA sued Domain in Dec 2024 for scraping 181 listings [verified: Online Marketplaces] and runs Kasada bot defences [verified: iTnews].
   - CoStar completed its takeover of Domain on 27 Aug 2025 [verified: CoStar 8-K]. CoStar has a long history of aggressive data-rights litigation in the US. Pricefinder and Domain Insight are now its own products.
   - "Rent achieved" and "days to lease" per agency come from listing and leased data that REA and CoStar/Domain own and monetise.
5. **Defamation exposure is missing.** In Victoria a corporation with fewer than 10 employees can sue for defamation [Defamation Act 2005 (Vic) s 9; verified: statute, general knowledge]. Most suburban PM offices are under 10 staff. Publishing mystery-shopped "slow response" scores about named small agencies is the cheapest weapon incumbents have.
6. **Tight market blunts the guarantees.** Melbourne vacancy was ~1.2% in May 2026, with rents up 4.8% y/y [verified: Cotality via Jellis Craig "The Report"]. "Days to lease" guarantees cost nothing to match today. They become a differentiator only when vacancy rises, and only then do they cost the founder anything.
7. **Fee-matching at LAF scale is a known pattern.** On an agent-comparison site, a share of users take the quote back to their existing agent. For PM, the incumbent can match the fee in a single phone call at zero switching cost. The brief does not budget for this leakage in funnel CAC.

## Improvements (ranked by impact)
1. **Make independence from sales the product, not the referral fee.** The pitch: "Your PM is never trying to win your sale listing. When you sell, we run a neutral agent tender and get you the best agent and commission." That is genuine counter-positioning in 7 Powers terms. A sales-led incumbent cannot adopt it without giving up the 1–2% commission its PM department exists to capture. Put the brief's guarantees, agent-neutral sale advisory and published service levels behind it. Cost-out funds this; it does not sell it.
2. **Re-baseline every cost gate to the VA-augmented incumbent.** Gate 2 becomes: all-in PM plus back-office cost ≤ ~$420 per property at 350 per PM, with outcome metrics (arrears, days to lease, maintenance cost per property) at or above top-quartile corridor agencies. If you cannot beat an offshore pod on cost **and** quality, you are a more expensive Ray White.
3. **Turn potential retaliators into roll suppliers.** Offer sales-only or sales-led boutiques in the corridor a "PM exit": buy their sub-scale roll, then refer selling landlords back to them for a defined period (disclosed, with no tie). That neutralises the main reason sales agencies outbid you (the vendor pipeline) and turns them from cold-callers into allies. It conflicts partly with a neutral comparison site, so choose per corridor and disclose it. Also target PM-only principals retiring and franchise offices being consolidated.
4. **Rebuild the comparison site to be retaliation-proof:**
   - Run it from a separate entity, with editorial rules published and no ranking of the founder's agency (show it as "also consider", labelled).
   - Publish only verifiable data: the agency's own published fees, licence status, VCAT tenancy case counts (public), and landlord reviews with proof of management.
   - Use mystery-shop timing only as aggregated suburb benchmarks, never as named scores for small agencies.
   - Offer an opt-in "verified service level" badge that agencies **choose** to join. Incumbents that refuse look worse, without you publishing a defamation-prone score.
   - Avoid scraping REA or Domain. Licence data, or ask landlords to upload their own statements (the "rent achieved" proof).
5. **Don't build what PropertyMe, MRI and Ailo will ship.** Sit on PropertyMe (AiMe Connect gives read access for agents today) and spend engineering only on exception routing, field scheduling density and outcome dashboards. The harness is not a moat, so minimise its capex.
6. **Lock in career PMs against poaching.** PMs at $110k with landlord loyalty are the obvious target for incumbents. Offer a book-equity or phantom-equity share tied to retention of their landlords (a step toward the brief's "platform for independent PMs"), enforceable non-solicits, and documented landlord ownership.
7. **Pace debt with fee deflation.** If agentic tools let every incumbent run ~250–300 per PM, competitive fee cuts and lower roll multiples follow in 2027–29. The founder wants to be the buyer during that repricing, not the seller covering an LVR covenant. Front-load funnel growth and back-load roll purchases.
8. **Licensed operator: buy one.** Acquire a small PM-only agency whose principal is retiring and keep the licensee-in-charge for 12–24 months on an earn-out. That solves the licence open item and the first roll purchase at once.

## Answers to open items in my domain
- **Is the 3–5 year window real?** As a cost window, no: count on 12–24 months before agentic vendor features plus VAs close most of the gap. As a positioning window, yes, and longer. Franchise economics (PM feeds sales) and franchisee autonomy stop incumbents from becoming agent-neutral, and no VC-funded operator is likely to re-enter after :Different. The binding window is the **roll acquisition** window: Victorian investor exits and sub-scale principals selling while multiples are still around 3x.
- **Defensible position:** suburb density (scale economies, local), agent-neutral lifecycle advice (counter-positioning), career-PM book equity (cornered resource, partly), review history (brand, slow). No network effects. Software provides no defensibility.
- **Kill criteria (my domain):** see the table. The two hard kills are losing roll auctions systematically to sales agencies, and failing Gate 3 on neutral sale advisory.
- **Licensed operator:** acquire one with the first roll (Improvement 8). Do not depend on a co-founder search.

## Questions I'd hand to other specialists
1. **Legal/regulatory:** Under the Estate Agents Act 1980 (Vic) and the Estate Agents (Professional Conduct) Regulations, what disclosure and consent do referral fees on a landlord's sale require? Can you be both the comparison operator and a listed agency without ACL s18/s29 exposure? How large is the s9 Defamation Act risk from named response-time scores?
2. **Finance/lenders:** Will roll lenders (Westpac, Bankwest, specialist lenders) lend at ~70% LVR on a roll whose service model has changed (AI-led, central PMs)? Do covenants reprice if corridor multiples fall to ~2.8x?
3. **Operations:** What exceptions per 100 properties per month does a VA-augmented incumbent pod actually run? We need that number to know whether 350 per PM is real.
4. **Market/data:** What is the true 2026 Melbourne clearing multiple by sub-market, and what share of corridor roll sales go to sales agencies versus PM-only buyers?
5. **People/HR:** What pay and equity structure keeps a $110k career PM from leaving with landlords, and are non-solicits for PMs enforceable in Victoria in practice?
6. **Customer research:** What share of corridor landlords would pay at-market fees for a PM with no sales conflict? This is the core hypothesis, untested.
