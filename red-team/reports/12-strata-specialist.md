# Owners Corporation (Strata) Management Specialist: red-team report

## Verdict
**Avoid strata as a wedge. Treat it as a year-3+ partnership adjacency at most, and never own OC management contracts to feed rentals.** Strata is a separate, low-margin business. A lot earns roughly $80–130 a year in profit before any commission ban, against $600–700 for a rental management. That means about 5,000 lots to replace $500k, run on committee sales cycles against PE-backed consolidators that already use AI. The single biggest issue is that the brief's premise, "strata contracts deliver investor-owner density for rentals", rests on cross-selling. Victoria's reform direction targets exactly that kind of conflicted benefit, and the owners register is not a marketing list. The density the brief wants is real, but you get it more cheaply through building-level lease-up and developer mandates than by managing the owners corporation.

## Attacks on the load-bearing assumptions

**A1. "Strata is attractive on timing because commissions are being banned" (High: largely wrong for Victoria as at Nov 2026).**
- NSW: the 1 Jan 2026 phase-out is a **voluntary SCA (NSW) member policy** over three years, not law [verified: insurancebusinessmag.com.au; SCA Vic bulletin Oct 2025]. The NSW Productivity and Equality Commission (PEC) delivered its commissions report on 27 Feb 2026, recommending a move to fee-for-service. Secondary sources disagree on whether it calls for prompt prohibition or a three-year phase-out. I found no legislated NSW ban [verified: nsw.gov.au PEC document library listing; REINSW Apr 2026; status of government decision unverified].
- Victoria: the expert panel (led by Marsha Thomson, final report late 2025) recommended banning manager commissions, rewards or other benefits except specified fees for service. The government's mid-2026 response supported 46 of 51 recommendations but **deferred the commission ban for further analysis** [verified: trade press via insuranceonline.com.au, lookupstrata.com.au, June 2026; I did not read the primary response document]. Secondary sources conflict on whether a bill was introduced on 3 June 2026 or is still being drafted. **This is time-sensitive and needs checking against the vic.gov.au review page before any decision.**
- Implication: Victorian incumbents are **not yet forced to reprice**. Even when they are, NSW shows the path: managers raise base fees and add an "insurance handling fee". About 24% of businesses already charge one, and 92% charge additional fees beyond base, up from 66% in 2022 [unverified: secondary summary of the 2026 Macquarie benchmarking report]. Repricing transfers revenue between line items; it does not open a market gap for a newcomer. SCA Vic says fees will rise if commissions go [verified: SCA Vic Oct 2025 bulletin].

**A2. "Strata contracts deliver investor-owner density for rentals" (High: overstated and legally fragile).**
- *Conflict:* an OC manager's client is the owners corporation, not the lot owner. Running rentals for some owners creates direct conflicts: by-law enforcement against your own tenants, arrears recovery against your own landlords, common-property versus lot-repair cost allocation, and contractor selection. British Columbia's regulator expressly treats rental PM for owners in a strata the brokerage manages as a conflict requiring management [verified: BCFSA guidelines]. Victoria has no equivalent guidance that I found. However, the OC Act's manager duties (honesty, good faith, due care, no improper use of information) apply. [unverified: exact wording of s 122; check legislation.vic.gov.au]
- *Data use:* the owners register exists for OC purposes. Using it to market a rental agency is a secondary use. That risks Privacy Act APP 6 exposure if the entity is covered (likely once rental and strata revenue sit in one group above $3M), plus Spam Act consent issues. This is the cross-sell channel, and it is the weakest legal link [estimate: legal reasoning; hand to privacy specialist].
- *Paid referrals:* a referral fee from your rental agency to an independent OC manager is exactly the "other financial benefit connected with duties" that the Victorian panel recommended banning. **Partnership via paid referral is the thing being regulated away.**
- *Conversion maths:* take a 100-lot Melbourne block with ~50–60 investor lots [estimate: middle-ring/inner apartments skew heavily rented; Greater Melbourne households renting 30.2% in 2021, apartments far higher, verified: Renting in Victoria snapshots / Census]. About 85% use a PM [estimate] and about 10–15% switch PM a year [estimate: industry churn]. That gives ~5–8 switchers per 100 lots a year. Capture from an OC-manager touchpoint is plausibly 10–30% of switchers. **Expected yield: about 0.5–2.5 new managements per 100 lots per year.** On 5,000 lots that is 25–125 a year. At brief values of $5–7k per management, it is worth $0.1–0.9M a year, but it needs ~$5M of strata roll first. As a feeder, strata costs more per signed management than the funnel gate (<$2k) by an order of magnitude unless the strata book pays for itself.
- *Investor quality:* Melbourne apartment investors are the cohort most likely to exit (flat capital growth, land tax changes). Active bonds fell ~25k in the year to Sep 2024 per the brief. Apartment rents are also below house rents, so fee per management is lower. Building density buys route efficiency but lower revenue per door and higher attrition.

**A3. "Slow committees" (Med: understated).** Switching happens at AGMs or EGMs. Committees tender, compare 3 quotes, and wait for contract expiry, so sales cycles run 3–9 months [estimate]. Removal is by ordinary resolution at a general meeting (or a ballot). Terms are capped at 3 years (s 119(1D); up to 5 for retirement-village OCs under s 143E) and auto-renewal at manager discretion is prohibited [verified: Law Handbook / FLS; AustLII s 143E]. That cuts both ways: easy to win, easy to lose. **A strata book has a contractual life of ≤3 years and a removal right at any general meeting.** It is a weaker asset than a rental management authority, and lenders price it that way.

**A4. "AI gives a 3–5 year window" (High in strata: the window is narrower).** Merlo AI integrates with MRI Strata Master and Microsoft 365. It handles inbox, AGM scheduling and compliance, and claims more than 150,000 lots supported nationally [verified: mrisoftware.com/au; Merlo Trustpilot listing]. Strata Master is the dominant system of record. Lots per FTE reportedly rose from 349 (2022) to 415 [unverified: secondary summary of 2026 Macquarie report]. Incumbents are already harvesting the productivity, so an AI newcomer has no cost gap to arbitrage.

**A5. Roll-up competition (High).**
- PICA Group: more than 185,000 lots, owns Ace Body Corporate (acquired 2018, ~60k lots, 118 franchise branches, Melbourne flagship) [verified: picagroup.com.au; realestatebusiness.com.au].
- Bright & Duggan: ~140k+ lots after SSKB, a Johns Lyng subsidiary. Johns Lyng was taken private by Pacific Equity Partners (with CPP) for ~$1.1–1.3B, completing Oct 2025 [verified: insurancenews.com.au, mergr]. So a PE-backed buyer with cheap capital is now bidding for strata rolls.
- These buyers will outbid a debt-funded newcomer on any Melbourne book worth having.

## Things the brief gets wrong, misses or understates
1. **"NSW industry phase-out from Jan 2026" is voluntary industry policy**, not regulation. The statutory process (PEC, Feb 2026) has not, to my knowledge, produced a ban. "Victoria consulting" should read: panel recommended a ban, government deferred it (mid-2026).
2. **"Strata Plus" and "Strata Community"**: I could not verify Strata Plus as a PICA brand [unverified]. "Strata Community" is the industry association (SCA), and Strata Community Insurance is an underwriting agency; neither is a roll-up competitor. The real Victorian landscape is PICA/Ace, Bright & Duggan (PEP), and a long tail of independents (Whittles, MBCM, VBCS and others; ownership unverified).
3. **Strata and residential PM economics differ by roughly 6x per unit.** One rental management yields about the profit of 6–8 strata lots. The brief treats them as adjacent; operationally they are different trades (finance and meetings versus tenancy).
4. **Victorian OC managers need registration with the Business Licensing Authority** (plus PI and fidelity cover) and no minimum education today [verified: CAV options-paper submissions]. Reform will likely add professional standards, raising compliance cost for a newcomer.
5. **Most Victorian OCs are tiny.** About 85,000+ active OCs cover ~772,000 lots, an average of ~9 lots per OC [verified: 2018 RIS and SCA Vic submission; figures look stale]. A large share are 2–5 lot plans, often self-managed. Dense investor stock is concentrated in perhaps a few thousand 50+ lot buildings.
6. **The real density play is new-build lease-up**, which the brief misses. Developers appoint the initial OC manager and often the leasing agent. Leasing 60–200 units at completion delivers building density on day one without any OC conflict.

## Improvements (ranked by impact)
1. **Drop "own strata contracts" from the wedge.** Remove it from the capital plan and the narrative to investors and lenders. It doubles the regulatory surface and the capital need for an uncertain feeder.
2. **Replace it with a "building density" play.** In the corridor, list every 30+ lot building (Landata plan search plus REA/Domain lease history). Rank buildings by leases per year and current agency share, then target owners in the top 20 buildings directly. Use the comparison funnel, letterbox and on-site signage, which are legal without the owners register. Inspection routing inside one building is where the field-cost saving is ($300–450/property may drop 30–40% [estimate]).
3. **Pursue developer and builder lease-up mandates** for 2027–28 completions in the corridor. One 100-unit completion can deliver 40–70 managements in a quarter at near-zero CAC. Offer guaranteed days-to-lease (already in the positioning).
4. **Non-paid information partnerships with independent OC managers.** Offer an "approved PM standard": defined response times for building issues, compliance with by-laws, and a single contact for the OC manager. Pay nothing; benefit both sides on service. This survives a Victorian commission ban.
5. **Keep an opportunistic option, years 3–5.** If Victoria legislates a ban and small independent OC books trade below ~1.5x fees, a cheap tuck-in of buildings where you already hold 20%+ of rentals could be justified. Structure it in a separate entity with an information barrier and a written conflicts policy.

| Test | Metric | Threshold | Timeframe |

| Building-targeted pre-sell (no register use) | Signed management authorities per 100 investor lots contacted | ≥2 per 100 within 90 days | 90 days |
| Developer lease-up pitch | Mandates from corridor completions 2027–28 | ≥1 mandate of 30+ units | 6 months |
| Independent OC manager partnership (unpaid) | Managers agreeing to a named-PM service standard | ≥2 of 10 approached | 60 days |
| Strata roll pricing check (only if revisiting) | Price/fees on 3 Victorian OC broker memoranda, plus commission share | Proceed only if <1.8x fees and commissions <10% of revenue | Year 3 |
| Regulatory watch | Victorian bill text on manager remuneration and conflicts | If it bans benefits linked to cross-referrals, kill any strata-rental tie-up | Ongoing; check Q1 2027 |

**Kill the strata adjacency entirely if** any of these holds:
- Building-targeted acquisition already yields ≥2 per 100 investor lots without strata. Then strata adds nothing.
- Victorian law bans cross-benefits.
- Melbourne OC rolls price above 2.5x fees.

## Answers to open items in my domain
**Strata model (fee per lot, lots per manager, commissions share):**
- **Revenue:** base $180–400/lot depending on size (towers lowest per lot, small plans highest). Total revenue about $450–600/lot including disbursements (~$40–100), additional fees, and insurance commissions (~$60–150/lot, 15–20% of revenue).
- **Productivity:** ~415 lots per FTE across the business; portfolio managers carry 500–900 lots.
- **Margin:** EBIT ~15–25%, i.e. $80–130/lot pre-ban. A commission ban without repricing removes most of the margin. With NSW-style repricing (insurance handling fees plus base increases), most of it comes back within 1–2 years.
- **Scale and capital:** income replacement needs ~5,000 lots and ~$5–6M of roll at 2–2.5x fees.
- **Asset quality:** contracts are ≤3 years and removable by ordinary resolution, so lenders will haircut LVR versus rental rolls [estimate].

**Kill criteria (strata-specific):** as above.

## Questions I'd hand to other specialists
1. **Legal/privacy:** can an OC manager in the same group lawfully use the owners register or levy communications to offer rental management (APP 6/7, Spam Act, OC Act duties)? What conflict protocol would CAV accept?
2. **Regulatory:** what exactly is in the Victorian government's response and any 2026 bill on manager remuneration, registration-to-licensing, and conflicts? Is a bill before Parliament as of Nov 2026?
3. **Finance/lending:** what LVR and covenants do Macquarie and other lenders offer on Victorian OC books versus rental rolls, given 3-year terms?
4. **Field ops:** what is the actual inspection and condition-report cost saving from 20+ managed units in one building versus a dispersed house mix?
5. **Acquisition:** do sales agencies in the corridor hold building-concentrated rental books from off-the-plan launches? These are likely the cheapest density buy.
6. **Funnel/marketing:** what CAC do building-targeted campaigns (signage, letterbox, comparison pages per building) achieve versus suburb-wide?
7. **Developer relations:** which corridor projects complete in 2027–28, and who holds their leasing and OC manager appointments?
