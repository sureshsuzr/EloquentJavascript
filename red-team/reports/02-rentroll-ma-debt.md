# Rent-Roll M&A and Debt Specialist: red-team report

## Verdict (3–5 lines: survives / survives-if / fatal, and the single biggest issue)
**Survives-if.** The acquisition arbitrage is real but much thinner than the brief implies. Sub-scale rolls are cheap mostly for good reasons: churn, discounted fees, landlords who are sellers-in-waiting, and size below bank lending minimums. The local buyers you compete against (sales agencies with an existing PM desk) already price in their own **marginal** bolt-on margin, which is higher than your steady-state 30–35%. **Biggest issue:** at current multiples (~$3.0–3.7 per $1 of commission), your claimed 32% margin gives a ~9–11% pre-tax cash yield after normal first-year attrition. That is barely above the cost of debt, and 10-year principal-and-interest (P&I) repayments absorb most of the cash. Debt-funded roll buying only replaces the founder's income if the cost-to-serve gate is proven first and pushes contribution to ~$900+/door. Buy after Gate 2, not before.

## Attacks on the load-bearing assumptions (only those in your domain; evidence, severity High/Med/Low)

**A1. "Rolls are priced off the incumbent's margin, so you capture the spread." Severity: High.**
Small rolls are not priced off the seller's margin. They are priced by the best-placed buyer, usually a nearby agency that already runs a PM department. That buyer adds 60–100 doors with no new fixed cost beyond perhaps half a PM, so its *marginal* margin on the bolt-on is ~50–65% [estimate: incremental PM wage ~$40–50k plus software per 100 doors against ~$200k gross revenue]. Your standalone 30–35% margin is *lower* than the incumbent's bolt-on margin until you have density. The spread you can capture is therefore only (your marginal margin at density) minus (their marginal margin), not (your margin) minus (their average margin). A sales agency also values the roll as a listing pipeline. That is another reason it pays more than a pure-PM buyer, and the brief treats it as your edge rather than theirs.

**A2. "Sub-scale rolls are neglected, therefore cheap." Severity: High.**
Neglect shows up in the numbers you will inherit:
- Discounted fees. In FY22 Victoria had the lowest average annual management income (AAMI) of the eastern states ($1,132). Real Estate Dynamics (RED) attributed this partly to more *partial* roll sales, where the transferred properties carried lower commission [verified: Elite Agent reporting the RED FY22 report]. Partial and sub-scale parcels are where low-fee "mates' rates" and listing-sweetener managements end up.
- Landlords who are sellers-in-waiting. Inside a sales agency, the PM book is curated to feed listings. Owners who are already thinking of selling stay close to the sales agent who is vending the roll.
- Ageing stock facing Victoria's minimum energy efficiency standards. Ceiling insulation and efficient replacement heating and hot water apply from **1 March 2027**, draughtproofing from July 2027 and cooling by 2030 [verified: CAV/energy.vic.gov.au rental MEES guide, via search]. Owners of older stock in a neglected book are the most likely to sell rather than upgrade in the next 12–24 months, which falls inside your retention and payback window. *Time-sensitive.*

**A3. "Retention: managements survive the move to an AI-led service." Severity: High.**
Two separate losses stack on top of each other:
1. **Change-of-agent attrition** in the first 6–12 months [estimate: 5–15% in a normal handover; worse when the landlord's PM leaves, the brand changes and the service model changes all at once].
2. **Structural attrition** from investors exiting Victoria. Active bonds fell 21,712 in the year to June 2024 and 24,726 (3.6%) in the year to September 2024 [verified: PropTrack and MacroBusiness via search]. As of mid-2026, more bonds were being refunded than lodged for the first time in 20+ years of records [verified: Property Update, June 2026, citing FoundIt; investor-industry source, so treat with caution].

Annual roll attrition of 12–20% is plausible in 2026–27 Victoria [estimate]. The standard "10–20% retained for ~3 months" in the brief only covers item 1, and only for 90 days.

**A4. "The roll is a liquid floor; bank finance up to ~70% LVR." Severity: Med–High.**
- NAB's rent-roll policy lends **up to 70% LVR** (some NAB pages say 80%) against the *bank's valuation*. It requires ≥100 residential managements, offers terms up to 10 years P&I (interest-only considered), and sets a **minimum 2.5x interest cover** [verified: NAB rent-roll policy page and NAB Real Estate Industry Flyer (Mar 2024), via search snippets; pages undated and inconsistent, so confirm with a banker].
- A broker's summary of Macquarie: up to 70% of value using Macquarie's *internal* multiplier, which must **amortise to 60% within 3 years** [unverified: Mortgage Choice broker page, not a Macquarie document].
- The bank values the roll at its own conservative multiple, often below the price you pay (one broker cites 2.6–2.8 for a good roll [unverified]). "70% LVR" can therefore mean only ~55–60% of the purchase price.
- A first-time buyer with no operating history, no licensed PM business and fewer than 100 doors may not qualify at all [estimate]. Expect a personal guarantee and a general security agreement over the company. Expect the bank to ask for a registered mortgage over the family home if serviceability is thin [estimate: standard small-business practice; confirm].
- Liquidity on the downside is weakest exactly when you need it. In a fee-deflation or investor-exodus year, multiples and buyer appetite fall together. Ross Hedditch's broker blog said prices "stagnated" in 2024 due to high rates and a lack of buyers [verified: Hedditch blog; promotional source].

**A5. "Funnel cost per management well under roll cost (~$5–7k)." Severity: Med (comparison only).**
The $5–7k figure is about right as a *headline* price per door. Effective cost per *retained, profitable* door is higher: divide by first-year retention (~0.85) and add DD, legal and integration costs (~$300–600/door [estimate]). That gives **~$6.5–8.5k per retained door**. Gate 1's $2k funnel target therefore has more headroom against acquisition than the brief assumes, which strengthens the funnel case.

## Things the brief gets wrong, misses or understates

1. **"Revenue ~$2.0k/property" is mixed with "multiple ~$3.70 per $1 management income".** The multiple applies to **commission only** (trailing management fees, usually excluding letting, admin, inspection and lease-preparation fees) [verified: Acumentis valuation method; PM/ONE 2026 session reported by Elite Agent]. FY22 Victorian AAMI was **$1,132**, not ~$2.0k [verified: RED FY22 via Elite Agent]. The FY22 price was therefore ~$4.2k/door. Today, with Melbourne median rent ~$580/wk (Sep Q 2025, DFFH) and fees ~5.1–5.9%, commission is ~$1,550–1,750/door. At 3.0–3.5x that prices at **~$4.7–6.1k/door** [estimate]. "1,000 properties ≈ $5M+" holds; the per-dollar logic needs to be stated properly.
2. **$3.70 was Victoria's *highest-in-the-eastern-states* multiplier in an exceptional year (FY22, cheap money).** It is not a central estimate for 2026. Undated valuer ranges for Melbourne are $2.80–3.50 [unverified date: Acumentis]. Older broker updates put inner Melbourne at $3.30–3.75 and outer suburbs at $2.50–2.80 [unverified date: BDH via REB]. Expect a **sub-100-door, sales-agency-held, outer/middle-ring roll at ~$2.6–3.2** in 2026 [estimate].
3. **The fee figure is inconsistent.** RED's FY22 year-end achieved fee was 5.14% (4.85% in the June quarter). LAF's own 2026 guide quotes Victoria at ~5.9% and Melbourne at ~5.8% [verified: both via search]. The first is *achieved on transferred rolls*; the second is *quoted*. Use achieved figures from trust-account data for pricing.
4. **Income-replacement maths ignores principal repayment.** Take 1,150 doors with ~half the price debt-funded (~$3M at ~8% [estimate: SME secured business lending; check current rates]). A 10-year P&I repayment is ~$445k/yr. Against ~$750k contribution (1,150 × $650), the founder gets **~$300k cash, not $500k**, until the debt amortises. Equity required is ~$3M+ before working capital. The brief never says where that comes from.
5. **Vendor conflict over the referral-fee thesis.** The sales agency that sells you the roll keeps the landlord relationships *and* the sales franchise. It will compete with your "run the landlord's agent selection" offer for every listing in that book unless the contract says otherwise.
6. **Licensing.** The entity that holds management authorities and the trust account must be licensed or have a licensed officer in effective control. Lenders and vendors will not engage without this. Without a licensed operator partner, there is no acquisition channel at all (handed to legal).
7. **Assignment.** REIV management authorities embed an assignment clause; non-REIV authorities may not [verified: REIV *Estate Agent* magazine via search]. Non-assignable authorities need landlord re-signing, which triggers a fresh decision point and more churn.

## Improvements (concrete changes to the plan, ranked by impact)

1. **Reverse the sequence: funnel and Crossub first, rolls second.** Make roll buying conditional on Gate 2 evidence (exceptions per 100 doors, PM load ≥250, contribution ≥$850/door on the first 100–150 doors). Before that, you are paying an incumbent-set price for a margin you have not proven.
2. **Adopt a hard pricing rule** (in place of "buy at the incumbent's multiple"):
   - *Base* = trailing-12-month **management commission only**, reconciled to the trust account (not the agent's spreadsheet). Exclude managements at fee <4.4%, properties listed for sale or with a known sale intent, related-party or staff landlords, and doors vacant >30 days.
   - *Multiple* = 3.0, minus 0.2 for each red flag: avg fee <5.0%; trailing attrition >15%; >25% of stock pre-1990 without ceiling insulation; arrears >3% of rent roll; any landlord holding >5% of doors; >20% of rents >10% below market; key PM leaving with no non-solicit. Floor 2.2.
   - *Yield check:* price per door ≤ (your **proven** contribution per door × 0.85 retention) ÷ 0.13. At $650 contribution that caps the price at ~$4.25k/door (~2.6x on $1,650 commission). At $900 it allows ~$5.9k (~3.5x). This one rule ties M&A to the cost-to-serve evidence.
   - *DSCR check:* ≥1.5x after a 15% attrition stress and a 0.5pt fee cut.
3. **Structure every deal for retention risk the brief underweights:**
   - 60–65% at settlement.
   - 20–25% retention, measured **per door at 6 and 12 months**. A management counts as "lost" if it leaves for any reason, *including the landlord selling*, unless you were the referring agent on that sale.
   - 10–15% vendor finance or earn-out, subordinated to the bank and paid from retained-door income.
   - Tightening the definition of a lost management and lengthening the retention period alone have been reported to save ~30% of price [verified: Elite Agent adviser case; anecdotal].
4. **Turn the vendor conflict into a feature: the "PM carve-out with listing referral back" deal.** Many sales agencies refuse to sell their roll because it is their listing pipeline. Offer them a **reciprocal referral**: they get a disclosed right to pitch for the sale when a landlord in the transferred book sells, in exchange for a price 0.3–0.5x lower and vendor finance. This expands deal flow (sellers who would never list the roll) at the cost of part of your referral-fee upside. Test both versions; the brief cannot have both full referral capture and cheap rolls from sales agencies.
5. **Finance plan in stages, not "~70% LVR from day one":**
   - Deals 1–2: equity plus vendor finance, building to ≥100–150 doors and 12 months of trust-account history.
   - Then refinance with a specialist desk (NAB, Macquarie, possibly regional lenders and non-bank cash-flow lenders [unverified]) at ≤55% of *bank valuation*, so a 20% revaluation fall does not breach a 70% covenant.
   - Negotiate interest-only for 12 months, an annual (not ad hoc) valuation, and a cure right by cash paydown.
   - Ring-fence the personal guarantee (capped amount, no home mortgage) and price the founder's equity risk explicitly.
6. **Covenant stress table in the model.** Example: price $5.3k/door, debt at 55% of price → LVR ~64% at a bank valuation 15% below price. Then a 10% fee cut plus a 0.3x multiple fall reduces value ~19%, taking LVR to ~79%: breach. Size debt so that this scenario stays ≤70%.
7. **Build acquisition DD tooling as the first AI product**, since it pays back on day one. Ingest the PropertyMe/Console export plus trust ledgers and produce per-door fee, arrears, rent-to-market (against DFFH/Domain), lease-expiry ladder, MEES exposure, landlord concentration and tenure. This makes you the fastest disciplined buyer in the corridor, which is a real edge over agencies that price on gut feel.

**Overall kill for the acquisition channel:** after 12 months of disciplined bidding, if you have bought <150 doors at ≤3.0x **or** retention is <80%, stop using debt and grow by funnel only.

## Answers to open items in my domain

- **Unit economics: funnel vs roll.**
  - Roll: ~$6.5–8.5k per retained door all-in; payback on proven contribution of $650 → ~10–13 years unlevered, $900 → ~7–9 years.
  - Funnel: $2k per door pays back in ~3 years.
  - Rolls make sense only for density (shared routes and PMs) and speed to the 100-door bank minimum, not as the main growth channel, until contribution is proven ≥$850.
- **Capital plan.**
  - Self-fund and use Crossub to 100 doors via the funnel.
  - One bolt-on of 60–150 doors with ~35% vendor finance and retention; equity ~$250–400k [estimate].
  - At ~250 doors with 12 months of trust history and proven ≥$850 contribution, approach a bank desk.
  - A seed or private-credit raise becomes credible at that milestone: three months of exception data plus one integrated acquisition with ≥88% retention.
  - Full income replacement via rolls needs ~$2.5–3.5M of equity or quasi-equity over 3–4 years. Do not plan on debt alone.
- **Kill criteria:** see the table above.
- **Pre-sell test:** add a "transferring landlord" pre-sell. Before buying, ask the vendor to let you run a joint letter or call to 20 landlords. A re-sign or explicit consent rate ≥70% is a better retention predictor than any DD metric.

## Questions I'd hand to other specialists

1. **Legal/regulatory:**
   - Can a disclosed referral fee on a landlord's sale be taken lawfully by the PM agency under the Estate Agents Act 1980 (Vic) and the ACL?
   - What exactly must the acquirer hold (licence, officer in effective control, trust account) before settlement?
   - Can bulk assignment of non-REIV authorities proceed without fresh consent?
2. **Ops/cost-to-serve:** what exceptions per 100 doors does an *acquired, neglected* book generate in months 0–6 compared with an organic one? My pricing rule needs that number.
3. **Market/demand:** what is the corridor-level forecast of investor exits in 2027 once MEES takes effect, and what share of landlords upgrade rather than sell?
4. **Finance/model:** model the covenant breach probability under correlated shocks (fee −10%, multiple −0.3, attrition 20%) and the equity needed to keep LVR ≤70% at the 90th percentile.
5. **Strata:** do owners corporation management contracts (3-year cap, removable by simple majority) have *any* financeable value to a bank? If not, strata density must be equity-funded.
6. **Competition:** who are the active consolidators in Melbourne in 2026? Known examples include Areal, which added 1,750 Victorian doors by buying propper [verified: REB 2023], Managex-style funds and franchise groups. What multiples did they pay?
