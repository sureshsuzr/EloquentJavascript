# Founder-Fit, Employment & Path Strategist: red-team report

## Verdict (3–5 lines: survives / survives-if / fatal, and the single biggest issue)
**Survives if** it is sequenced as a hybrid that keeps the salary until a hard, dated signal, and if the founder chooses early where his pricing edge earns its money: as a **principal** (investing in rolls) or as a **neutral vendor** (selling to everyone who buys rolls). He can't credibly be both.
**Biggest issue: the retention-pricing edge is worth ~10x more used as a principal than sold as software.** A 5-point retention error is worth ~$100k on a $2M roll, but a DD report sells for ~$5–15k. And the transaction market behind product A is small: Victoria had ~1,300 broker-transferred managements in FY22 in one broker's dataset.
Quitting a ~$530k package for a part-time-validated product A is the worst of the paths I modelled. Employment and IP risk is manageable but real. The binding constraints during employment are **duties owed now** (outside-interest consent, fidelity, IP, confidential information), not post-employment non-competes.

## Verdict on the software business (which product, if any, is the business, from your lens)
- **A (rent-roll intelligence)** is the **wedge and the founder's best use of time**, but as a standalone vendor business it is a consulting-sized market. Its value is best captured **inside a capital position**.
- **C (maintenance cost intelligence)** and **B (book health for lenders and franchise HQs)** are the only candidates for **recurring revenue that doesn't conflict with owning rolls**. They are the software company, if there is one.
- **D (agentic DD and integration)** is a services feature of A. Don't build it first: it competes with the vendors' own agentic roadmaps (PropertyMe AiMe).

From a founder-fit lens the business is: **A as proprietary tooling of a roll-up he co-invests in, generating the data asset; B/C as the external SaaS once that data exists.** Decide by 31 Dec 2027 whether that SaaS is worth quitting for.

## Attacks on the thesis (only in your domain; evidence, severity High/Med/Low)

**1. Selling A neutrally and investing in an acquirer is a structural conflict. (High)**
- Product A ingests a **target's** full ledger and landlord list, often before exclusivity.
- If the founder also owns part of a roll-up, every vendor, broker and rival acquirer that uploads data to his tool hands confidential deal information to a competing bidder.
- Brokers, who control the deal flow, will see this immediately. Rent-roll broking is a relationship market, and one leak story ends distribution.
- **Fix:** pick a side per product. Keep A proprietary to the roll-up, or sell it only to non-competing users (lenders' credit teams, vendors preparing for sale), behind a written information barrier and with no roll-up investment. Sell B and C openly.

**2. The edge monetises badly as fees and well as equity. (High)**
- **Fee capture:** on a $2M roll the modelled saving is ~$100k [brief/round 2]. A DD fee of $5–15k captures 5–15% of it [estimate: comparable valuer fees for business valuations; unverified].
- **Volume:** Real Estate Dynamics' FY22 data shows 1,292 managements transferred in Victoria, 3,281 in NSW and 5,691 in Queensland [verified: Elite Agent coverage of the RED FY22 report via search summary; likely RED's own deal flow, not the whole market].
- **Market size:** even if the national market is 5–10x that, it is ~300–800 roll deals a year [estimate]. At ~$10k a deal, a $3–8M national transaction-fee pool is not a $500k-plus-equity business for a single vendor.
- **The equity alternative:** a capital partner paying ~15% less for a roll, or avoiding one bad roll, books the full spread.

**3. A part-time founder can't sell to banks. (High for B-to-lenders; Low for A-to-agencies)**
- Banks lending against rolls (NAB, Macquarie) treat an analytics feed used for covenant monitoring as an outsourced service.
- APRA CPS 230 (operational risk, including material service providers) applies from 1 Jul 2025 [known regulatory date; not re-checked this session]. Vendor onboarding takes 6–12 months, with security questionnaires a one-person company working evenings can't pass.
- **Implication:** lenders are a year 2–3 customer, or a strategic design partner via a paid pilot. They aren't a 90-day signal.

**4. The binding employment risks are present duties, not restraints. (Med–High; depends on the contract)**
- **Typical senior-executive contract** [estimate: standard Australian executive templates]:
  - full time and attention, with no other business interest without written consent;
  - broad IP assignment ("in the course of employment, or relating to the business, or using company resources");
  - confidentiality;
  - a 6–12-month post-employment non-compete and non-solicit (cascading in Victoria, where the common-law reasonableness test applies).
- **Statutory and equitable duties also apply** if he is an officer: Corporations Act ss 182–183 (improper use of position or information) and the fiduciary corporate-opportunity rule.
- **Case law favours him on IP if he is careful.** *Victoria University of Technology v Wilson* [2004] VSC 33: absent an express clause, or work he was paid to do, the employer is unlikely to own the invention [verified: commentary summaries, not the judgment]. Express "relating to the business" clauses override that.
- **Risk is near zero** if the employer has nothing to do with property, data or fintech. It is **severe** if the employer is in proptech, lending, insurance or data (e.g. REA, Domain/CoStar, Cotality, PropertyMe, a bank). In that case don't start without a written carve-out.
- **The practical contamination vector is AI tooling.** Building models on an employer laptop, under the employer's enterprise LLM or cloud account, or in hours logged as work creates an ownership claim and a discovery problem at exit (acquirer DD asks for IP chain of title).

**5. The non-compete ban is irrelevant to him, and the brief misstates it. (Low severity, but a factual correction)**
- The ban is an **exposure draft released 7 Sep 2026**: the Competition and Fair Work Legislation Amendment (Banning Unfair Non-Competes) Bill 2026. It isn't law yet.
- The threshold is the Fair Work high-income threshold, **$190,100 for FY27** [verified: Treasury ministerial release via HSF/Baker McKenzie summaries]. It isn't ~$180k.
- Above the threshold, non-competes stay enforceable only if necessary for a legitimate interest and reasonable. That restates the common law; it doesn't release him.

**6. The LAF legacy: confidentiality survives and the channel overlaps. (Med)**
- Any LAF restraint has probably expired, depending on his exit date (unverified).
- His **confidentiality obligations don't expire**. LAF holds agency performance, listing and fee data, and its funding round was News Corp-led [verified: SmartCompany/Elite Agent].
- Product B, which benchmarks agencies' book health, overlaps conceptually with LAF's agent-performance data. Any dataset or method traceable to LAF is a claim waiting to happen.
- **The upside:** LAF could be a **channel or customer**, since it sells leads to agents and owns a conveyancing firm (Dott & Crossitt, Feb 2024).
- **Action:** read the exit deed and talk to LAF's CEO before building B.

**7. Cheap capital is a trap here. (Med)**
- His record (Fulfilio exit, LAF CEO, BCG) makes a $1–2M pre-seed easy.
- But sub-scale Australian proptech and SaaS M&A is clearing at ~2–4x revenue [unverified: low-authority blogs]. PropTech Group paid ~4.6x revenue / 26x EBITDA for Eagle Software [verified via search summary; dated].
- A $10–15M exit needs ~$3–4M ARR. Venture money would push him to chase a market that may cap at $10–20M ARR in ANZ before the data asset exists.

**8. Job satisfaction is better than the operator path, but not the job he imagines. (Med)**
- **What he'd enjoy:** building the models and the product.
- **Where the time goes in years 1–2:** ~60% selling to agency principals and brokers (small-business buyers, low ticket, relationship-driven), plus data-wrangling of messy PropertyMe/Console exports.
- **Comparison:** better than the operator (trust accounts, staff, VCAT). Probably more autonomy but less leverage than a $500k executive role.
- **What makes it satisfying:** the hybrid, where he is also a principal making investment decisions on his own models.

## Things the brief gets wrong, misses or understates
1. **The non-compete ban** (see attack 5): a draft, not law; threshold $190,100; irrelevant to him.
2. **Tax timing is missing, and it is time-sensitive.**
   - The May 2026 Budget proposes replacing the 50% CGT discount with indexation plus a 30% minimum tax from **1 Jul 2027** [verified: Forbes AU, Guerdon Associates, SmartCompany summaries].
   - Startup and small-business carve-outs are moving: a Tax Reform No. 4 Bill was reportedly introduced 27 Oct 2026 with a 3-year-hold startup discount [unverified: single source]. Sources conflict on grandfathering, and one says the startup carve-out protects only equity issued **from** 1 Jul 2027.
   - Grandfathering, the issue date of founder shares, the small-business active-asset concessions and trust structure (a 30% minimum tax on discretionary trusts from 1 Jul 2028) change the after-tax exit value by $1–3M on a $10M exit.
   - **Get tax advice before incorporating.** This decision falls before 30 Jun 2027 and the brief doesn't mention it.
3. **"Beat $500k" is mis-specified for a software company.** Founder salary in years 1–3 will be $0–200k. Any win comes from exit equity or from co-investment, so the test must be stated as **7-year after-tax net worth**, which is how I model it below.
4. **The brief doesn't ask whether the edge should be sold or used.** That is the central founder-strategy question, and the answer is mostly "used".
5. **Data rights are a founder-structure issue.**
   - If the roll-up provides the data, the roll-up's shareholder agreement must license de-identified operational data to SoftwareCo, with rights that survive exit.
   - That use has to be lawful under APP 6 secondary use and the agency's landlord authorities.
   - Without this, the "moat" belongs to whoever owns the roll-up.
6. **No minimum time budget.** A senior executive realistically has 10–15 hours a week. 90 days ≈ 150 hours, enough for data-access deals and a backtest, not for a product.

## Improvements (concrete changes to the plan, ranked by impact)
1. **Adopt the principal-first hybrid.**
   - **The roll-up:** co-invest $300–500k as capital and data partner in a roll-up run by a licensed operator (round 1's retiring-principal acquisition). Take 15–30% plus a board seat, and hold **written data rights**.
   - **Product A:** build it as that roll-up's proprietary acquisition engine.
   - **SoftwareCo (his own company):** owns all IP and licenses it to the roll-up royalty-free for acquisitions only. It sells B and C externally. Never sell A to competing acquirers or brokers while he holds roll equity.
2. **Get written employer consent before any customer contact.**
   - It should name the venture, confirm no IP claim, confirm it isn't a competitor, set a time cap (e.g. ≤10 hours a week, outside hours), and include a "passive investment" acknowledgment for the roll-up stake.
   - Use your own laptop, cloud, LLM accounts, email and company. Log hours.
   - If the employer is adjacent to property or data and refuses, choose now between the job and the venture. Don't run it quietly.
3. **Fix the tax structure before 30 Jun 2027.** Take advice on the founder share-issue date under the CGT changes, a company versus trust holding (the 2028 trust minimum tax), and eligibility for the small-business active-asset concessions.
4. **Clear LAF:** read the exit deed and have a 30-minute conversation with LAF's CEO. Explore LAF as a distribution partner for B.
5. **Capital: bootstrap SoftwareCo to the G2 signal**, capped at $250k cash. Revenue comes first from paid DD for vendors preparing a sale and from lender pilots.
   - **Strategic money:** only non-exclusive. A rent-roll broker or lender as a paid design partner, with no ROFR and no exclusivity.
   - **Angel round:** ESIC-eligible, $0.75–1.5M, only after G2.
   - **No VC before $1M ARR.**
6. **Hire a part-time data engineer** (contract, ~$60–80k for 6 months) to run export ingestion. This preserves founder hours for model validation and selling, and it is eligible for the R&D incentive.

## Tests & kill criteria (cheap experiments, metric, threshold, timeframe)

**Wealth comparison over 7 years** (Nov 2026 to Nov 2033; incremental after-tax net worth after $150k a year living costs, savings compounded at 7%, exit gains taxed ~25% effective) [estimate: my model in the session scratchpad, crude; use it for ranking only]:

| Path | Scenarios | Wealth | Satisfaction (1–5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Stay | — | ~$1.75M (including haircut equity grants) | 3 (unknown role) |
| 2 Quit for software at month ~9 | 45% fail by yr 2 (~$0.67M: $250k burnt, re-entry at $450k) / 35% lifestyle ($4M exit, 85% owned) / 20% scale ($15M exit, 65% owned) | **EV ~$2.5M**, wide spread | 4 |
| 3 Capital partner only, while employed | $750k into a roll-up, 30% of ~$6.75M net roll equity / bad case $2M | **EV ~$1.7M** ≈ stay | 3 |
| Hybrid (employed to ~Dec 2027, $400k into roll-up, SoftwareCo B/C) | Fail floor ~$1.5M | **EV ~$3.4M** | 4–5 |

The hybrid dominates because the salary funds the option period and the edge earns money as equity even if the SaaS fails. The model is sensitive to the roll-up's net equity and to the probability of a $15M exit. Treat any EV difference under ~$0.5M as noise.

**90-day test (Feb–Apr 2027, ~$20–30k):**
- **Retention backtest:** pre-sale exports plus 24-month post-sale outcomes for ≥5 historical rolls, via 2 brokers or the acquirers.
- **Metric:** the model's 24-month retention error vs the broker multiple's implied retention.
- **Continue if:** absolute pricing error falls by ≥15% on ≥3 of 5 rolls.
- **Kill A as a standalone product if:** there is no improvement, or fewer than 2 parties will share exports.
- **Paid DD:** continue if ≥3 paid DD engagements at ≥$5k each within 90 days. Kill vendor-A if fewer than 2 parties pay at any price.
- **Maintenance:** continue if ≥2 agencies, or the roll-up target, provide 3-year invoice histories, and a simple tariff predicts book-level cost within ±20%.

## Answers to the round-3 questions in my domain

**Q5: Can it start part-time? Yes for validation, no for selling to lenders.**
- **Conditions:** written consent, your own resources, no customer overlap with the employer, LAF deed cleared.
- **Capital:** ≤$250k for SoftwareCo to G2, plus $300–500k co-investment once the roll-up has a licensed operator and a signed acquisition.
- **Time:** 10–15 hours a week.

**Quit-ready signal (all four):**
1. ≥$250k contracted ARR from B/C, **or** ≥$400k trailing revenue including paid DD;
2. ≥8 paying customers across ≥2 segments, at least one a lender or franchise HQ;
3. data rights covering ≥50k property-years;
4. a backtest that beats multiples.

The brief's $300–500k ARR bar is right in spirit, but part-time it takes ~2–3 years. Pair a lower ARR bar with proof of data and model performance, and keep ~24 months of personal runway.

**Q6: How it compares with rounds 1–2.**
- **Operator (rounds 1–2):** ~2,500–3,100 properties, $1M+ at risk, satisfaction 2/5.
- **Capital partner alone:** ≈ staying on wealth.
- **Pure software:** the highest variance.
- **The hybrid:** the best risk-adjusted outcome, because it keeps the salary through the option period and monetises the pricing edge as equity.

## Questions I'd hand to other specialists
1. **Tax counsel:** under the 1 Jul 2027 CGT reform and the Oct 2026 startup carve-out bill, when should founder shares be issued, and in what holding structure, to keep a 50% discount or small-business concessions on a 2030–33 exit?
2. **M&A/market:** what is the true national annual count of residential roll transactions and their average size? Is RED's VIC figure of 1,292 a market total or its own deals?
3. **Privacy:** can a roll-up lawfully license de-identified ledger and maintenance data to a related SoftwareCo under APP 6 and the landlord management authority?
4. **Competitive:** would Cotality or PropertyMe partner with a third-party B/C product (marketplace listing), or block exports?
5. **Lending:** would NAB or Macquarie take a paid covenant-monitoring pilot from a startup in 2027, and under what CPS 230 conditions?
6. **Employment law:** what does the founder's actual contract say (outside interests, IP scope, restraint length)? Every conclusion above depends on it.
