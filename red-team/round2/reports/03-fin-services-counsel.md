# Financial Services & Credit Counsel: red-team report

*Lens: ASIC/credit/insurance regulation (NCCP Act, Corporations Act Ch 7, Insurance Act, Privacy Act Part IIIA), plus where those meet the Estate Agents Act. Written 2 November 2026. Most legislation, ASIC, CAV and Treasury hosts were blocked from this session, so I relied on search summaries for statute text. The points marked for counsel need a credit/AFSL lawyer to confirm them before anyone relies on them.*

## Verdict
**Survives-if.** Every finance service in the thesis can be run legally and cheaply. The structure is a separate finance company acting as a credit representative under an aggregator's ACL, with insurance handled by referral or as an insurer's distributor. The economics are a different matter. Finance is a second-order revenue line, and it only works on the **landlord** side (refinance and next-purchase loans). The tenant-to-buyer funnel will not carry the platform. **The single biggest issue:** almost every finance or insurance dollar that touches a client's property runs into s 48A of the Estate Agents Act (rebates must be passed to the client). One product also hides an unlicensed-insurance problem the brief doesn't name: the **flat-fee maintenance** offer, where the operator absorbs repair-cost variance for a fixed fee. If it is designed as risk transfer it is insurance business. Design it as a service contract or don't sell it.

## Verdict on the platform thesis
- **Carries economics (from my lens): landlord mortgage broking** (refinance, equity release, the next investment purchase). Landlords hold the large balances and refinance every few years, and buying the next property also produces a new management. Per 1,000 managed properties (~650–750 landlords [estimate]), capturing 10–15% of refinance and purchase events gives roughly 25–45 loans a year. That is ~$65–120k of upfront commission plus a trail book growing ~$15–30k a year after the aggregator split [estimate: $480k average net drawn × 0.55–0.65% upfront, 0.15% trail, 10% aggregator cut]. It is real money, but it is a margin line, not a replacement for PM scale.
- **Doesn't carry economics:** tenant home-buying finance (below), landlord insurance commissions ($22 new / $11 renewal per policy from Terri Scheer [verified: Terri Scheer commission page], and s 48A probably requires them to be passed to the landlord anyway), rent reporting (no Part IIIA pathway), and savings products (no margin without an ADI).
- **Tenant equity products** are worth building as **retention and brand assets** that cost almost nothing to run, not as revenue lines.

## Attacks on the thesis

**1. s 48A swallows most landlord-side finance and insurance income (High).** Under s 48A an agent must "immediately pay" the client any rebate (any discount, commission or other benefit, including non-monetary ones) received "in relation to the sale, management or leasing" of the client's property. Section 50 strips commission entirely where the s 49A rebate statement is defective, and a County Court award of about $3m against agents shows how hard the courts enforce it [verified: CAV rebate-statement form; Mills Oakley/Pointon Partners commentary]. Landlord insurance arranged at onboarding is almost certainly "in relation to management". A refinance secured on the managed property arguably is too. An investment loan for a **different** property bought through the platform is a closer call. *Survives if* broking sits in a **separate legal entity**, under a **separate engagement** the landlord signs, with fees disclosed in the s 49A statement. Model insurance income at $0.

**2. Flat-fee maintenance may be unauthorised insurance business (High, and new; round 1 only flagged the arrears guarantee).** The offer is a fixed annual fee, priced on age, type and condition, under which the operator absorbs the variance in repair cost. That is a promise to bear the cost of an uncertain future event in exchange for a premium, which is the core of a contract of insurance. Carrying on insurance business without APRA authorisation breaches the Insurance Act 1973. Retailer "service plans" typically either say they are not insurance (because the promise is to *repair*, not to pay) or are underwritten by an insurer (e.g. Domestic & General behind The Good Guys) [verified: Retravision/Good Guys plan terms]. Which side the product falls on depends on the drafting: an obligation to *perform defined services* versus an obligation to *indemnify for loss*. I could not verify the APRA or case-law test here [unverified]. *Survives if* it is drafted as a defined-scope service subscription: preventive schedule plus a capped number of call-outs, delivered by your own trades, with no promise to pay third-party costs or reimburse. Catastrophic items (roof, hot-water replacement, structural) go to the landlord's insured or fully-charged path. Alternatively, have an APRA-authorised insurer underwrite the variance component and act as its distributor. Get written advice before pricing it.

**3. The tenant-to-homebuyer funnel is too thin (High for economics, Low for legality).** Converting renters into owners is the emotional heart of the thesis, but the arithmetic is small. Roughly 3–5% of private-renter households become first-home buyers each year [estimate: ~110–120k FHB loans a year nationally against ~2.5–3m private-renter households]. Per 1,000 managed tenancies that is ~30–50 purchases a year. At 15% capture, ~5–8 loans × ~$3k net upfront ≈ **$15–25k a year**. Competition is intense: brokers wrote a record **81.6%** of new home loans in the June 2026 quarter [verified: MFAA/Cotality], and Equifax and Occubuy launched an "Open Score" renter-readiness product on 3 September 2026 [verified: Equifax/IT Brief]. The 5% Deposit Scheme (from 1 Oct 2025: no income caps, unlimited places, $950k cap for Melbourne/Geelong [verified: multiple broker/Housing Australia summaries]) has turned FHB lending into a commodity.

**4. "Rent history as credit evidence" has no Part IIIA pathway (Med).** Comprehensive credit reporting covers *consumer credit* repayment history supplied by licensed credit providers [verified: Experian/Legal Aid Qld]. A residential lease is not consumer credit, so a PM cannot report rent payments as repayment history information. That is a clear yes on the regulatory question and a no on the product. The lawful routes are: (a) give the tenant a verified ledger, because several lenders accept 3–12 months of rent paid through a licensed PM as **genuine savings** [verified: Home Loan Experts, RateCity, St George policy reports]; (b) consent-based CDR/open-banking scoring (Equifax Open Score). Option (a) costs nothing and is more useful today. Treasury's credit reporting framework review (Richards) is with Treasury (policy moved there in May 2025) [verified: treasury.gov.au], so watch for alternative-data reforms. Don't build for them.

**5. "Investment support" crosses three regimes the brief doesn't name (Med).** (a) Real property is not a financial product. However, telling a landlord to refinance, restructure, or stay in a particular loan is **credit assistance** under NCCP s 8 (suggesting or assisting a consumer to apply for, or remain in, a particular credit contract). Investment-property loans to individuals are regulated credit. That triggers ACL or credit-rep status and the **best-interests duty** (s 158LA) [unverified-from-memory; standard law]. (b) Any suggestion to use **super or an SMSF** to buy property is financial product advice that needs an AFSL [verified: ASIC 13-304MR]. Unscrupulous property investment schemes and super-switching lead-gen models remain on ASIC's **2026 enforcement priorities** after Shield and First Guardian [verified: Two Birds, KWM, DLA summaries]. (c) Hold/sell analytics that weigh negative gearing or CGT outcomes for a fee are a **tax agent service** under the Tax Agent Services Act unless a registered tax agent provides them [unverified; confirm with TPB]. Hold/sell valuation and yield analytics are fine. "Restructure your debt and sell before June" is regulated.

**6. Broker-side conflicts and BID scrutiny (Med).** ASIC REP 840 (13 Oct 2026) reviewed BID practices across 8 large broker groups. It found that the processes exist but are applied unevenly, and singled out recommendations of loans that are not the lowest cost [verified: ASIC 26-240MR summary]. A platform broker that steers landlords toward lenders whose valuations or turnaround suit the agency's sale or lease pipeline will be judged against that standard. Volume-based and campaign bonuses are banned, upfront commission is calculated on amount drawn net of offset, and clawbacks can't be passed to the consumer (NCCP Pt 3-5A, since 1 Jan 2021) [unverified-from-memory].

**7. Insurance distribution is low value and full of traps (Low–Med).** Landlord insurance is a general insurance product. Distributing it needs either a "mere referral" arrangement or authorised-representative/distributor status under an insurer's AFSL (general advice only, FSG, DDO target-market distribution and reporting). The hawking prohibition bans unsolicited real-time offers, so you cannot cold-call landlords or tenants at lease signing. The add-on **deferred sales model exemptions** (home building, home & contents and, by the 2021 list, landlord insurance) were due to expire on **5 Oct 2026**. Treasury released draft regulations in July 2026 extending them to 5 Oct 2031, but I could not confirm they were made [verified: Treasury consultation c2026-783033 summary; finalisation **unverified**, time-sensitive]. If they lapsed, offering contents insurance to a tenant "in connection with" a lease could trigger a 4-day deferral. That needs checking now.

**8. Third-line forcing and tying (Med).** Making a tenancy approval, lease renewal or management agreement conditional on using the platform's broker, insurer or maintenance plan is third-line forcing under CCA s 47(6)/(7), which is per se illegal unless notified to the ACCC. It also undermines BID and RTA protections. The "super app" is only legal if every service can be unbundled.

**9. Using tenant application data to sell finance (High reputational, Med legal).** Income and employment data collected through the Victorian standard application form is collected *for the tenancy decision*. Using it to identify "tenants who can afford to buy" and market broking to them is a secondary use and direct marketing under APPs 6/7. As round 1 noted, it likely removes the small-business exemption, and it collides with the 10 Dec 2026 automated-decision transparency rules. Finance marketing must be opt-in and fed by data the tenant supplies afresh, never by application data.

## Things the brief gets wrong, misses or understates
- **Misses:** flat-fee maintenance may be insurance (attack 2). This is a bigger licensing risk than the arrears guarantee because it is a core product.
- **Understates:** s 48A's reach beyond referral fees. It applies to *any* benefit linked to managing the client's property, including insurance commissions, trade discounts on maintenance, and potentially broker commissions on loans secured on that property.
- **Gets wrong (implicitly):** "rent history as credit evidence" assumes bureau reporting is possible. It isn't under Part IIIA. Genuine-savings evidence and CDR scoring are the real channels.
- **Misses:** the DSM exemption sunset (5 Oct 2026) and REP 840 (Oct 2026). Both are live this quarter.
- **Misses:** the MFAA/aggregator **mentoring requirement for new-entrant brokers** (about two years) [unverified-from-memory]. The founder cannot broke on day one. Hire or partner with an experienced broker.
- **Understates:** the competition. Equifax/Occubuy already target renter-to-owner readiness. Brokers hold 81.6% share. Every franchise PM desk already refers to an in-house broker.
- **Correct as stated:** referral fees to the managing agent are exposed under s 48A. The arrears guarantee may be insurance. Real property advice itself is unregulated.

## Improvements (ranked by impact)
1. **Create a separate FinCo (Pty Ltd) from day one,** with a different name and its own engagement, privacy notice and FSG/credit guide. It becomes a **corporate credit representative of a major aggregator** (AFG, Connective, Finsure, LMG, etc.), with an experienced broker who holds Cert IV + Diploma (FNS40821/FNS50322) and is past mentoring. Cost: aggregator fees ~$3–8k/yr plus a 5–15% split, PI and AFCA through the licensee, broker paid on a split or $100–130k base [estimate]. Time: 6–10 weeks. This avoids your own ACL until volume justifies it.
2. **Redesign flat-fee maintenance as a defined service subscription.** Specify scheduled preventive visits, X reactive call-outs, labour included, parts at cost, and an exclusions list. The trades network performs the work and there is no indemnity or cash promise. Get written insurance-law advice before launch. If the variance itself is the selling point, partner with an insurer and be its distributor.
3. **Run a s 48A/49A opinion and design** (alongside the round 1 opinion): which FinCo income is a "rebate in relation to management", and whether the landlord can lawfully engage FinCo directly so that broker commission isn't a rebate to the agency. Fill in the rebate statement truthfully. Model insurance at $0 and landlord refinance commission at 50% until the advice lands.
4. **Productise the "verified rental ledger and genuine-savings pack" as a free tenant benefit:** a lender-ready letter, 12-month ledger, bond status, and a pointer to the 5% Deposit Scheme. It needs no licence (factual information). It builds loyalty and is the honest version of "rent history as credit evidence". Partner with Equifax Open Score instead of building scoring yourself.
5. **Keep investment support as analytics, not advice:** yield, vacancy, maintenance-cost forecasts and sale-value ranges. Anything about loans goes to FinCo under BID. Super/SMSF is never mentioned (hard rule, enforced in prompts and scripts). Tax questions go to a referred registered tax agent.
6. **Insurance:** start as a mere referrer, with commission passed through and disclosed. Move to distributor/AR status only if you build an embedded product with real volume. Never sell at lease signing (hawking/DSM).
7. **Unbundle everything** (CCA s 47). No service is a condition of a lease, a renewal, or the management agreement. Write that into the terms.
8. **Run an information barrier** between PM and FinCo. Tenant financial data flows only on fresh, specific, opt-in consent. Application data is never used for marketing.

## Answers to the round-2 questions in my domain
**Q1 (which services carry economics):** From a finance lens, add **landlord mortgage broking** second, after the PM anchor, through FinCo under an aggregator. Insurance, savings, rent reporting and tenant broking stay as retention features with near-zero revenue.

**Q2 (LTV):** Landlord finance adds roughly **$100–180 per managed property per year** in steady state (upfront plus accreting trail, before s 48A haircut) [estimate]. That trims the property count needed for a $500k draw by ~5–10%, not by half. Tenant finance adds **<$25/tenancy/yr**. The trail book is a saleable asset (separate valuation), which matters for exit more than for the draw.

**Q3 (regulated vs disqualifying):**
- *Regulated but manageable:* broking (ACL/credit rep plus BID), insurance distribution (AFSL/AR or referrer, DDO, anti-hawking), deposit/savings (ADI partner or referral only), hold/sell analytics (unregulated if kept clear of credit, super and tax).
- *Disqualifying unless restructured:* (a) keeping commissions linked to managing a client's property (s 48A); (b) flat-fee maintenance or arrears guarantee structured as indemnity (unauthorised insurance); (c) any SMSF-property recommendation; (d) tying services to tenancy or management (third-line forcing); (e) marketing finance to tenants from application data (privacy).
- *Agency conflict:* advising the landlord's tenant to buy and leave is a conflict with your principal. It is **manageable** because the tenant's right to leave exists anyway, and the agency gains a re-lease and possibly a new management. It needs a separate entity, information barriers, and no use of tenant-departure intelligence to the landlord's detriment or benefit without consent.

**Q5 (founder edge):** It is decisive for loan-structure analytics, for serviceability and refinance-trigger models that tell FinCo which landlords to call, and for pricing a maintenance subscription. It is irrelevant to licensing, where the bottleneck is experienced, credentialled people and a clean compliance record.

**Q6 (smallest test):** The landlord loan-health-check on the acquired book (table above). It needs no own licence and gives a real conversion rate within 6 months.

**Licence roadmap:**
- **Q4 2026–Q1 2027:** legal opinions (s 48A, maintenance-as-insurance, DSM status), $15–30k, 4–8 weeks. FinCo incorporated. Referral agreement with an established broker as interim.
- **Q1–Q2 2027:** FinCo becomes a corporate credit rep under an aggregator, with a hired experienced broker; 6–10 weeks, ~$10–20k set-up. Landlord-insurance referrer agreement (pass-through).
- **2028+, only if >150 loans/yr:** own ACL, with ~$60–150k first-year cost (legal, responsible managers, compliance, PI, AFCA, CSLR, audit) [estimate] and ASIC decision in 150–240 days.
- **Never:** own AFSL for personal advice. It isn't needed.

## Questions I'd hand to other specialists
- **Vic regulatory counsel:** does s 48A catch broker commission on a loan *secured on* the managed property when the landlord engages FinCo directly? Can the flat maintenance fee be split with a renter at all under the RTA?
- **Insurance/APRA counsel:** is the service-subscription drafting enough to keep flat-fee maintenance out of "insurance business"?
- **CFO modeller:** plug in $100–180/property/yr landlord-finance contribution with a 50% s 48A haircut, and value the trail book separately.
- **Privacy specialist:** can FinCo lawfully receive any PM-held tenant data on opt-in, given the Dec 2026 ADM rules?
- **Maintenance/ops:** what share of repair-cost variance comes from catastrophic items that must sit outside the subscription?
