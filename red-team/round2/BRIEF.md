# Round 2 brief: the platform thesis

Round 1 (`red-team/BRIEF.md`, `red-team/SYNTHESIS.md`) red-teamed an AI-native PM *agency*. The founder's response: that framing undersold the idea. This round tests the thesis as the founder actually holds it.

## The founder's edge
Computer scientist, statistician and CFO (plus ex-BCG, ex-CEO of LocalAgentFinder, founder of Fulfilio, acquired by Australia Post). Heavy hands-on AI user. Melbourne. Success = replacing ~$500k income; international scale is upside.

## The thesis
Use AI and data not only to cut operating cost but to **redesign the offer around the rental relationship**, with the managed property as the distribution channel for a set of priced services. The founder knows this risks being "a super app" and wants it attacked on that basis.

**Landlord side**
- Property management (the anchor relationship).
- **Tenant evaluation** as a priced, data-driven product (risk scoring, rent-setting).
- **Investment support**: portfolio analytics, yield/hold/sell decisions, refinancing, buying the next property (which also feeds new managements).
- **Flat fee for regular maintenance**: the operator absorbs repair-cost variance for a fixed annual fee, priced on property age/type/condition, delivered through a dense, owned or contracted trades network with preventive maintenance. The fee could be split between landlord and tenant.

**Tenant side**
- Help tenants **grow equity** (savings, rent history as credit evidence, deposit pathways).
- Help them **find another place to live** when they need to move (including within the managed portfolio).
- Help them **find and buy their future home** (search, buyer support, finance).

**Data and AI**
- Operational autonomy: agents doing transactional PM work.
- Underwriting: tenant risk, maintenance cost, rent and vacancy prediction, landlord churn/sale propensity.

## Constraints already established in round 1 (take as given unless you can overturn them with evidence)
- A licensed officer in effective control is required; the founder can't hold a licence before ~Q4 2027. The likely entry route is buying a licensed agency of 250–500 managements. From 25 Nov 2026 all agents' representatives must register individually.
- Pure AI cost-out isn't a durable edge: PropertyMe (agentic AiMe announced; AiMe Connect read-only), MRI and Ailo ship automation to all agencies within 12–24 months, and offshore-VA incumbents already run at ~$430–580 per property.
- Management-only economics: revenue ~$1.86k per property ex-GST; churn of ~10–14%/yr × replacement cost is the master variable; a $500k draw needs ~2,000–2,500 properties on management fees alone.
- Referral fees to the managing agent are likely caught by s 48A Estate Agents Act (rebates to the client). An arrears guarantee that pays lost rent may be insurance (APRA). Victorian standard application form (Mar 2026) limits screening data. Privacy Act automated-decision transparency applies from 10 Dec 2026; taking referral or other benefits for personal information likely loses the small-business exemption.
- The Victorian Residential Tenancies Act restricts fees chargeable to renters and puts repair obligations on the rental provider.
- Landlords distrust "AI" (only 36% of Australians trust AI). Lead with a named human and transparency.
- Durable edge identified in round 1: **independence from sales** (a PM specialist can give neutral advice at the point of sale).

## Questions this round must answer
1. Which **one or two** services carry the economics, and in what order to add them?
2. What does lifetime value per landlord and per tenant relationship look like, and does it change the scale needed for a $500k draw?
3. Which services are regulated (credit licence, AFSL, insurance, tenancy-law fee bans) and which conflicts are disqualifying rather than manageable?
4. Is autonomous rental management technically and legally feasible now, and what is the realistic autonomy frontier by 2027–2029?
5. Where is the founder's CS/stats/CFO edge decisive, and where is it irrelevant?
6. What is the smallest test that proves or kills the platform thesis?
